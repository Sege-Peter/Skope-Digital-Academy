A busy client's calendar is full of meetings, calls, deadlines and personal plans. A good VA keeps it accurate so the client is never double-booked, late or confused. When clients and their contacts live in different countries, you also need to handle **time zones** correctly.

## Calendar management basics

When you add an event in Google Calendar, include:

- A **clear title**: "Call – Mr Mutua (supplier, Machakos)" not just "Call".
- The correct **date, start and end time**.
- The **location** or **video link** (Google Meet, Zoom).
- **Guests** – add their emails so they get an invitation.
- **Notes** – agenda, phone numbers, documents to read.
- **Reminders** – for example 1 day and 30 minutes before.

### Good calendar habits

- **Buffer time:** leave 10–15 minutes between meetings for travel or breaks.
- **Colour coding:** for example, blue for client meetings, green for personal, red for deadlines.
- **Block focus time:** protect hours for the client's own important work.
- **Confirm the day before:** send a short reminder to people attending.

## Understanding time zones

Kenya uses **East Africa Time (EAT)**, which is **UTC+3**. Kenya does not change its clocks during the year.

UTC (Coordinated Universal Time) is the reference point. Other places are a number of hours ahead (+) or behind (–) UTC.

| Place | Standard time | Difference from Kenya (standard time) |
|---|---|---|
| Kenya, Uganda, Tanzania | UTC+3 | same |
| Rwanda, South Africa | UTC+2 | 1 hour behind |
| Nigeria | UTC+1 | 2 hours behind |
| United Kingdom (winter) | UTC+0 | 3 hours behind |
| New York, USA (winter) | UTC–5 | 8 hours behind |
| Dubai, UAE | UTC+4 | 1 hour ahead |
| India | UTC+5:30 | 2½ hours ahead |

> **Warning:** Many countries in Europe and North America use **daylight saving time** – they move their clocks forward one hour in their summer. During that period, the UK is only **2 hours** behind Kenya and New York is **7 hours** behind. The change dates differ by country, so always check with a time zone tool such as a world clock or Google Calendar's time zone setting.

### Worked example

Your client in London wants a call at **2 pm London time** in January (UK winter, UTC+0). Kenya is 3 hours ahead, so the call is at **5 pm in Kenya**.

In July (UK summer time), the same 2 pm London call is at **4 pm in Kenya**.

### Tips to avoid mistakes

1. **Always write the time zone** in messages: "Tuesday 10 am EAT / 7 am GMT".
2. **Use Google Calendar's time zone features** – you can show a second time zone in Settings, and events created with guests show in each guest's own time.
3. **Use a world clock** (on your phone or a website) to double-check before confirming.
4. **Watch the date**, not just the hour – 11 pm in New York can already be the next morning in Nairobi.

## Scheduling a meeting step by step

1. Ask the client for their preferred days and times.
2. Check the calendar for conflicts.
3. Offer 2–3 options to the other person, **with time zones**.
4. Once agreed, create the event with guests, link and agenda.
5. Send a reminder the day before.

```text
Hi Sarah, Mr Ouma would like to meet to discuss the order.
Would any of these work for you?
- Tuesday 11 March, 9:00 am GMT (12:00 pm EAT)
- Wednesday 12 March, 1:00 pm GMT (4:00 pm EAT)
The call will be on Google Meet and take about 30 minutes.
```

## Practice task

In Google Calendar, add a second time zone (for example London or New York) in Settings. Then create a practice event for "Call with a client in London" next Tuesday at 3 pm London time, with a Google Meet link, a 30-minute reminder and a short agenda. Check what time it shows in Kenya time – and confirm with a world clock whether the UK is on winter or summer time on that date.
