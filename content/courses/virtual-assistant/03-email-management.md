Many business owners have hundreds of unread emails. Important messages from customers get buried under newsletters and adverts. One of the most valued VA services is **inbox management**: keeping the inbox clean, making sure nothing important is missed, and handling simple replies.

## Agree the rules first

Before touching a client's inbox, agree on these points in writing:

- Which emails can you **reply to** yourself, and which must the client answer?
- Can you **delete** emails, or only archive them?
- Which senders are **VIPs** (always urgent)?
- How should you **sign** replies – as yourself ("Amina, Assistant to Mr. Ouma") or in the client's name?
- How often should you check the inbox?

> **Note:** Archiving in Gmail removes an email from the inbox but keeps it in "All Mail", so it can always be found again. When in doubt, archive rather than delete.

## A simple system: labels and four actions

Gmail uses **labels** instead of folders. Create a few clear labels, for example:

| Label | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 1-Urgent | Client must see today |
| 2-To Reply | Needs an answer |
| 3-Waiting | Waiting for someone else to reply |
| 4-Receipts | Invoices, payments, M-Pesa statements |
| 5-Reference | Information to keep |

Then, for each email, choose one of four actions:

1. **Delete or archive** – adverts, old notifications.
2. **Do it now** – if a reply takes under two minutes, write it.
3. **Label it** – for things that need the client or take longer.
4. **Forward** – send to the right person (for example, the accountant).

The goal is an inbox with only a few items left, each clearly labelled.

## Filters save time

Gmail **filters** can label or archive emails automatically. For example, every email from a bank or payment service could go straight to "4-Receipts". To create one, use the search bar options (the small slider icon), enter the sender, then choose "Create filter".

## Writing good replies

Many replies are similar, so VAs keep **templates** for common questions.

```text
Subject: Re: Price list for school uniforms

Dear Mrs Akinyi,

Thank you for your enquiry. Please find our current price list
attached. Orders placed by Wednesday are ready for collection
the following Monday.

If you have any questions, I'm happy to help.

Kind regards,
Amina Hassan
Assistant to Peter Ouma | Ouma Uniforms, Kisii
```

A good email has a clear **subject**, a polite **greeting**, a short **body** with one main point, a **closing**, and a **signature**.

## Daily summary for the client

At the end of your session, send the client a short summary so they don't need to open the inbox at all:

```text
Inbox update – Tuesday
- 3 urgent: supplier invoice due Friday, school order (KES amount
  needs your approval), meeting request from the bank.
- 8 replied using templates (price lists, opening hours).
- 2 waiting: delivery date from the printer.
- Inbox now at 4 emails.
```

> **Warning:** Be extra careful with emails asking for payments or changes to bank details. Scammers sometimes pretend to be suppliers and ask for money to be sent to a "new account". Always flag these to the client and let them confirm by phone with a known contact.

## Practice task

In your own Gmail, create the five labels from this lesson and set up **one filter** (for example, label all emails from a newsletter you receive). Then write a **template reply** to a common question a small business near you might get, such as opening hours or prices.
