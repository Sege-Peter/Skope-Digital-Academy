A **virtual assistant (VA)** helps a busy person or business with everyday tasks – from a distance, using the internet. Think of the secretary or office assistant in a busy office. A VA does much of the same work, but from home, a cyber café or a co-working space, and often for clients in other towns or countries.

Business owners hire VAs because they have too many small tasks and not enough time. Every hour they spend answering emails or typing receipts is an hour they are not selling, teaching or building their business.

## Common VA tasks

| Area | Example tasks |
|---|---|
| **Email** | Sorting the inbox, replying to simple messages, flagging urgent ones |
| **Calendar** | Booking meetings, sending reminders, avoiding double bookings |
| **Data entry** | Typing receipts, updating customer lists, cleaning spreadsheets |
| **Research** | Finding suppliers, comparing prices, collecting contact details |
| **Documents** | Typing, formatting and proofreading letters, reports and forms |
| **Social media** | Scheduling posts, replying to comments, simple Canva graphics |
| **Customer support** | Answering enquiries by email or chat |
| **Project support** | Updating task boards, following up on deadlines |

You do not have to offer all of these. Many VAs start with two or three tasks they do well, then add more.

## General VA or specialist?

- A **general VA** does a bit of everything for one or a few clients.
- A **specialist VA** focuses on one area – for example, a "social media VA", "real estate VA" or "e-commerce VA" who knows the tools of that industry.

Beginners usually start general, notice what they enjoy and what clients pay for, and then specialise.

## Qualities clients look for

Skills can be learned. These qualities are what make clients keep a VA for years:

- **Reliability** – doing what you said, when you said.
- **Attention to detail** – correct names, correct numbers, no typos.
- **Discretion** – keeping the client's information private.
- **Initiative** – noticing problems and suggesting solutions.
- **Clear communication** – short, polite updates.

> **Warning:** As a VA you may get access to a client's email, calendar, files and even customer details. Never share or misuse this information. Kenya has a Data Protection Act, and many clients abroad have their own privacy laws – treat every file as confidential. You can read about Kenyan data protection on the official site, odpc.go.ke.

## Meet Wanjiru

Wanjiru from Nyeri is good with computers and very organised. Her uncle runs a small transport business and is always losing receipts. She offers to type his receipts into a Google Sheet every Friday and send him a weekly summary. Within a month he recommends her to a friend who runs a school. Wanjiru now has two regular clients – and real experience to show on her LinkedIn profile.

## What you need

- A laptop (best) or a reliable smartphone
- Stable internet and a backup data bundle
- A professional Gmail address
- Basic typing speed and good written English
- A quiet place for calls
