Clients often ask VAs to "find out" something: suppliers for a product, venues for an event, competitors' prices, contacts of schools in a county, or the best tool for a job. Good research saves the client hours – and a clear research report makes you look like a professional.

## Step 1: Understand the question

Before searching, make sure you know exactly what the client needs. Ask:

- **What** information do you need? (names, prices, phone numbers, reviews?)
- **How many** results? (top 5? all of them?)
- **Where?** (Kisumu only? all of Kenya? online only?)
- **By when?**
- **What format?** (a Google Sheet, a short Doc, a WhatsApp summary?)

For example, "Find printers" is vague. "Find 5 printing shops in Eldoret that can print 500 A5 flyers in colour, with prices and phone numbers, in a Google Sheet by Thursday" is clear.

## Step 2: Search smartly

Google search has simple tricks that save time:

| Search trick | Example | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| Quotes | `"flyer printing" Eldoret` | Finds that exact phrase |
| Minus sign | `printing Eldoret -jobs` | Removes results with "jobs" |
| site: | `site:go.ke youth fund` | Searches only Kenyan government sites |
| filetype: | `catering price list filetype:pdf` | Finds PDF documents |
| OR | `venue Kisumu OR Kakamega` | Either word |

Also use **Google Maps** to find local businesses, their opening hours, phone numbers and reviews.

## Step 3: Check that sources are reliable

Not everything online is true or up to date. Ask yourself:

- **Who wrote this?** An official site (a `.go.ke` government site, a company's own website) is more reliable than an anonymous blog or forum.
- **When was it updated?** Prices and contacts change. Look for a date.
- **Do other sources agree?** Check important facts in at least two places.
- **Is it selling something?** Reviews on a seller's own page may be one-sided.

> **Tip:** For anything official – government services, tax, licences, fees – always use the official website as your source, and tell the client where you found it. Never guess.

## Step 4: Record as you go

Keep a sheet open while you search, and add a column for the **source link** and the **date checked**.

| Business | Location | Service | Price (KES) | Phone | Source | Checked |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Example Prints | Eldoret town | 500 A5 colour flyers | Asked – awaiting reply | 07xx xxx xxx | Google Maps | 2026-03-10 |

If a price isn't online, write "Not listed – call to confirm" instead of guessing. You can also offer to call or message businesses for the client.

## Step 5: Present the results

Clients are busy. Start with the answer, then the details.

```text
Research: Flyer printing in Eldoret (500 A5, colour)

Summary: I found 5 printers. Two confirmed prices by phone;
the cheapest confirmed option was Example Prints. Three have
not yet replied – details in the sheet.

Recommendation: Example Prints – confirmed price, good
reviews on Google Maps, can deliver in 2 days.

Full table: [link to Google Sheet]
```

> **Warning:** Respect privacy. Collect only business contact details that are publicly shared for business, and never collect or share private personal information without permission.

## Practice task

Do a mini research job: find **five places in your town** that offer a service (for example printing, catering or tent hire). Record the name, location, price (or "not listed"), phone, source link and the date you checked in a Google Sheet. Then write a three-line summary with one recommendation.
