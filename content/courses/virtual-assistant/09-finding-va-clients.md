You now have the core skills. The next step is finding people who will pay for them. Most VAs get their first clients through a mix of **people they know**, **local businesses** and **online platforms**.

## Step 1: Decide your offer

Clients don't buy "VA services" – they buy solutions. Turn your skills into a clear offer:

| Vague | Clear offer |
|---|---|
| "I am a VA" | "I manage inboxes and calendars for busy business owners" |
| "I do data entry" | "I type your receipts into Google Sheets every week and send a summary" |
| "I can do social media" | "I design and schedule 3 Canva posts a week for your shop" |

## Step 2: Prepare your proof

- A short **LinkedIn profile** with a clear headline such as "Virtual Assistant | Inbox & Calendar Management | Google Sheets".
- **Two or three samples** in a Google Drive folder: a tidy spreadsheet, a sample email template, a Canva post, a research summary. Label practice samples honestly.
- A one-page **service sheet** (made in Canva or Docs) listing what you offer.

## Step 3: Where to look

### Close to home
Start with people who already trust you: relatives, neighbours, your former school, church or mosque, local SACCOs, shops and small businesses. Many owners in Kenyan towns would welcome help with records, WhatsApp replies or posters – they just haven't thought of hiring someone.

### Online
- **Upwork** and **Fiverr** – search for "virtual assistant", "data entry", "calendar management" and study the job posts and gigs.
- **LinkedIn** – post about what you've learned, connect with small business owners, and check the Jobs section for remote assistant roles.
- **Kenyan job boards** such as BrighterMonday, Fuzu and MyJobMag sometimes list remote administrative roles.

> **Note:** Platform rules, fees and payment options change. Read each platform's official help pages before you join, and set up a payment method in your real name before you accept your first job. ## Step 4: Reach out politely

A short, personal message works better than a long advert:

```text
Hello Mama Wanjiku, I hope business is going well.
I've been training as a virtual assistant and help small
businesses keep their records tidy. I could type your weekly
receipts into a simple sheet and send you a summary every
Friday, so you always know your spending.
Would you like me to do the first week as a trial?
```

Follow up once after a few days if there's no reply – then move on politely.

## Step 5: Start small, deliver well

Your first client may be small, and that's fine. Deliver on time, communicate clearly, and ask for:
- a short **testimonial** you can show others (with permission), and
- a **referral**: "Do you know anyone else who might need this kind of help?"

One happy client often leads to the next.

## Staying safe

> **Warning:** Be careful of "VA jobs" that ask you to pay for training, software or registration, that want you to receive and forward money or parcels, or that ask for your ID and bank details before any proper interview. Real clients pay you for work – you never pay them to get it.
