As a VA, you are often the voice of your client's business. You write to their customers, suppliers and partners. How you communicate affects how people see the client – so it must be clear, polite and reliable.

## Communicating with your client

### Check-ins and updates

Agree how and when you will update the client: a daily message, a weekly report or a quick call. A simple **end-of-day update** builds trust:

```text
Update – Thursday
Done:
- Typed 45 receipts into the March sheet (total KES 312,400)
- Booked Monday 10 am call with the bank (invite sent)
In progress:
- Supplier research – 3 of 5 done, finishing tomorrow
Need from you:
- Approval for the Easter poster (link in Trello)
```

Notice the three parts: **Done – In progress – Need from you**. The client can read it in 30 seconds.

### Asking good questions

When instructions are unclear, ask – but ask well. Group your questions and offer options:

| Weak | Strong |
|---|---|
| "I don't understand the task." | "For the supplier list, should I include suppliers outside Nairobi, or only Nairobi?" |
| Five separate messages | One message with numbered questions |
| "What should I do?" | "I suggest A or B. Which do you prefer?" |

## Communicating on the client's behalf

- **Use the client's tone.** Some businesses are formal, others friendly. Read their past emails and match them.
- **Be accurate.** Never promise prices, delivery dates or refunds unless the client has approved them.
- **Know your limits.** If a customer is angry or asks about something you aren't sure about, reply politely that you will check and get back – then ask the client.

```text
Dear Mr Kamau,
Thank you for your message. I have shared your request with
Ms Njeri and will get back to you by tomorrow, 12 pm, with
an answer.
Kind regards,
Amina, Assistant to Ms Njeri
```

## Calls and video meetings

- Test your internet, microphone and camera before the call.
- Find a quiet place; use earphones if there is background noise.
- Join a minute or two early.
- Mute when you are not speaking in group calls.
- Take notes and send a short summary afterwards with action points.

> **Tip:** If your connection is weak, turn off your video and tell the others. Clear audio matters more than seeing your face.

## Professional boundaries

- Agree your **working hours** and response times, especially with clients in other time zones.
- Keep **work chats on work channels** (email, Slack, the platform) where possible, rather than your personal WhatsApp.
- Keep all client information **confidential** – don't discuss clients' business with friends or post screenshots online.
- If you make a mistake, **say so quickly**, fix it, and explain what you'll do to avoid it next time.

> **Warning:** If someone claiming to be your client's boss, bank or supplier asks you by email or chat to make an urgent payment or share login details, stop and confirm directly with your client on a known number. This is a common trick used by fraudsters.
