**Financial statements** are summaries that show how a business performed and what it owns and owes. Large companies have accountants for this, but even a kiosk owner benefits from understanding the three main statements in simple form.

## 1. Profit and loss statement (income statement)

Shows whether the business made a profit or loss **over a period** such as a month or a year.

```text
Kiprono's Phone Repairs – Profit and loss for March
Sales (repairs and accessories)            KES 52,000
Less: Cost of parts and accessories sold   KES 20,000
Gross profit                               KES 32,000

Less: Expenses
  Shop rent                     6,000
  Electricity and internet      2,500
  Transport                     1,500
  Total expenses                           KES 10,000
Net profit                                 KES 22,000
```

- **Gross profit** = sales − cost of the goods sold.
- **Net profit** = gross profit − all other expenses.

> **Note:** Owner's drawings do not appear as an expense. They are taken out of the profit afterwards.

## 2. Balance sheet

Shows what the business **owns** and **owes** on a particular day – a snapshot.

- **Assets** – things the business owns: cash, M-Pesa balance, stock, tools, money customers owe it.
- **Liabilities** – what the business owes: supplier bills, loans.
- **Owner's equity** (capital) – the owner's share: what would be left if all debts were paid.

The balance sheet always balances:

```text
Assets = Liabilities + Owner's equity
```

```text
Kiprono's Phone Repairs – Balance sheet on 31 March
Assets
  Tools and soldering station   15,000
  Stock of parts                12,000
  Cash and M-Pesa                8,000
  Customers who owe him          3,000
  Total assets                          38,000
Liabilities
  Owed to parts supplier         5,000
  SACCO loan balance            10,000
  Total liabilities                     15,000
Owner's equity (38,000 − 15,000)        23,000
```

Check: 15,000 + 23,000 = 38,000. It balances.

## 3. Cash flow statement

Shows where cash actually **came from and went to** during the period. It explains why the cash balance changed, even when profit was high. In the [cash flow lesson](cash-flow) you made a forecast; the cash flow statement is the same idea looking back at what really happened.

## Why these matter for a small business

| Statement | Question it answers |
|---|---|
| Profit and loss | Did I make money this month? |
| Balance sheet | What is my business worth, and how much do I owe? |
| Cash flow statement | Where did my cash go? |

Lenders, SACCOs and investors may ask for these. Good daily records (see the Bookkeeping course) make them easy to prepare. For official accounts or tax returns, work with a qualified accountant and check requirements on kra.go.ke.

```exercise
Q: Sales are KES 30,000, cost of goods sold KES 12,000 and other expenses KES 8,000. Fill in gross and net profit.
Gross profit = KES ___, net profit = KES ___
A: 18000 / 18,000 | 10000 / 10,000
```

```exercise
Q: Total assets are KES 50,000 and liabilities are KES 20,000. What is owner's equity?
Owner's equity = KES ___
A: 30000 / 30,000
H: Assets = Liabilities + Owner's equity.
```
