To price well and plan ahead, you need to know what kind of costs your business has. Costs fall into two main groups.

## Fixed costs

**Fixed costs** stay about the same each month, whether you sell a lot or nothing at all.

- Stall or shop rent
- Monthly internet bundle for the business
- A helper's fixed monthly pay
- Loan repayments
- County permit (spread over the year)

## Variable costs

**Variable costs** go up when you sell more and down when you sell less, because they are linked to each item.

- Ingredients and raw materials
- Packaging (cups, bags, labels)
- Stock bought for resale
- Transport per delivery
- Mobile money charges per transaction

## Example: Kiprono's tailoring shop, Eldoret

| Cost | Type | Monthly amount (KES) |
|---|---|---|
| Shop rent | Fixed | 4,000 |
| Electricity (mostly fixed) | Fixed | 1,000 |
| Phone and internet | Fixed | 500 |
| Fabric, thread, buttons – KES 300 per school uniform | Variable | depends on sales |

In a month when he makes 50 uniforms:

```text
Fixed costs:     4,000 + 1,000 + 500  = KES 5,500
Variable costs:  50 × 300             = KES 15,000
Total costs:     5,500 + 15,000       = KES 20,500
```

If he sells each uniform for KES 600, revenue is 50 × 600 = KES 30,000, and profit is 30,000 − 20,500 = **KES 9,500**.

> **Note:** Some costs are partly fixed and partly variable – electricity rises when the sewing machines run longer. For a small business it is fine to put each cost in the group it mostly belongs to.

## Why the difference matters

- **Fixed costs must be paid even in a bad month.** Keep them low when you are starting – rent a small space or share one.
- **Variable costs tell you the minimum price** you can charge per item. If a uniform costs KES 300 in materials, selling at KES 280 loses money on every single sale.
- Knowing both lets you calculate your **break-even point** (next lesson).

## Cost per unit

To find what one item really costs, add its variable cost to its share of the fixed costs.

```text
Fixed costs per uniform (50 made): 5,500 ÷ 50 = KES 110
Variable cost per uniform:                      KES 300
Full cost per uniform:              110 + 300 = KES 410
```

If he made only 25 uniforms, each one would carry 5,500 ÷ 25 = KES 220 of fixed costs, so the full cost would be KES 520. Selling more spreads fixed costs thinner.

```exercise
Q: Classify each cost: shop rent, then flour for bread.
Shop rent is a ___ cost; flour is a ___ cost.
A: fixed | variable
```

```exercise
Q: Variable cost is KES 40 per item and you make 100 items. What is the total variable cost?
Total variable cost = KES ___
A: 4000 / 4,000
```

## Practice task

List **every cost** your business has (or would have) in one month. Mark each as fixed or variable and put a KES amount next to it. Add up the fixed costs and work out the variable cost of one unit of your product or service.
