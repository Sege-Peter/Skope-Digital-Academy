A **business budget** is a plan for how much you expect to sell and spend in a coming period, usually a month. A cash flow forecast focuses on *when* money moves; a budget focuses on *how much* you plan to earn and spend, and whether the plan leaves a profit.

## Why budget?

- It forces you to set a sales target.
- It shows whether your plan makes a profit before you spend.
- It gives you something to compare with reality at the end of the month.
- Lenders such as SACCOs often want to see that you can plan.

## Steps to build a monthly budget

1. **Estimate sales** – use past records if you have them. Be realistic, not hopeful.
2. **List variable costs** – these follow sales (materials, packaging, transport).
3. **List fixed costs** – rent, internet, permit share, helper's pay.
4. **Plan your own pay** (drawings) and any savings or loan repayments.
5. **Calculate the expected profit** and check it is enough.

## Example: Fatuma's liquid soap business, Mombasa

Fatuma expects to sell 300 litres at KES 150 per litre. Her variable cost is KES 70 per litre.

| Item | Budget (KES) |
|---|---|
| **Sales** (300 × 150) | **45,000** |
| Variable costs (300 × 70) | 21,000 |
| Stall rent | 3,000 |
| Delivery boda (fixed monthly deal) | 4,000 |
| Phone and data | 1,000 |
| **Total costs** | **29,000** |
| **Expected profit** (45,000 − 29,000) | **16,000** |
| Owner's pay (drawings) | 10,000 |
| Kept in business for growth | 6,000 |

## Compare budget with actual

At the end of the month, put what really happened next to the budget. The difference is called the **variance**.

| Item | Budget | Actual | Variance |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sales | 45,000 | 39,000 | 6,000 less |
| Variable costs | 21,000 | 18,200 | 2,800 less |
| Fixed costs | 8,000 | 8,000 | 0 |
| **Profit** | **16,000** | **12,800** | **3,200 less** |

She sold 260 litres instead of 300 (260 × 150 = 39,000 and 260 × 70 = 18,200). Now she can ask *why*: Was it the rainy season? Did a big customer stop buying? This is how a budget turns into better decisions.

> **Tip:** Make three versions when you are unsure: a low, a likely and a high estimate of sales. Plan your fixed costs so that even the low estimate does not sink you.

## Budgeting habits

- Review the budget weekly, not just at month end.
- Include small costs – they add up.
- Set aside money for things that come once a year (permit renewal, equipment repairs) by saving a little every month.
