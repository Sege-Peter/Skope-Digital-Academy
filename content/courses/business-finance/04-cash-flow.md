**Cash flow** is the movement of money into and out of your business over time. A business can be profitable on paper and still fail because it runs out of cash at the wrong moment. Cash is what pays rent, suppliers and you.

## Profit is not the same as cash

Achieng sells school bags to a shop in Kisii. In January she delivers 40 bags at KES 800 each – that is KES 32,000 of sales. But the shop will pay her at the end of February. Meanwhile she has already spent KES 18,000 on materials and has rent due.

On paper her January profit looks good. In reality her M-Pesa balance is almost empty. That is a **cash flow problem**.

## Cash in and cash out

| Cash comes in from | Cash goes out to |
|---|---|
| Cash and M-Pesa sales | Buying stock and materials |
| Customers paying what they owe | Rent, electricity, internet |
| Money you put in (your capital) | Wages |
| Loans received | Loan repayments |
| | Owner's drawings |

## A simple cash flow forecast

A **cash flow forecast** predicts your cash for the coming weeks or months, so you can see a shortage before it happens.

| | Week 1 | Week 2 | Week 3 | Week 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opening cash | 5,000 | 3,000 | 6,500 | 2,500 |
| + Cash in | 8,000 | 9,500 | 7,000 | 10,000 |
| − Cash out | 10,000 | 6,000 | 11,000 | 7,000 |
| **Closing cash** | **3,000** | **6,500** | **2,500** | **5,500** |

Each week: Closing cash = Opening cash + Cash in − Cash out. The closing cash of one week becomes the opening cash of the next.

Week 3 is tight because a big stock order goes out. Knowing this early, the owner can delay a purchase, collect a debt or keep a small reserve.

## Ways to improve cash flow

- **Get paid faster** – ask for a deposit (for example half upfront) on custom orders.
- **Limit credit** – set clear payment dates and follow up politely.
- **Pay suppliers on time, not early** – unless there is a discount for early payment.
- **Do not overstock** – stock sitting on a shelf is cash you cannot use.
- **Keep a cash reserve** – enough to cover at least a few weeks of fixed costs.
- **Watch seasons** – January school shopping, December holidays and rainy seasons change sales.

> **Warning:** Do not solve a cash shortage by taking one mobile loan to repay another. That spiral quickly becomes very expensive. Plan ahead with a forecast instead.
