**Business finance** is about how money comes into a business, how it is spent, and how you make sure there is always enough to keep going. You do not need to be good at maths – you need to be organised and honest with yourself about the numbers.

## Key words

| Word | Meaning | Example (Fatuma's juice stand) |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue (sales) | Money customers pay you | 60 cups × KES 50 = KES 3,000 |
| Costs (expenses) | Money spent to run the business | Fruit, sugar, cups, stall rent |
| Profit | Revenue minus costs | KES 3,000 − KES 1,900 = KES 1,100 |
| Capital | Money and items put into the business to start or grow it | Blender, cooler box, first stock |
| Cash | Money you can use right now (in hand or on M-Pesa) | KES 2,400 on the business line |

> **Note:** Revenue is not profit. A business can sell a lot and still lose money if its costs are too high.

## The biggest rule: separate the money

Many small businesses fail not because nobody buys, but because the owner spends the business money on personal things – lunch, airtime, a relative's emergency – without noticing.

Treat your business as a separate "person":

- Use a **separate M-Pesa line, Till number or Pochi la Biashara** for business.
- Keep business cash in a separate envelope or box, never in your personal wallet.
- Record every shilling that goes from the business to you.

## Pay yourself a salary

Instead of taking money whenever you need it, decide on a fixed amount you pay yourself – for example every Friday. Whatever is left stays in the business for stock and growth.

```text
Fatuma's week
Profit for the week:          KES 6,000
Her "salary" (fixed):         KES 3,500
Left in the business:         KES 2,500  → used to buy a second blender later
```

If a week is bad and the profit is lower than her salary, she takes less, not more. Over time, as the business grows, she can raise her salary.

## Owner's drawings

Money you take out of the business for personal use is called **drawings**. Drawings are not a business cost – they come out of profit. Writing them down shows you how much the business is really giving you.

> **Warning:** Never share your M-Pesa PIN with anyone, including a shop assistant or family member who helps at the stall. Give helpers a separate float and check it daily.

```exercise
Q: Fatuma sold 60 cups at KES 50 each. What was her revenue?
Revenue = KES ___
A: 3000 / 3,000
```

```exercise
Q: Fill in the term for money an owner takes out of the business for personal use.
Money taken out for personal use is called ___
A: drawings
```

## Practice task

If you run any business (or plan to), decide **how you will separate the money**: which line, envelope or account will be the business one? Write it down, and choose a fixed weekly amount you will pay yourself. If you do not have a business yet, track your own money in and money out for three days.
