You have now written HTML, CSS, a little JavaScript and real Python scripts. That is a genuine start. This lesson helps you decide where to go next and shows how people turn coding into income.

## Three popular paths

| Path | What you build | Main languages | Good first project |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Web development** | websites, online shops, booking systems | HTML, CSS, JavaScript, then PHP or Node.js | a site for a local business |
| **Data and automation** | reports, dashboards, scripts that save time | Python, SQL, spreadsheets | a sales summary from a CSV file |
| **Mobile apps** | Android and cross-platform apps | Kotlin, or JavaScript/Dart frameworks | a simple shop price list app |

There is no wrong choice. Many coders start with web development because you can see results instantly and every business needs a website. Others love data because it connects to fields like agriculture, health and finance.

> **Tip:** Pick the path that matches the problems you enjoy. If you like design and seeing things on screen, try web. If you enjoy numbers and patterns, try data.

## How coders earn

Income usually grows in stages. Amounts vary a lot by skill, client and location, so treat these as examples, not promises.

1. **Small local jobs** – a one-page site for a salon, setting up a Google Business profile, a simple price list. Beginners sometimes charge a few thousand shillings for small jobs while they build a portfolio.
2. **Freelancing online** – platforms like Upwork and Fiverr connect you to clients worldwide. Competition is high, so a strong portfolio and clear communication matter more than the price.
3. **Internships and attachments** – many tech companies and NGOs in Nairobi, Kisumu and Mombasa take attachés. You learn from experienced developers.
4. **Employment** – junior developer, data analyst or IT support roles. Employers look for a portfolio, a GitHub profile and evidence that you keep learning.
5. **Your own product** – a school management tool, a booking system or an app that solves a local problem.

## Build proof, not just certificates

Employers and clients want to **see** what you can do. Start now:

- Create a free **GitHub** account and upload your projects.
- Keep a simple portfolio page (you already know enough HTML to make one).
- Write a short description for each project: the problem, what you built, what you learned.

## Stay safe

> **Warning:** Scammers target new coders. Never pay a "registration fee" to get a job, never share your M-Pesa PIN or passwords, and be careful with "clients" who send a cheque or overpay and ask for a refund. Real clients pay you; you do not pay them to work.

## A realistic learning plan

- Practise **30–60 minutes a day**, five days a week. Consistency beats long, rare sessions.
- After this course, take a full course in your chosen path (for example, Skope's Python Programming or JavaScript Programming courses).
- Join a local tech community or online group to ask questions and find collaborators.
