Welcome to your first step into coding! You do not need a laptop full of software or a maths degree to start. You only need curiosity, a phone or computer with a browser, and the patience to try things more than once.

## Programs are instructions

A **program** is a list of instructions that a computer follows. **Programming** (or **coding**) is the skill of writing those instructions in a language the computer understands.

You already use dozens of programs every day:

- M-Pesa checks your PIN, your balance and then moves money.
- WhatsApp sends your message and shows two blue ticks when it is read.
- A matatu booking app shows seats, takes payment and prints a ticket.

Every one of those actions was written, line by line, by a person who learned the same basics you are learning now.

## Computers are fast but not clever

A computer can do millions of calculations a second, but it has no common sense. It does **exactly** what you tell it – nothing more, nothing less.

Imagine telling a friend, "Make me tea." Your friend knows to boil water, add tea leaves, milk and sugar. A computer would need every step spelled out:

1. Fill the sufuria with water.
2. Put it on the jiko.
3. Wait until the water boils.
4. Add one spoon of tea leaves.
5. Add milk and let it boil again.
6. Pour into a cup through a sieve.

If you forget step 2, the computer will wait forever for cold water to boil. That is what a **bug** is: an instruction that is missing, wrong or in the wrong order.

## Programming languages

Computers really only understand ones and zeros. Writing in ones and zeros would be painful, so people invented **programming languages** – special kinds of English-like text that are translated into machine instructions.

| Language | What it is often used for |
|---|---|
| HTML and CSS | The structure and look of web pages |
| JavaScript | Making web pages interactive |
| Python | Data, automation, school projects, back-end systems |
| Java / Kotlin | Android apps |
| SQL | Asking questions of databases |

In this course you will try **Python** and **HTML**, two of the friendliest starting points.

## Your first program

Here is a one-line Python program. Press **Try it Yourself »** and then the run button to see it work.

```example python
print("Habari! I am learning to code.")
```

```output
Habari! I am learning to code.
```

`print()` tells Python to show whatever is inside the brackets. The text inside the quote marks is called a **string**.

> **Tip:** Change the message to your own name and run it again. Breaking and changing examples is the fastest way to learn.

## Why coding is a useful skill

Coding is not only for people who want to become software engineers. A shop owner in Eldoret can use a small script to total daily sales. A secretary can automate a boring report. A designer can build a portfolio website. Many coders also earn by taking on small projects for local businesses or online clients, which you will look at in week 4.

> **Warning:** Be careful of adverts promising "learn to code in 3 days and earn KES 100,000 a month". Real skills take steady practice over weeks and months.
