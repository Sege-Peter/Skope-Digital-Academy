Customer service is one of the most common entry-level remote jobs. Many companies – in Kenya and abroad – hire support agents to answer customers by chat, email or phone from home or from a contact centre. The skills you've learned in this course are exactly what they look for.

## Types of customer service roles

| Role | What you do | Where |
|---|---|---|
| **Contact centre agent** | Answer calls and messages for a company | Office-based contact centres, sometimes hybrid |
| **Chat / email support agent** | Help customers in writing on live chat or email | Often remote |
| **Social media support** | Reply to comments and messages for a brand | Remote or in-office |
| **Freelance support VA** | Handle a small business's customer messages | Remote, via freelancing platforms |
| **Front desk / reception** | Greet and assist visitors in person | Hotels, hospitals, offices |

## Skills employers look for

- **Clear written English** – correct spelling, grammar and polite tone. Kiswahili and other languages are a bonus.
- **Typing speed and accuracy** – chat agents often handle more than one conversation at a time.
- **Computer skills** – using a browser, Google Workspace or Microsoft tools, and learning new systems quickly.
- **Patience and empathy** – especially with frustrated customers.
- **Problem-solving** – finding answers in help articles and knowing when to escalate.
- **Reliability** – logging in on time for every shift.

## Tools you might use

Support teams use **help desk** software to manage customer messages as "tickets". You don't need to know any specific tool before you start – employers usually train you – but knowing the idea helps in interviews.

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Ticket** | One customer request, with its full conversation history |
| **Queue** | The list of tickets waiting to be answered |
| **Escalate** | Pass a ticket to a senior person or specialist team |
| **Macro / canned response** | A saved reply for common questions (personalise it!) |
| **Knowledge base** | Help articles that agents and customers can search |
| **Response time** | How quickly you first reply to a customer |

## Setting up to work from home

- A **laptop** (many roles don't allow phone-only work) and a **headset** with a microphone.
- **Reliable internet** plus a **backup** (for example a mobile data bundle or hotspot).
- A plan for **power cuts** – a charged laptop, a power bank, or a small UPS for your router.
- A **quiet space** for calls.
- Good **time-zone awareness** – shifts for international customers may be early mornings, evenings or weekends.

## Applying for roles

1. Update your **CV** to highlight service experience – a school leadership role, helping in a family shop, church or community service, or a holiday job all count.
2. Prepare a **short writing sample**: a polite reply to a complaint email.
3. Practise **typing tests** online.
4. Look on LinkedIn, Kenyan job boards (such as BrighterMonday, Fuzu and MyJobMag), company career pages and freelancing platforms.
5. Prepare for common interview questions:

```text
- Tell us about a time you helped someone with a problem.
- How would you handle a customer who is shouting?
- What would you do if you didn't know the answer?
- Why do you want to work in customer service?
```

Use the methods from this course – active listening, LEARN and calm phrases – in your answers.

## Avoiding fake job offers

> **Warning:** Customer service job scams are common. Be very careful if a "recruiter" asks you to pay for training, uniforms, medical checks or a "placement fee"; offers a job without any interview; communicates only via WhatsApp or Telegram from a personal number; or asks for your ID, bank PIN or M-Pesa PIN. Check that the company has a real website and official contacts. For jobs abroad, Kenya's National Employment Authority (nea.go.ke) publishes information about registered recruitment agencies – check there before you apply or pay anything.

## Practice task

Write a **three-line summary of your customer service experience** for your CV (any experience counts – family business, school roles, volunteering). Then take a free online typing test and record your words-per-minute and accuracy, so you can track your progress each week.
