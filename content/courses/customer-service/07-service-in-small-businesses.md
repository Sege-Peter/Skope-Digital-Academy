Most Kenyans buy daily goods from small businesses: kiosks, salons, boda boda riders, mama mbogas, hardware shops, cyber cafés, tailors and online sellers. Big companies have call centres and training departments. A small business has something just as powerful: **personal relationships**. Good service is often the main reason customers choose one small shop over the one next door.

## What makes small-business service special

| Advantage | How to use it |
|---|---|
| **You know your customers** | Remember names, usual orders and preferences |
| **You can decide quickly** | Solve problems on the spot without long approvals |
| **You're close to the community** | Support local events, schools and groups |
| **You can be flexible** | Deliver, reserve items, offer to call when stock arrives |

## Simple service habits that win customers

1. **Be open when you say you're open.** Put your hours on the door, on Google Maps and on WhatsApp – and keep them.
2. **Show prices clearly.** Customers trust a business that doesn't change prices depending on who is asking.
3. **Keep the place clean and organised.** It shows care and respect.
4. **Give correct change and receipts** – even for small amounts, or at least an M-Pesa confirmation.
5. **Remember regular customers:** "Karibu tena, Mama Brian! The usual two loaves?"
6. **Follow up:** a short WhatsApp message after a big purchase – "Is the gas cooker working well?"
7. **Make payment easy:** clearly display your Till or Paybill number and the business name customers should see when they pay.

## Example: Achieng's salon

Achieng runs a small salon in Kisumu. She noticed customers were leaving because they waited too long without knowing when they'd be served. She made three changes:

- Customers can **book on WhatsApp**, and she sends a reminder the day before.
- Walk-in customers are told honestly how long the wait is, and offered tea or a seat.
- She keeps a small **notebook of regular customers** – their preferred styles and products.

Her customers now recommend her in their WhatsApp groups, and her bookings have grown steadily.

## When you are the employee

If you work for a small business owner, you represent them completely. Customers will judge the whole business by how you behave. Treat the business's money, stock and customers as if they were your own:

- Be **honest** with cash and M-Pesa payments.
- **Tell the owner** about complaints and ideas you hear from customers.
- **Don't promise** discounts or credit without the owner's permission.

## Credit and "nitakulipa kesho"

Many small businesses lose money by giving goods on credit. If your business allows credit:

- Have a clear rule about **who** can get credit and **how much**.
- **Write it down** – name, phone, amount, date, promised payment date.
- Send polite reminders.

```text
Hello Mr Kamau, hope you're well. A friendly reminder about
the KES 1,200 balance from 3 March. You can pay to our Till
number anytime. Thank you for being a valued customer!
```

> **Tip:** Being friendly and being firm can go together. A polite, written system protects the relationship as well as the money.

## Online small businesses

Many young Kenyans sell on Instagram, TikTok, Facebook Marketplace or WhatsApp. Online customers can't see or touch your products, so trust matters even more:

- Use **real photos** of your actual products.
- State **prices, delivery areas and delivery costs** clearly.
- **Reply quickly** and politely, even to "how much?" with no greeting.
- Deliver **what you showed**, when you said.

## Practice task

Visit or think of a small business you use often. Write down **three small, low-cost changes** that would improve its customer service (for example, showing prices clearly or sending booking reminders). If you know the owner, share one idea politely.
