Many Kenyan customers contact businesses by phone call or WhatsApp before they visit or buy. For a small business, that first call or message is the "front door". Etiquette means the polite, professional way of doing things.

## Answering the phone

### A professional greeting

Answer within a few rings and use a clear greeting with four parts: **greeting + business name + your name + offer to help**.

```text
"Good morning, Baraka Hardware, this is Kiprono speaking.
How may I help you?"
```

### During the call

- **Smile while you speak** – it changes your tone.
- **Speak clearly** and not too fast. Avoid eating or chewing.
- **Use the caller's name** once you know it.
- **Take notes**: name, number, what they need.
- **Ask before putting someone on hold**: "May I put you on hold for a minute while I check?" – then thank them when you return.
- **Don't leave callers waiting** for long; if it will take time, offer to call back and give a time.

### Ending the call

Summarise what will happen next and close politely:

```text
"So I'll deliver the 10 bags of cement tomorrow before
11 am to Kondele. Thank you for calling Baraka Hardware,
Mr Otieno. Have a good day."
```

> **Tip:** If you miss a call from a customer, call back as soon as you can. In a small business, a missed call can be a missed sale.

## WhatsApp for business

WhatsApp is often the main sales and service channel for Kenyan small businesses. Many use the **WhatsApp Business** app, which lets you set up a business profile, catalogue, quick replies, labels and an away message.

### WhatsApp do's and don'ts

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| Reply quickly – or use an away message outside working hours | Leave customers on "blue ticks" for hours |
| Greet properly: "Hello Fatuma, thank you for contacting us!" | Reply with just "?", "yes" or "ok" |
| Write in short, clear messages with correct prices | Send ten tiny messages one after another |
| Use clear photos of products with prices | Send blurry photos with no information |
| Use voice notes only if the customer is comfortable with them | Send long voice notes for information they need to read later |
| Use emojis lightly, if the customer uses them too | Use slang or too many emojis with new customers |
| Respect privacy and opt-outs | Add customers to broadcast lists or groups without permission |

### Useful features (WhatsApp Business)

- **Quick replies** – save common answers like opening hours, location and payment details.
- **Labels** – mark chats as New customer, Pending payment, Order complete.
- **Catalogue** – show products with prices and descriptions.
- **Away message** – "Thank you for your message! We're open 8 am – 6 pm, Monday to Saturday. We'll reply first thing tomorrow."

## Payment messages and safety

When customers pay by M-Pesa, confirm the payment in your **own M-Pesa messages or app** before releasing goods – never from a screenshot the customer forwards, as these can be faked.

> **Warning:** Never ask customers for their M-Pesa PIN, and never share yours. Be careful with messages asking you to "reverse" a payment sent "by mistake"; check your real balance first and use official Safaricom channels for reversals.
