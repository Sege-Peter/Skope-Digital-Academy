Feedback is information from customers about their experience – good or bad. It tells a business what to keep doing, what to fix and what new things customers want. Businesses that listen and act on feedback improve faster than those that guess.

## Ways to collect feedback

| Method | How it works | Good for |
|---|---|---|
| **Ask in person** | "How was everything today?" | Small shops, salons, restaurants |
| **WhatsApp follow-up** | A short message after a purchase or service | Delivery, repairs, online sellers |
| **Google Forms survey** | A short online form with a link | Schools, events, training, online businesses |
| **Google reviews** | Customers rate the business on Google Maps | Any business with a location |
| **Suggestion box** | Customers write notes anonymously | Offices, hospitals, schools |
| **Social media comments** | Customers comment publicly | Online sellers, brands |

## Asking good feedback questions

Keep surveys **short** – 3 to 5 questions. Busy customers won't fill in 20.

```text
1. How happy were you with our service today? (1–5)
2. What did we do well?
3. What is one thing we could do better?
4. Would you recommend us to a friend? (Yes / Maybe / No)
5. Anything else you'd like to tell us?
```

Mix **rating questions** (easy to count) with **open questions** (which explain the "why").

> **Tip:** The best time to ask is soon after the service, while the experience is fresh – for example, a WhatsApp message the same evening.

## Responding to feedback

### Positive feedback
Say thank you – briefly and personally.

```text
Thank you so much, Kiprono! We're glad the delivery was fast.
See you next time!
```

### Negative feedback
Thank them, apologise for the experience, and explain what you'll do. Use the same calm approach as in [Handling Complaints](handling-complaints).

```text
Thank you for telling us, Grace. We're sorry the wait was
so long on Saturday. We've added a second stylist on weekends
to reduce waiting time. We hope to serve you better next time.
```

Never delete honest negative reviews or reply angrily. Never post fake reviews of your own business – customers can often tell, and platforms like Google have rules against them.

## Turning feedback into action

Collecting feedback is useless unless you act on it. Use a simple log:

| Date | Feedback | Category | Action | Done? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3 Mar | "Waited 40 minutes" | Speed | Add weekend stylist | Yes |
| 5 Mar | "Can you deliver to Kakamega?" | New request | Check delivery cost | In progress |
| 8 Mar | "Very friendly staff" | Praise | Thank the team | Yes |

Each month, look for **patterns**: if five customers mention the same problem, it's a priority. If many ask for the same thing, it may be a new business opportunity.

## Close the loop

When you make a change because of feedback, **tell customers**: "You asked, we listened – we now deliver on Sundays!" This shows customers their voice matters, and they will give more feedback in future.

## Respect privacy

Only collect the information you need. If you collect phone numbers or emails in a survey, tell customers why and don't use them for anything else without permission.

## Practice task

Create a **five-question Google Form** for a real or imaginary small business, using the sample questions as a guide. Send it to at least three friends or family members, then write down one pattern you notice and one action the business could take.
