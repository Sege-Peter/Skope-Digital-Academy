Email is slower than WhatsApp but more formal, and it creates a permanent record. Banks, schools, hotels, online shops and companies abroad use email for customer service every day. A well-written email solves the problem the first time and makes the business look professional.

## The parts of a good customer email

| Part | Purpose | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Subject line** | Tells the customer what the email is about | "Your order #2041 – delivery update" |
| **Greeting** | Polite opening using their name | "Dear Ms Wambui," / "Hello Brian," |
| **Thank or acknowledge** | Show you read their message | "Thank you for letting us know about…" |
| **Answer / solution** | The main information, clearly | "Your parcel will arrive on Thursday." |
| **Next step** | What happens now, and when | "Our rider will call you before delivery." |
| **Closing** | Friendly ending | "Kind regards," |
| **Signature** | Name, role, business, contact | "Achieng Odhiambo, Customer Care, Lake Fashions" |

## Example: answering a delay complaint

```text
Subject: Your order #2041 – delivery update

Dear Ms Wambui,

Thank you for your email, and I'm sorry your order has not
arrived as expected.

I have checked with our delivery team. Your parcel left our
Nairobi store yesterday and is now at our Nakuru pickup point.
It will be delivered to your address on Thursday between
10 am and 1 pm.

Our rider will call you on 0712 xxx xxx before arriving.
If that time does not suit you, just reply to this email
and we will arrange another time.

Kind regards,
Achieng Odhiambo
Customer Care | Lake Fashions
```

Notice: it **apologises** once, gives **specific facts**, explains the **next step** and gives the customer a **choice**.

## Writing rules

- **Keep it short.** Short paragraphs of 2–3 sentences. Use a list for steps.
- **Answer every question** the customer asked. Re-read their email before sending.
- **Be specific.** "Thursday between 10 am and 1 pm" is better than "soon".
- **Use plain English.** Avoid jargon and overly formal phrases like "kindly be advised that…".
- **Check names and spelling.** Spelling a customer's name wrong feels disrespectful.
- **No capital letters for whole sentences.** IT LOOKS LIKE SHOUTING.
- **Check attachments** are actually attached before you click Send.

## Tone: the "read it as the customer" test

Before sending, read your email as if you were the customer. Does it sound helpful, or cold?

| Cold | Warm and professional |
|---|---|
| "As stated in our policy, refunds take 14 days." | "Your refund has been approved. It usually takes up to 14 days to reach your account – I'll check on it for you if it hasn't arrived by then." |
| "You did not provide your order number." | "Could you please share your order number so I can check this for you?" |

*(The 14 days above is only an example – always use your own company's real policy.)*

## Response time

Customers expect a reply, even if you don't have the full answer yet. Send a quick acknowledgement:

```text
Thank you for your email. I'm checking this with our
warehouse and will update you by 3 pm today.
```

> **Warning:** Customer emails often contain personal data – phone numbers, addresses, ID numbers, payment details. Never forward them outside the business or reply-all to people who shouldn't see them. Kenya's Data Protection Act protects personal data; you can learn more on odpc.go.ke.

## Practice task

Write a reply email to this customer: *"I paid for a school bag three days ago and haven't heard anything. Is this a scam?!"* Use all seven parts, apologise once, give specific next steps, and apply the "read it as the customer" test before you finish.
