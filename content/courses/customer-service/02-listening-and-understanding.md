Most customer problems get worse because nobody really listened. The customer explains, the staff member interrupts, jumps to the wrong answer, and the customer has to explain again – now angrier. **Active listening** means paying full attention so you understand what the customer actually needs.

## Hearing vs listening

- **Hearing** is when sound reaches your ears.
- **Listening** is when you focus, understand and respond to what was said – and how it was said.

You can hear someone while scrolling your phone. You cannot truly listen that way.

## The four steps of active listening

| Step | What you do | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 1. **Attend** | Stop other tasks, face the customer, make eye contact | Put the phone down, turn towards them |
| 2. **Let them finish** | Don't interrupt, even if you think you know the answer | Nod, say "mm-hmm", "I see" |
| 3. **Ask** | Ask questions to fill in missing details | "When did you buy it?" |
| 4. **Confirm** | Repeat the main point in your own words | "So the phone stopped charging two days after you bought it – is that right?" |

Step 4 is powerful. It shows the customer they were understood, and it catches misunderstandings before you act.

## Open and closed questions

| Type | Starts with | Gets | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Open** | What, how, why, tell me | Explanations and detail | "What happened when you tried to pay?" |
| **Closed** | Did, is, can, do | A short yes/no or fact | "Did you receive an M-Pesa confirmation message?" |

Use **open questions** to understand the situation, then **closed questions** to confirm details.

## Listening to feelings, not just facts

Customers often tell you how they feel without saying it directly. Listen for words like "again", "nobody", "always", "I've been waiting". These tell you the customer is frustrated, not just reporting a fault.

Show you understand – this is called **empathy**:

```text
"I understand – it's really frustrating to come back
a second time for the same problem. Let's sort it out now."
```

Empathy is not the same as agreeing that the business was wrong. It simply recognises the customer's feelings.

## Body language and tone

On the phone, your **tone of voice** carries a lot of the meaning. Face to face, your **body language** does too.

| Shows you're listening | Shows you're not |
|---|---|
| Eye contact, nodding | Looking at your phone or around the room |
| Open posture, facing the customer | Crossed arms, turned away |
| Calm, warm voice | Sighing, flat or impatient tone |
| Taking notes | Asking the same question twice |

> **Tip:** Write things down – especially names, phone numbers, order numbers and dates. It shows care and prevents mistakes. Read numbers back: "That's 0722 123 456?"

## Language and respect

In Kenya, customers may prefer English, Kiswahili, Sheng or a local language. If you can, respond in the language the customer is comfortable with. If you can't, speak slowly and clearly, and find a colleague who can help.

## Practice task

Have a five-minute conversation with a friend or family member where they describe a problem they had with a product or service. Practise all four steps: don't interrupt, ask at least two open questions, and finish by confirming: "So what you're saying is…". Ask them afterwards whether they felt listened to.
