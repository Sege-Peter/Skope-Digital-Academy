Think of a shop you always go back to. Maybe the attendant remembers your name, the prices are fair, or they sort out problems quickly without arguing. Now think of a place you avoid – maybe someone was rude, ignored you, or made you wait without explanation.

That difference is **customer service**: how a business treats people before, during and after they buy.

## Why service matters

- **Customers come back.** It usually costs a business much more effort to find a new customer than to keep an existing one.
- **Word of mouth spreads.** In Kenya, people talk – in the matatu, in chamas, on WhatsApp groups and Facebook. One bad experience can be shared with hundreds of people in an hour. So can a good one.
- **Good service can beat lower prices.** Many customers will pay a little more where they feel respected and helped.
- **It's a job skill everywhere.** Banks, hospitals, hotels, shops, schools, telcos and online businesses all need people who can serve customers well.

## Who is a customer?

A customer is anyone you serve. That includes:

- **External customers** – people who buy from or use the business.
- **Internal customers** – colleagues who depend on your work, such as the cashier who needs you to restock, or the manager waiting for your report.

Treating colleagues well is part of good service too.

## What is a service mindset?

A service mindset is the attitude: *"How can I help this person get what they need?"* rather than *"How can I finish quickly and get rid of them?"*

| Without a service mindset | With a service mindset |
|---|---|
| "That's not my department." | "Let me take you to the right person." |
| Continues chatting on the phone while a customer waits | Puts the phone away and greets the customer |
| "We don't have it." | "We don't have it today. It should arrive Thursday – shall I call you?" |
| Blames the customer | Looks for a solution |

## The basics that always work

1. **Greet** every customer – "Habari yako? Karibu!" – and make eye contact.
2. **Smile** and use a friendly tone. Customers can hear a smile even on the phone.
3. **Be ready** – clean workspace, products in place, phone away.
4. **Use names** when you know them: "Thank you, Mama Achieng."
5. **Keep promises.** If you say you'll call back at 3 pm, call at 3 pm.
6. **Thank them** – even if they didn't buy.

> **Tip:** Respect is the foundation. Serve every customer with the same respect, whether they arrive in a big car or on foot, whether they speak English, Kiswahili or another language.

## Meet Brian

Brian works at a phone accessories shop in Nakuru. A customer walks in looking for a charger for an old phone model. Brian doesn't have it. Instead of just saying "Hatuna", he checks with a supplier, finds one for the next day, takes the customer's number, and sends a WhatsApp message when it arrives. The customer buys the charger – and later brings her whole family to buy phone covers from Brian.

## Practice task

Next time you buy something – at a shop, a kiosk, a supermarket or online – **observe the service**. Write down three things the business did well and one thing they could improve. Then write how *you* would have handled that one thing.
