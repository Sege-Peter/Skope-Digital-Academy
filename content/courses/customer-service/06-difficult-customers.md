Most customers are reasonable. But sometimes you'll meet someone who shouts, insults, demands the impossible or refuses to listen. Handling these moments calmly is one of the most respected skills in customer service – and it protects you, your colleagues and the business.

## Stay calm first

You can't calm someone else if you're upset yourself.

- **Breathe slowly** before you respond.
- **Lower your voice** rather than raising it – people often match the tone you use.
- **Don't take it personally.** They are angry about the situation.
- **Keep your body language open** – no crossed arms, eye-rolling or sighing.

## Common types and how to respond

| Type | What they do | How to respond |
|---|---|---|
| **The angry customer** | Shouts, complains loudly | Let them vent, then use LEARN; move to a quieter spot if possible |
| **The demanding customer** | Wants special treatment or something impossible | Be polite but clear about what you *can* do; offer options |
| **The indecisive customer** | Can't decide, asks many questions | Be patient; ask questions to narrow choices; recommend 2–3 options |
| **The know-it-all** | Insists they know better | Respect their knowledge; focus on facts and solutions |
| **The silent / unhappy customer** | Doesn't complain but looks dissatisfied | Approach and ask gently: "Is everything okay with your order?" |
| **The bargainer** | Keeps pushing for a lower price | Stay friendly; explain value; know your limits on discounts |

## Phrases that help

| Instead of… | Say… |
|---|---|
| "Calm down!" | "I can see this is really frustrating. Let's fix it." |
| "That's not my problem." | "Let me find out who can help with this." |
| "You're wrong." | "Let me check the details with you." |
| "We can't do that." | "Here's what I can do for you…" |
| "It's our policy." (alone) | "Our policy is…, and here's how I can still help…" |

The word **"Calm down"** almost always makes people angrier. Show understanding instead.

## Setting boundaries

Good service does **not** mean accepting abuse. If a customer insults you, threatens you, or becomes aggressive:

1. Stay calm and polite but firm: *"I want to help you, but I need us to speak respectfully."*
2. If it continues, **call your supervisor** or a colleague.
3. If there is any threat to your safety, **step away** and get help – security, your manager or the police.

On WhatsApp, email or social media, you can end an abusive conversation politely and pass it to your supervisor.

> **Warning:** Some "difficult customers" are actually scammers – for example, someone who insists you release goods based on a forwarded M-Pesa screenshot, or pressures you to "reverse" a payment quickly. Pressure and urgency are warning signs. Always confirm payments in your own records first.

## Public complaints online

Sometimes a customer complains on Facebook, X (Twitter) or Google reviews. Reply **calmly and publicly once**, then move the details to a private chat:

```text
Hello Kevin, we're sorry about your experience at our Thika
branch yesterday. We'd like to make this right – please send us
a message with your phone number so our manager can call you today.
```

Never argue in public. Other customers will read your reply, and they judge the business by how calm and helpful it is.

## Look after yourself

Dealing with difficult people can be tiring. After a hard conversation, take a short break if possible, talk to a colleague, and remember that one angry customer is not a judgement of your worth.
