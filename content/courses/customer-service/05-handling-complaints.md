Nobody enjoys complaints, but they are valuable. A customer who complains is giving the business a chance to fix the problem. Many unhappy customers never complain – they simply leave and tell their friends. Handle a complaint well, and an angry customer can become a loyal one.

## Why customers complain

- The product was faulty or not as described.
- The service was slow, rude or wrong.
- A promise was broken (late delivery, no call back).
- They were charged the wrong amount.
- They feel ignored or disrespected.

Often the last one – feeling ignored – makes the others much worse.

## The LEARN method

Use this simple five-step method for almost any complaint:

| Step | What you do | What you might say |
|---|---|---|
| **L – Listen** | Let them explain fully without interrupting | "Please tell me what happened." |
| **E – Empathise** | Recognise their feelings | "I understand why you're upset – that's not the service you should get." |
| **A – Apologise** | Say sorry sincerely, once | "I'm sorry this happened." |
| **R – Resolve** | Find and agree a solution | "I can replace it now, or give you a refund. Which do you prefer?" |
| **N – Notify / follow up** | Do what you promised and check back | "I'll call you tomorrow to confirm it's working." |

## Example

Fatuma bought a blender from a shop in Mombasa. Two days later it stopped working, and she comes back upset.

```text
Fatuma: "I bought this blender on Monday and it's already dead!
         I need it for my juice business."

Staff:  "I'm sorry to hear that. Please tell me what happened."
        [listens fully, takes notes]
        "I understand – you depend on it for your business, so
        this is really inconvenient. I'm sorry.
        Let me test it… Yes, the motor isn't working. Since it's
        within our return period, I can replace it right now.
        Would that work for you?"

Fatuma: "Yes, please."

Staff:  "Here's the new one – let's test it together before you
        leave. And here's my number; call me if you have any
        trouble this week."
```

## What to avoid

- **Arguing** or proving the customer wrong. Even if they are partly wrong, winning the argument means losing the customer.
- **Blaming** colleagues, suppliers or "the system".
- **Making promises you can't keep** – check what you're allowed to offer.
- **Passing the customer around** from person to person.
- **Taking it personally.** The customer is upset with the situation, not with you as a person.

## Know your limits and the policy

Every business should have a clear policy on returns, refunds and replacements. Learn it. If the customer asks for something you can't approve, say so honestly and involve your supervisor:

```text
"I can't approve a full refund myself, but I'll call my
supervisor now so we can sort this out for you today."
```

> **Note:** Customers in Kenya have legal consumer rights, for example under the Consumer Protection Act. If you are unsure what a customer is entitled to, follow your company policy and ask your supervisor – don't guess about the law.

## Record complaints

Write down every complaint: date, customer, problem, what was done, and whether it was resolved. If the same problem keeps coming up – for example, one product keeps breaking – the business can fix the cause. We look at this more in [Using Customer Feedback](using-customer-feedback).

## Practice task

Think of a complaint you once had as a customer (or invent one). Write a short dialogue like Fatuma's showing how a staff member should handle it using all five LEARN steps. Then role-play it with a friend, taking turns as the customer and the staff member.
