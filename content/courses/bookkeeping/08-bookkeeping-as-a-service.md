Many small businesses – salons, hardware shops, schools, chamas, churches – know they should keep records but do not have the time or skills. That is an opportunity for you to offer **bookkeeping as a paid service**.

## What you can offer as a beginner

| Service | What you do |
|---|---|
| Daily or weekly record keeping | Enter receipts, invoices and M-Pesa statements into a cash book or spreadsheet |
| Monthly summary | Prepare a one-page summary of sales, expenses, profit, debtors and creditors |
| Reconciliation | Check records against M-Pesa and bank statements and explain differences |
| Debtors follow-up list | Keep a list of who owes the business and for how long |
| Chama or group records | Track contributions, loans and repayments for members |
| Setting up a system | Design a simple spreadsheet or notebook system the owner can maintain |

> **Note:** Preparing official financial statements, audits and tax returns for clients is professional accounting work. In Kenya, accounting qualifications are examined by KASNEB and the profession is regulated. Stay within basic bookkeeping, and refer clients to a qualified accountant for tax filing and audits. Check what is allowed before offering any tax service.

## Finding your first clients

1. Start with people who know you – a relative's shop, your church, your chama.
2. Offer a **free first month** for one or two businesses in exchange for a written reference.
3. Show a sample monthly summary (with made-up numbers) so owners see the value.
4. Talk about **benefits**, not bookkeeping words: "You'll know your real profit every month" and "you'll have records ready if you apply for a SACCO loan."

## Pricing your service

Charge a **fixed monthly fee** based on how many transactions and hours the work takes, rather than a percentage of sales.

```text
Example only – your prices depend on your area and the client's size
Small kiosk, about 4 hours a month       KES 1,500 a month
Busier shop, about 8 hours a month       KES 3,000 a month
```

With three small clients and one busier one, the example monthly income would be 3 × 1,500 + 3,000 = KES 7,500. Real earnings vary a lot; treat these as illustrations, not promises.

## Professional habits

- **Confidentiality** – a client's numbers are private. Never discuss them with others. Kenya has a data protection law; handle personal data carefully.
- **Written agreement** – write down the services, fee, due date and what the client must give you each week.
- **Never handle client money** you are not authorised to, and never ask for their M-Pesa PIN or banking passwords. Ask them to download statements for you.
- **Deadlines** – deliver the monthly summary on the same date every month.
- **Keep learning** – free spreadsheet tutorials and, later, formal accounting qualifications if you enjoy the work.

## Course summary

You learned to:

1. Explain why records matter and identify transactions.
2. Issue and file receipts, invoices and other source documents.
3. Keep a cash book with a running balance.
4. Keep sales, debtors and expense records.
5. Apply debit and credit rules in double-entry bookkeeping.
6. Post to ledgers and prepare a simple trial balance.
7. Use a spreadsheet and produce a monthly summary.
8. Offer bookkeeping as a service professionally.

## Practice task

Write a **one-paragraph offer** for your bookkeeping service aimed at a specific type of business near you (for example, salons in your town). Include what you will do each month, what the owner gets, and an example fee. Show it to one business owner and ask for feedback.
