The **cash book** is the most important book for a small business. It records all money received and all money paid out, and shows how much money the business should have at any time.

## Layout of a simple cash book

A beginner-friendly cash book has these columns:

| Date | Details | Ref | Money in (KES) | Money out (KES) | Balance (KES) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

- **Details** – what happened ("Sales", "Bought stock from Wanjiku Wholesalers").
- **Ref** – the source document number (receipt 0118, M-Pesa code).
- **Balance** – a running total: previous balance + money in − money out.

## Worked example: Fatuma's cosmetics stall, June

Fatuma starts the month with KES 2,000 in the business.

| Date | Details | Ref | Money in | Money out | Balance |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Jun | Opening balance | – | | | 2,000 |
| 1 Jun | Sales | Sales book p.1 | 3,500 | | 5,500 |
| 2 Jun | Stock – lotions | Rcpt W221 | | 2,800 | 2,700 |
| 2 Jun | Transport | Note | | 200 | 2,500 |
| 3 Jun | Sales | Sales book p.2 | 4,100 | | 6,600 |
| 4 Jun | Electricity tokens | M-Pesa SJ7… | | 500 | 6,100 |
| | **Totals** | | **7,600** | **3,500** | |

Check the closing balance another way:

```text
Opening balance + total in − total out
2,000 + 7,600 − 3,500 = 6,100  ✓
```

If the two methods agree, the arithmetic is right.

## Cash and M-Pesa columns

Many Kenyan businesses hold money in two places: the cash box and M-Pesa (or a bank). A **two-column cash book** has separate in/out columns for each, so you can check both:

| Date | Details | Cash in | M-Pesa in | Cash out | M-Pesa out |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5 Jun | Sales | 1,200 | 2,300 | | |
| 5 Jun | Stock | | | | 3,000 |

When cash is deposited to M-Pesa (or withdrawn), record it as out of one column and in to the other. This is called a **contra entry** – the business has not gained or lost money, it has only moved it.

## Checking the cash book

At the end of each day or week:

1. **Count the cash** in the box and compare with the cash balance.
2. **Check the M-Pesa or bank balance** against the M-Pesa balance in the book.
3. If they differ, look for a missing entry, a wrong figure or a personal withdrawal not recorded.

> **Warning:** Never "adjust" the book to match the cash without finding out why. A shortage could be an error, a forgotten expense – or theft.

```exercise
Q: The balance is KES 4,000. Sales bring in KES 1,500 and you pay KES 900 for stock. What is the new balance?
New balance = KES ___
A: 4600 / 4,600
H: Balance + in − out.
```

```exercise
Q: Name the entry used when money moves from the cash box to M-Pesa.
Moving money between cash and M-Pesa is called a ___ entry.
A: contra
```
