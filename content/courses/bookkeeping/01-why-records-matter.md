**Bookkeeping** is the daily work of recording a business's money transactions – every sale, purchase, payment and receipt – in an organised way. **Accounting** uses those records to prepare reports, analyse performance and deal with tax. A bookkeeper makes sure the raw information is complete and correct.

## A story from Kisumu

Brian runs a small electronics shop. Every evening he counts the cash, and if there is "a lot", he feels the day was good. One month he bought a new phone for himself, paid his brother's fees, and restocked – all from the till. At the end of the month he could not say whether the shop had made a profit, how much stock he had, or how much he owed his supplier.

His neighbour Fatuma, who sells similar items, writes down everything. When a SACCO asked both of them for six months of records before giving a loan, only Fatuma could show them.

## What good records let you do

| Benefit | Example |
|---|---|
| Know your profit | "This month I made KES 18,400 after all costs." |
| Control cash | Notice that KES 1,000 is missing on the same day, not weeks later |
| Track debts | Know which customers owe you and which suppliers you owe |
| Make decisions | See which product sells best and which one wastes money |
| Get funding | Show a SACCO, bank or investor real numbers |
| Meet legal duties | Have the information needed to file tax returns correctly |

> **Note:** Businesses in Kenya have record-keeping and tax filing duties. The details depend on the type and size of business and can change. Check the current requirements on [kra.go.ke](https://www.kra.go.ke) or ask a qualified accountant.

## What is a transaction?

A **transaction** is any event that changes the money or property of the business:

- selling goods for cash or M-Pesa,
- buying stock,
- paying rent or wages,
- a customer buying on credit,
- the owner putting money in or taking money out.

Every transaction should be backed by a **source document** – a receipt, invoice, M-Pesa message or statement. You will learn about these in the next lesson.

## Golden rules of good bookkeeping

1. Record transactions **on the same day** – memory is unreliable.
2. Keep **business and personal** money separate.
3. Keep a **source document** for every entry.
4. Be **honest and complete** – record bad days too.
5. **Check** your records against cash and statements regularly.

## Practice task

For **one full day**, write down every money transaction you make (even personal ones): what it was, the amount, and whether you have proof (receipt, M-Pesa SMS). At the end of the day, count how many had a source document.
