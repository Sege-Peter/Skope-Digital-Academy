**Double-entry bookkeeping** is the system used by accountants around the world. Its idea is simple: every transaction affects **two** accounts, once as a **debit** and once as a **credit**, for the same amount. Because both sides are always equal, errors are easier to find.

## Accounts

An **account** is a record for one item: Cash, Sales, Rent, Stock purchases, Capital, a supplier, and so on. Accounts fall into five groups:

| Group | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Assets** – what the business owns | Cash, M-Pesa, bank, equipment, debtors |
| **Liabilities** – what it owes | Suppliers (creditors), loans |
| **Capital** – the owner's investment | Capital, drawings |
| **Income** | Sales |
| **Expenses** | Purchases, rent, transport, wages |

## Debit and credit rules

"Debit" (Dr) just means the **left side** of an account; "credit" (Cr) means the **right side**. Whether a debit increases or decreases an account depends on the group:

| Group | Increase is a… | Decrease is a… |
|---|---|---|
| Assets | Debit | Credit |
| Expenses (and drawings) | Debit | Credit |
| Liabilities | Credit | Debit |
| Capital | Credit | Debit |
| Income | Credit | Debit |

> **Tip:** A memory aid: **assets and expenses go up on the debit side**; everything else goes up on the credit side.

## Worked examples: Brian's shop, March

| Date | Transaction | Debit | Credit | KES |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Mar | Brian puts his savings into the business as cash | Cash | Capital | 20,000 |
| 2 Mar | Buys stock, pays cash | Purchases | Cash | 8,000 |
| 5 Mar | Sells goods for cash | Cash | Sales | 12,000 |
| 10 Mar | Pays shop rent in cash | Rent | Cash | 3,000 |
| 15 Mar | Buys stock on credit from Wanjiku Wholesalers | Purchases | Wanjiku Wholesalers | 5,000 |

Let us check the reasoning for two of them:

- **2 Mar:** Purchases is an expense that increases → debit. Cash is an asset that decreases → credit.
- **15 Mar:** Purchases increases → debit. Brian now owes Wanjiku Wholesalers, a liability that increases → credit. No cash moves yet.

Total debits = 20,000 + 8,000 + 12,000 + 3,000 + 5,000 = **48,000**. Total credits are the same 48,000, because each line has the same amount on both sides.

## The journal

Accountants often write transactions first in a **journal** before posting them to accounts:

```text
Date     Account                       Dr        Cr
15 Mar   Purchases                   5,000
           Wanjiku Wholesalers                 5,000
         (Stock bought on credit, invoice W-778)
```

The debit line comes first, the credit line is indented, and a short narration explains the entry.

> **Note:** In this course, buying stock for resale is recorded in a "Purchases" expense account – a common simple method. Larger businesses may use other methods for stock; an accountant can advise.

```exercise
Q: Brian pays KES 500 cash for transport. Which account is debited and which is credited?
Debit ___, credit ___
A: transport | cash
H: Transport is an expense that increases; cash is an asset that decreases.
```

```exercise
Q: Fill in the side on which assets increase.
Assets increase on the ___ side.
A: debit / dr
```
