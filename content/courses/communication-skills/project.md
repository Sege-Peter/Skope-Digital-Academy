## The situation

Fatuma is looking for a one-month attachment at a small office in Mombasa. The manager will judge her by how she writes, speaks and presents herself long before they meet. In this project you will make a "communication pack" for the same situation: an email, a chat message, a short talk and a feedback message. You can use your own life or a made-up person.

> **Note:** Use made-up details for names, phone numbers, email addresses and places of work. Do not post real screenshots of private chats, and never share anyone's personal information.

## What you will produce

One document (or a document plus a short voice note link) with four parts:

1. **Email** – a professional email of 80 to 150 words to a made-up manager, "Mr Kiprono at Pwani Supplies", asking for a one-month attachment. It needs a clear subject, greeting, purpose, a list of two or three strengths, a clear request with a date, and a signature.
2. **WhatsApp** – two versions of a message to the same manager: one that is too casual and one that follows the golden rules from the course. Add one sentence explaining the difference.
3. **Talk outline** – a 3-minute talk titled "Who I am and what I can do". Write the opening, three main points and the closing in bullet form (about 120 to 200 words). If you want, add a link to a voice note or video of yourself practising it.
4. **Feedback** – a short SBI message (situation, behaviour, impact) of 40 to 80 words thanking someone for help, plus 2 to 3 sentences about what you learned when you practised the talk in front of someone.

## Steps

1. Choose your person (you or a made-up one) and write down two strengths.
2. Write the email, then read it aloud to catch mistakes.
3. Write both WhatsApp versions.
4. Draft the talk and practise it once for a friend or family member.
5. Write the feedback message and your reflection.

## What to submit

- Your pack in the text box **or** as a PDF/Word file (max 5 MB).
- Optional: a link to a voice note or video (Google Drive).

## Tips

- Write the way you would speak to a respected elder: polite and clear, not stiff.
- Keep sentences short. One idea per sentence.
- Do not include a real phone number, only a made-up one.
