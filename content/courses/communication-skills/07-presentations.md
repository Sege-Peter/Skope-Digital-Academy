Sooner or later you will need to speak in front of a group: pitching your business at a youth fund event, explaining a project to your team, giving a speech at church or presenting in college. Many people fear this more than anything. The good news: a simple structure and practice make it much easier.

## A simple structure

Every short presentation can follow three parts:

| Part | What to do | Time (5-minute talk) |
|---|---|---|
| Opening | Grab attention and say what you will talk about | about 30 seconds |
| Body | Your 2–3 main points, each with an example | about 4 minutes |
| Closing | Summarise and say what you want the audience to do | about 30 seconds |

Three main points is a good number: easy for you to remember and for the audience to follow.

## Strong openings

Avoid starting with "Eeh, my name is… so today I will be talking about…". Try one of these instead:

- **A question:** "How many of you have lost money to a fake online job?"
- **A short story:** "Last year, my neighbour Kevin started selling eggs with KES 2,000…"
- **A surprising fact** (only if you are sure it is true and you know the source).

## A sample outline

```text
Topic: Why our youth group should start a car-wash business

Opening: "Every Saturday, more than 50 cars park outside our church.
          How many of them leave clean?"

Point 1: The need – cars wait 2–3 hours, owners want them washed.
Point 2: The cost – we already have water access; we need buckets,
         soap and a pressure pump (about KES 25,000 – see budget).
Point 3: The plan – 6 members on a rota, KES 300 per wash,
         M-Pesa till for payment.

Closing: "With your approval today, we can start next month.
          I'm asking for a vote to use the group savings."
```

## Using slides

Slides (PowerPoint, Google Slides or Canva) should **support** you, not replace you.

- One idea per slide.
- Few words: key phrases, not full paragraphs.
- Large text that people at the back can read.
- Pictures and simple charts instead of tables full of numbers.
- Never read your slides word for word; face the audience.

## Handling nerves

Nervousness is normal; even experienced speakers feel it. Ways to manage it:

1. **Practise out loud** at least three times, including once in front of a friend.
2. **Arrive early** and check the projector, mic and room.
3. **Breathe slowly** before you start.
4. **Find friendly faces** in the audience and speak to them.
5. **Accept small mistakes**; just continue. The audience rarely notices.

## Answering questions

- Listen to the whole question.
- Thank the person and repeat the question if the room is big.
- Answer briefly. If you do not know, say so honestly: "Good question. I don't have that figure now, but I'll find out and share it with you."

> **Tip:** Time yourself when practising. Running over time is one of the most common mistakes, and organisers notice.

```exercise
Q: Complete the presentation structure.

```

## Practice task

Choose a topic you know well, such as your favourite hobby, your home town or a small business idea. Prepare a 3-minute talk using the opening, body (three points) and closing structure. Practise it, time it, then present it to at least two people and ask for one thing you did well and one thing to improve.
