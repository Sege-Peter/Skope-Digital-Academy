Email is still the main way businesses, employers and international clients communicate formally. A clear, polite email makes you look professional even before anyone meets you.

## The parts of a good email

| Part | Tips |
|---|---|
| Subject line | Short and specific: "Quotation for 200 branded T-shirts" not "Hello" |
| Greeting | "Dear Ms. Wambui," or "Hello Brian," depending on how formal |
| Opening line | Say why you are writing |
| Body | Short paragraphs, one idea each; use a list for several items |
| Action | What you need from them, and by when |
| Closing | "Kind regards," or "Best regards," then your full name |
| Signature | Name, role or business, phone number |

## A sample email

```text
Subject: Request for quotation – 200 branded T-shirts

Dear Mr. Kamau,

I am writing on behalf of the Nyeri Youth Football Club to request a
quotation for 200 branded T-shirts for our tournament in April.

Our requirements are:
- 200 cotton T-shirts (100 medium, 100 large)
- Our club logo printed on the front, in two colours
- Delivery to Nyeri town by 20 March

Could you please send the total cost, including delivery, by Friday
7 March? I have attached our logo.

Kind regards,
Fatuma Wairimu
Secretary, Nyeri Youth FC
0712 000 000
```

## Formal versus casual

| Too casual | Professional |
|---|---|
| hi bro, sendeni ile quote haraka | Hello, could you please send the quotation by Friday? |
| ok | Thank you, I have received it and will reply by tomorrow. |
| why haven't u paid??? | I'm following up on invoice 012, which was due on 5 March. |

Avoid slang, all capital letters (it looks like shouting), many exclamation marks, and SMS spelling like "u" and "pls".

## Replying and following up

- **Reply within one working day**, even if only to say "Received, I'll get back to you by Thursday."
- **Reply All** only when everyone in the email needs your answer.
- **Follow up politely** if you get no reply after a few days: "I'm following up on my email below about…"

## Attachments

- Mention the attachment in the email: "Please find my CV attached."
- Use PDF for documents you do not want changed.
- Give files clear names: `Fatuma-Wairimu-Invoice-012.pdf`, not `IMG_20260305.pdf`.
- **Check that the attachment is actually attached** before you press Send.

> **Warning:** Be careful with emails asking you to click a link and "verify your account" or enter passwords. Check the sender's real address. Banks, M-Pesa and employers will not ask for your PIN by email.

## Before you press Send

1. Is the subject clear?
2. Is the name of the person spelled correctly?
3. Is the action and deadline clear?
4. Did I attach the file?
5. Did I read it once more for mistakes?

## Practice task

Write a professional email to a local business (real or imagined) asking for information: prices, opening hours, or whether they offer attachments for students. Include a clear subject, greeting, purpose, a list, an action with a deadline, and a signature. Send it to yourself first and check how it looks.
