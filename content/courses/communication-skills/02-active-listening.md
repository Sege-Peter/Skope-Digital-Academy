Most people think communication is mainly about talking well. In reality, **listening** is half of it, and maybe the more important half. People trust and like those who truly listen to them.

## Hearing versus listening

**Hearing** happens automatically: sound enters your ears. **Listening** is a choice to pay attention, understand and respond. You can hear your mother talking while you scroll TikTok, but you are not really listening.

**Active listening** means listening with full attention and showing the speaker that you understand.

## The five habits of an active listener

| Habit | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| Pay attention | Put the phone away, face the person, make comfortable eye contact |
| Show you are listening | Nod, say "mm-hmm", "I see", lean slightly forward |
| Do not interrupt | Let them finish before you respond, even if you disagree |
| Ask questions | "What happened next?" "What do you mean by…?" |
| Summarise | "So what you're saying is…" to check you understood |

## Open and closed questions

A **closed question** can be answered with one word, often yes or no: "Did you like the dress?"

An **open question** invites the person to explain: "What did you like about the dress?"

Open questions are powerful for understanding customers and colleagues.

```text
Customer: I want a phone.
Closed:   Do you want Samsung?           -> "No."
Open:     What will you mostly use the phone for?
          -> "Mainly WhatsApp, M-Pesa, and photos for my small
              business. My budget is around KES 15,000."
```

The open question gave the seller everything they needed to recommend the right phone.

## Paraphrasing

**Paraphrasing** means repeating the main point in your own words. It shows you understood and gives the speaker a chance to correct you.

```text
Client:  I need the logo by next week, something simple, green and
         white, because we sell organic vegetables in Limuru.
You:     So you'd like a simple green-and-white logo that shows
         you're an organic vegetable business, ready by next week.
         Is next Friday okay?
Client:  Yes, Friday is perfect.
```

## Common listening mistakes

- **Planning your reply** while the other person is still talking.
- **Finishing their sentences** for them.
- **Jumping to advice** when they only wanted to be heard.
- **Changing the subject** to your own story: "That's nothing, let me tell you what happened to me…"

> **Tip:** When someone is upset, start by acknowledging their feelings: "That sounds really frustrating." People calm down faster when they feel heard.

## Practice task

Have a 10-minute conversation with a family member or friend about their day or their work. Use at least three open questions and paraphrase them at least twice. Afterwards, write down one thing you learned about them that you did not know before.
