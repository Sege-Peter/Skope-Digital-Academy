Your first job brings new people: a supervisor, colleagues of different ages, customers, maybe suppliers. Each relationship needs slightly different communication. This lesson covers the everyday situations that new workers often find tricky.

## Talking to your supervisor

- **Be on time** and tell them early if you will be late or absent, with a reason.
- **Confirm instructions.** Repeat key details: "So you'd like the stock count done by 4 p.m., and the results in the exercise book?"
- **Bring problems early**, ideally with a suggestion: "The printer is jammed again. Should I call the technician, or use the one upstairs for now?"
- **Give updates without being asked**, especially on long tasks.

## Asking for help

New workers often stay silent because they fear looking stupid. In fact, asking good questions shows you care about doing it right.

```text
Weak:   "I don't understand anything about this system."
Better: "I've entered the sales, but I'm not sure how to print the
        daily summary. Could you show me once? I'll write down the
        steps."
```

## Dealing with customers

| Situation | What to say |
|---|---|
| Greeting | "Karibu! How can I help you today?" |
| You don't know | "Let me check that for you." (never just "I don't know") |
| Delay | "I'm sorry for the wait. It will be about 10 more minutes." |
| Complaint | Listen, apologise for the experience, explain what you will do |
| Saying no | "We don't have that size, but we have this one, or I can call you when it arrives." |

## Handling an angry customer: LEAP

- **L**isten without interrupting.
- **E**mpathise: "I understand why that's frustrating."
- **A**pologise for the experience (even if it was not your personal fault).
- **P**roblem-solve: offer a fix or call your supervisor.

Stay calm and never shout back. If someone becomes abusive or threatening, step back and get your supervisor or security.

## Respect and professionalism

- Use people's names and proper titles until invited to do otherwise.
- Avoid gossip. What you say about a colleague often reaches them.
- Keep personal phone use for breaks.
- Treat everyone with respect, from the cleaner to the director.
- Respect confidentiality: customer details, prices and company plans are not for sharing outside work.

## Difficult conversations

Some conversations are uncomfortable: asking for a day off, reporting a problem, or telling your boss you made a mistake. Prepare by writing down:

1. What happened (facts only).
2. How it affects the work.
3. What you suggest.

Then choose a good time and speak privately and calmly.

> **Warning:** If you face harassment or unfair treatment at work, write down what happened (date, time, what was said, any witnesses) and report it to your supervisor, HR or a trusted senior person. You can also seek advice from official labour offices.
