Employers, clients and colleges increasingly look you up online before they meet you. What you post, comment and share is communication too, and it can open doors or close them. This final lesson covers online communication and then tests what you have learned.

## Your digital footprint

Everything you post online leaves a **digital footprint**. Even "deleted" posts may have been screenshotted. Before posting, ask:

- Would I be comfortable if my future employer saw this?
- Is it true, and is it kind?
- Does it share anyone's private information, including my own?

## Professional online habits

| Do | Avoid |
|---|---|
| Use a clear profile photo and your real name on work platforms | Insulting people in comments, even when you disagree |
| Share useful content about your skills or business | Posting while angry |
| Reply to client messages within a working day | Forwarding unverified "news" |
| Disagree politely with reasons | Sharing other people's photos or chats without permission |
| Keep work and personal accounts separate if needed | Posting your ID, address or travel plans publicly |

## Online meetings

Online meetings on Google Meet, Zoom or Teams follow the same rules as in-person ones, plus a few more: join on time, mute when not speaking, turn your camera on if asked, use the chat for short questions, and avoid eating or moving around on camera.

## Writing for social media and clients

- Short sentences and clear calls to action: "Order via WhatsApp 0712 000 000."
- Correct spelling. Use a free tool like the spell-check in Google Docs before posting.
- Answer public complaints politely and move the details to a private message.

> **Warning:** Do not click unknown links or give login codes to people who message you online, even if they use a friend's account. Accounts get hacked and scammers use them to trick contacts.
