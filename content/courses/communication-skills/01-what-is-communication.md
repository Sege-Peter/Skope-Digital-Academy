Whatever work you do, whether you sell phones in Nairobi, design posters on Canva, serve guests at a hotel in Diani or start your own business, you will spend a large part of every day communicating. Employers regularly list "good communication skills" as one of the things they want most. This course will help you build them step by step.

## What is communication?

**Communication** is sharing information, ideas and feelings so that the other person **understands** them the way you meant. The key word is *understands*. If you said something but the other person understood something different, the communication did not fully work.

Every message has:

| Part | Example |
|---|---|
| Sender | Kiprono, the shop supervisor |
| Message | "Please restock the cooking oil shelf before 2 p.m." |
| Channel | Spoken face to face, a phone call, WhatsApp, email |
| Receiver | Fatuma, the new shop assistant |
| Feedback | Fatuma replies, "Okay, the 1-litre and 2-litre ones?" |

Feedback is how the sender knows the message was understood.

## Types of communication

- **Verbal**: spoken words, in person or on the phone.
- **Non-verbal**: body language, facial expressions, tone of voice, how you dress.
- **Written**: emails, WhatsApp, SMS, letters, reports, social media posts.
- **Visual**: charts, posters, pictures, slides.

Good communicators choose the right type for the situation. Bad news to a customer, for example, is usually better delivered by a phone call than a cold one-line text.

## What gets in the way?

Barriers are things that stop the message from being understood:

- **Noise**: a busy matatu stage, music, a poor network.
- **Language**: using English words the listener does not know, or heavy slang (sheng) in a formal setting.
- **Assumptions**: thinking the other person already knows what you mean.
- **Emotions**: anger or stress make it hard to listen.
- **Distractions**: checking your phone while someone talks.

> **Tip:** When a message is important, check understanding. Ask "Just to confirm, you need the report by Friday at 5 p.m.?"

## Communication and income

Clear communication helps you earn. A freelancer who replies clearly and quickly to clients gets repeat work. A salesperson who listens to what the customer needs sells more. A worker who explains problems early is trusted with more responsibility.

## Practice task

For one full day, notice every time a message is misunderstood around you, at home, in a shop, on WhatsApp. Write down three examples and, for each one, name the barrier (noise, language, assumption, emotion or distraction) and how it could have been avoided.
