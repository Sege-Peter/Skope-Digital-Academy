In Kenya, a lot of business happens on WhatsApp. Customers order on it, employers share shift schedules in groups, and freelancers chat with clients. Because it feels casual, people often forget it is still work communication. This lesson shows you how to message professionally.

## The golden rules of work messages

1. **Greet first.** "Good morning, Mrs. Otieno" before your question.
2. **Say everything in one message.** Do not send "Hi" and wait for a reply before asking your question.
3. **Be brief and clear.** Put the main point first.
4. **Use proper spelling.** Short forms like "pls", "u", "2moro" look careless with clients and bosses.
5. **Respect time.** Avoid messaging clients or managers late at night unless it is urgent or agreed.

## One complete message

```text
Weak (four messages):
  Hi
  Are you there?
  Ile order
  Iko aje?

Strong (one message):
  Good afternoon Mr. Kiprotich. I'm following up on order #45
  (2 bags of chicken feed) placed on Monday. Could you confirm
  if it will be delivered to Eldoret tomorrow? Thank you.
```

## Voice notes

Voice notes are convenient but can be annoying for the receiver, who may be in a meeting or a noisy matatu.

- Ask first, or use them only with people who like them.
- Keep them short (under one minute).
- Put important details like amounts, dates and addresses in **text** so they can be found later.

## Business replies to customers

If you sell on WhatsApp, every reply is part of your brand.

```text
Customer: Hi, how much is the blue handbag?

You: Hello, thank you for contacting Achieng Bags! The blue handbag
     is KES 1,800. It's genuine leather and fits an A4 file. We
     deliver within Kisumu for KES 150. Would you like to order?
```

The reply is friendly, answers the question, adds useful details and ends with a clear next step.

> **Tip:** WhatsApp Business (free) lets you set a greeting message, quick replies and a catalogue. It saves time and looks professional.

## Group etiquette

Work and class groups can get messy. Be the person who:

- Stays on topic; no forwarded jokes, politics or chain messages in work groups.
- Replies privately when the message is only for one person.
- Does not share screenshots of private chats without permission.
- Checks facts before forwarding "news", especially about jobs, money or health.

## Tone in writing

Without a face or voice, short messages can sound rude. "Ok." or "Fine." may read as angry. Add a few warm words: "Okay, thank you, I'll do that by 3 p.m."

> **Warning:** Never send your M-Pesa PIN, passwords or one-time codes on WhatsApp or SMS, even to someone who claims to be from Safaricom, your bank or your employer. Scammers often pretend to be people you trust.

## Practice task

Scroll through your recent WhatsApp chats with a teacher, employer, client or an older relative. Pick two messages you sent and rewrite them using the golden rules. If you run a small business, write a professional greeting message and two quick replies for your most common customer questions.
