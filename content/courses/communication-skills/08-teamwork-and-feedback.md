Very few jobs are done alone. Shop staff cover each other's shifts, a design agency works as a team, even a freelancer works with clients and sometimes other freelancers. How you communicate in a team affects how much gets done and how pleasant work is.

## What good team communication looks like

| Habit | Example |
|---|---|
| Agree on goals | "So our target is 100 flyers distributed by Saturday." |
| Share information early | "I'll be 30 minutes late; Achieng can open the shop." |
| Ask when unsure | "Do you want the report in Word or PDF?" |
| Respect others' ideas | "That's a good point. Could we also try…?" |
| Keep promises | Do what you said, by when you said it |
| Admit mistakes | "I sent the wrong price list. I've corrected it." |

## Disagreeing respectfully

Disagreement is normal and can lead to better ideas. Keep it about the **problem**, not the **person**.

```text
Attacking: "That's a stupid idea, Brian. It will never work."
Respectful: "I see why you suggest posting only on TikTok. I'm worried
            older customers aren't there. Could we try both TikTok and
            Facebook for two weeks and compare?"
```

## Giving feedback

Feedback helps people improve. Good feedback is **specific**, **kind** and about **behaviour**, not personality. A simple method is **Situation – Behaviour – Impact (SBI)**:

| Step | Example |
|---|---|
| Situation | "During yesterday's market day…" |
| Behaviour | "…you arrived at 10 a.m. instead of 8 a.m." |
| Impact | "…so I had to serve the morning rush alone and some customers left." |

Then add a request or question: "Can we agree that you'll be here by 8 on market days? Is something making it hard?"

Give praise the same way: "In today's meeting (situation) you explained the budget clearly with numbers (behaviour), and everyone understood our costs (impact). Thank you."

> **Tip:** Give critical feedback in private and praise in public when you can.

## Receiving feedback

Hearing criticism is hard. Try these steps:

1. **Listen fully** without interrupting or defending yourself.
2. **Thank them.** It took effort to tell you.
3. **Ask questions** to understand: "Can you give me an example?"
4. **Decide what to do.** You do not have to agree with everything, but look for the useful part.
5. **Follow up.** Show them you made the change.

```text
Supervisor: Your reports have had several calculation errors.
You:        Thank you for telling me. Which ones were wrong, so I
            can see where I went wrong? From now on I'll double-check
            totals in Excel before sending.
```

## Working in remote teams

When your team is spread across towns or works online, communicate even more clearly: share updates in the group, use shared documents (Google Docs or Sheets), and confirm decisions in writing.

```exercise
Q: Complete the SBI feedback method.
S stands for ___.
B stands for ___.
I stands for ___.
A: Situation | Behaviour / Behavior | Impact
H: Where it happened, what the person did, and what effect it had.
```

## Practice task

Think of someone who helped you recently: a friend, teacher, parent or colleague. Write and send them a short message of positive feedback using SBI. Then ask one person you trust for one piece of honest feedback on how you communicate, and practise receiving it using the five steps.
