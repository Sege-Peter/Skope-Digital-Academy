You do not need a "big" English vocabulary or a perfect accent to be a good speaker. Clear speakers use simple words, organise their ideas and speak at a pace people can follow. These are skills anyone can practise.

## Know your point first

Before you speak, especially in a meeting or on the phone, ask yourself: **What is the one thing I want them to know or do?** If you can say it in one sentence, you are ready.

```text
Unclear: "Eeh, so, there was this thing with the delivery, the guy
         came, but then the boxes, you know, some were okay and..."
Clear:   "Three of the ten boxes from today's delivery are damaged.
         Should I return them or call the supplier?"
```

The clear version gives the problem and asks for a decision. It takes less than ten seconds.

## Simple words win

Use everyday words. "We need to start the project" is better than "We need to commence the implementation of the project." Simple language is not a sign of low education; it is a sign of respect for your listener's time.

## Pace, volume and pauses

| Problem | Fix |
|---|---|
| Speaking too fast when nervous | Breathe, slow down, finish each sentence fully |
| Speaking too softly | Speak to the person at the back of the room |
| Too many fillers ("eeh", "like", "you know") | Replace them with a short silent pause |
| Flat, boring tone | Vary your voice; stress the important words |

A **pause** feels long to you but sounds natural and thoughtful to the listener.

## Language and setting

Kenya is multilingual, and that is a strength. Choose the language that suits your listener:

- **Formal English or Kiswahili** for interviews, meetings, clients and official calls.
- **Casual language or sheng** with friends, but be careful with it at work.
- If a customer is more comfortable in Kiswahili or a local language you share, switching can build trust.

## Talking on the phone

On a phone call, people cannot see your face, so your voice does all the work.

1. Greet and introduce yourself: "Good morning, this is Wanjiru from Bidii Printers."
2. State the reason for calling in one sentence.
3. Speak slowly and spell out names and numbers: "That's 0722, 123, 456."
4. Confirm what was agreed before you hang up.

> **Tip:** Smile when you answer the phone. People can hear it in your voice.

## Building confidence

Confidence grows with practice, not before it. Start small: answer a question in a group, make a call you have been avoiding, introduce yourself to someone new at church or a youth event. Each small success builds the next.

## Practice task

Record a one-minute voice note on your phone introducing yourself as if to a new employer: your name, where you are from, what you are good at, and what you want to do. Listen back and count your fillers. Record it again, aiming to cut them in half.
