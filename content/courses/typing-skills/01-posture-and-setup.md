Fast, accurate typing is one of the most useful skills you can build after school. Office jobs, online data-entry and transcription work, customer support, writing, coding and even filling in university or job applications all go faster when you can type well without looking at the keyboard. This is called **touch typing**, and this course will teach it step by step.

Before you learn a single key, set up your body and your workspace. Good posture lets you type for longer without pain, and comfort leads to speed.

## Why touch typing?

Many people type with two fingers while staring at the keys. They can be quick for short messages, but they tire easily, make more mistakes and cannot read what they are copying at the same time. Touch typists use all ten fingers, keep their eyes on the screen or the document, and improve steadily with practice.

> **Note:** Learning touch typing may make you *slower* for the first one or two weeks. That is normal. Stick with the correct method; it soon overtakes your old habit.

## Sitting correctly

| Body part | Good position |
|---|---|
| Feet | Flat on the floor (or on a box or a thick book if your feet don't reach) |
| Back | Upright and supported by the chair; don't hunch forward |
| Shoulders | Relaxed, not raised towards your ears |
| Elbows | Close to your sides, bent at about 90 degrees |
| Wrists | Straight and floating slightly above the keyboard; don't bend them up or rest them heavily while typing |
| Fingers | Gently curved, resting lightly on the keys |
| Eyes | Looking at the screen; top of the screen at about eye level |

## Setting up your workspace

- **Screen distance:** about an arm's length away.
- **Keyboard position:** directly in front of you, with the **B** key roughly in line with your belly button.
- **Chair height:** adjust so your forearms are roughly level with the desk.
- **Laptop users:** if you type for hours, a cheap external keyboard, or raising the laptop on books with a separate keyboard, helps your neck.
- **Lighting:** avoid strong light or a window shining directly on the screen.

## Breaks protect your hands

Typing for hours without rest can cause sore wrists, neck and eyes. Build these habits now:

- Every 20–30 minutes, stand up, stretch your fingers and shoulders, and look at something far away for about 20 seconds.
- Shake out your hands and gently roll your wrists between practice sessions.
- If you feel pain that doesn't go away, rest and talk to a health worker rather than pushing through.

## No computer at home?

Many learners in Kenya practise at a cyber café, a school or college lab, a library, or on a family member's laptop. Short, regular sessions are what matter. You can also practise finger positions on a printed keyboard diagram at home, and use a phone with a cheap OTG keyboard. Some online typing tools work on phone browsers with an external keyboard attached.

## Check your current level

Before you start, find out where you are. Open any typing test website (or wait for the speed tool in lesson 6) and type for one minute. Write down your speed and accuracy, even if they are low. You will compare later and be surprised by your progress.
