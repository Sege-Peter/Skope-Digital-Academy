With the home row in place, you can now reach up to the **top row** (Q W E R T Y U I O P) and down to the **bottom row** (Z X C V B N M , . /). The secret is that each finger moves in its own **column**, stretching up or down, pressing the key and returning home.

## Which finger types which key

| Finger | Top row | Home row | Bottom row |
|---|---|---|---|
| Left little | Q | A | Z |
| Left ring | W | S | X |
| Left middle | E | D | C |
| Left index | R, T | F, G | V, B |
| Right index | Y, U | H, J | N, M |
| Right middle | I | K | , (comma) |
| Right ring | O | L | . (full stop) |
| Right little | P | ; | / |

The index fingers have the most work: each one covers **two columns**, the home column and the one towards the middle of the keyboard.

> **Tip:** The columns slant slightly to the left as they go down. For example, the left index finger reaches down and slightly left from F to V. Let your fingers follow the slant naturally instead of keeping your hand stiff.

## Capital letters: the Shift keys

Use the **opposite hand's little finger** for Shift:

- For a capital letter typed with the **left** hand (such as the E in *Eldoret*), hold the **right** Shift.
- For a capital letter typed with the **right** hand (such as the K in *Kisumu*), hold the **left** Shift.

So for **N**airobi (N is a right-hand key), hold **left** Shift with your left little finger and press N with your right index finger. For **E**ldoret (E is a left-hand key), hold **right** Shift and press E with your left middle finger.

> **Warning:** Avoid Caps Lock for capitals at the start of words. It's slower and leads to mistakes like "nAIROBI" when you forget it is on.

## Learn in small steps

Don't try to learn all the new keys at once. Add two or three keys per session.

**Top row drills** (type each line three times):

```text
ded ded kik kik ded kik
frf frf juj juj ftf ftf jyj jyj
sws sws lol lol aqa aqa ;p; ;p;
the the that that their their
we are here; it is true; our quiet tour
```

**Bottom row drills:**

```text
fvf fvf jmj jmj fbf fbf jnj jnj
dcd dcd k,k k,k sxs sxs l.l l.l aza aza
cab cab van van man man zinc zinc
come back; a calm man; a big van.
```

**Mixed sentences using every row:**

```text
Amina sells fresh mangoes at Mombasa market.
Kiprono drives a blue van to Nakuru every Monday.
Wanjiru types quickly and checks her work twice.
```

## Common problems and fixes

| Problem | Fix |
|---|---|
| Hand lifts off the home row | Keep the fingers you are not using resting lightly on their home keys |
| Using the wrong finger for B or Y | B belongs to the **left** index finger; Y belongs to the **right** index finger |
| Looking down for the bottom row | Slow right down; say the finger and key in your head ("left middle, C") |
| Forgetting full stops and commas | Practise sentences with punctuation from the start |

## Checking yourself

Type the alphabet sentence below. It uses every letter from A to Z, so it is a quick way to check that you know every key:

```text
The quick brown fox jumps over the lazy dog.
```

Write your own version with Kenyan words if you like. Here is one idea (it uses most, but not all, letters, so try to find which are missing):

```text
Jumping zebras quickly fled the dusty plains of Maasai Mara.
```

```exercise

```

## Practice task

Do 20 minutes today: 5 minutes of home row warm-up, 10 minutes of the top and bottom row drills (two or three new keys at a time), and 5 minutes typing the three mixed sentences. Use the home row tool from the last lesson to warm up, then type the sentences in any text editor without looking at your hands.
