Every beginner wants to be fast. But the quickest way to become a fast typist is, surprisingly, to slow down and type **accurately** first. This lesson explains why and gives you a method to follow.

## Why accuracy comes first

- **Mistakes cost time.** Each error means stopping, pressing Backspace, finding your place and retyping. A typist who rarely makes mistakes is often faster overall than one who rushes.
- **Your fingers learn what you repeat.** If you keep hitting R when you mean T, your fingers practise the wrong movement. Slow, correct repetitions build correct habits.
- **Employers care about accuracy.** In data entry, transcription and office work, one wrong figure in a payment, a name or an ID number can cause real harm. Many tests and jobs set a minimum accuracy, often in the high 90s.

Compare two typists in a 5-minute test:

| Typist | Raw speed | Accuracy | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| Brian | 55 WPM | 85% | Lots of corrections; work must be rechecked; fails a test asking for 95% |
| Fatuma | 38 WPM | 98% | Clean work that can be trusted; speed will rise with practice |

Fatuma is in the stronger position, and she will keep getting faster.

## The 95% rule

Use this simple rule in every practice session:

- If your accuracy is **below 95%**, slow down until it rises.
- Once you can stay **at or above 95%** for several sessions, push your speed a little.
- If accuracy drops again, ease off again.

> **Tip:** Think of speed as something that *grows out of* accuracy. You don't chase it directly; it arrives as your fingers become sure of where every key is.

## Rhythm beats rushing

Many errors come from uneven typing: fast bursts on easy words, then long pauses on hard ones. Try to type at a **steady rhythm**, like a slow, even drumbeat. Easy words should not be rushed and hard words should not cause panic. Some typists practise with a slow metronome app for a few minutes to feel this rhythm.

## Find your problem keys

Most people make the same few mistakes again and again. Common ones are:

| Mistake | Typical cause |
|---|---|
| Swapped letters ("teh" for "the") | Rushing; one hand gets ahead of the other |
| Wrong row (R instead of 4, or V instead of B) | Fingers not returning to home position |
| Missing capitals | Releasing Shift too early |
| Double letters ("committee", "Mississippi") | Losing count; slow down on double letters |
| Space bar missed or doubled | Thumb resting too heavily or too far away |

Keep a short list of your own problem keys and words. Spend two or three minutes of every session on drills that use them, for example:

```text
the the the then them they there
rtr rtr ftf ftf rt rt tr tr art tart start
vbv vbv fvf fbf brave vibe above
```

## Correct or keep going?

In a practice tool, should you fix mistakes with Backspace or carry on?

- **While learning:** fix them. It trains you to notice errors, which matters in real work.
- **But notice them:** a good practice tool still counts a fixed mistake, so your accuracy figure stays honest. The tools in this course do this.
- **At work:** always proofread before you submit.

## Calculating accuracy

Accuracy is the percentage of keystrokes that were correct:

```text
Accuracy = (correct keystrokes / total keystrokes) x 100

Example: 400 keystrokes, 12 of them wrong
Correct = 400 - 12 = 388
Accuracy = 388 / 400 x 100 = 97%
```
