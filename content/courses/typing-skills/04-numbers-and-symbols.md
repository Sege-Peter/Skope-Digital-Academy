Office and online work is full of numbers: phone numbers, prices in KES, dates, ID and invoice numbers, M-Pesa transaction codes. Many people who type letters well still look down for every number. In this lesson you will learn the **number row**, the most common **symbols** and the **numeric keypad**.

## The number row

The number row sits above the top row. The same fingers keep the same columns, they just reach further up:

| Finger | Number keys |
|---|---|
| Left little | 1 (and the key to its left) |
| Left ring | 2 |
| Left middle | 3 |
| Left index | 4, 5 |
| Right index | 6, 7 |
| Right middle | 8 |
| Right ring | 9 |
| Right little | 0, - (and = and the keys to the right) |

> **Note:** Some typists use the left index finger for 6 as well. Either is fine; pick one way and stay consistent.

The reach is long, so move your whole hand slightly if you need to, then return to home position straight away.

## Symbols with Shift

Most symbols are on the number row or near the right little finger, typed with **Shift**. On a standard UK or US layout (used on most computers in Kenya):

| Symbol | Name | How to type it (US layout) |
|---|---|---|
| ! | exclamation mark | Shift + 1 |
| @ | at sign (emails) | Shift + 2 |
| # | hash | Shift + 3 |
| % | percent | Shift + 5 |
| & | ampersand ("and") | Shift + 7 |
| ( ) | brackets | Shift + 9 and Shift + 0 |
| : | colon | Shift + ; |
| ? | question mark | Shift + / |
| " | quotation marks | Shift + ' |

> **Warning:** Keyboard layouts differ. On a **UK** layout, Shift + 2 gives " and the @ sign is next to the Enter key (Shift + '). If a symbol appears that you didn't expect, check which layout your computer uses in its language or keyboard settings.

## The numeric keypad

Many desktop keyboards have a **numeric keypad** on the right. For number-heavy jobs like entering sales figures, it is much faster than the number row.

- Rest your **right index finger on 4**, middle finger on **5** and ring finger on **6**. The 5 key usually has a bump.
- Index finger: 7, 4, 1. Middle finger: 8, 5, 2. Ring finger: 9, 6, 3 and the decimal point.
- Thumb: 0. Little finger: Enter and +.
- Make sure **Num Lock** is on.

Laptops often have no keypad. A cheap USB keypad can be worth buying if you take data-entry work.

## Accuracy matters more for numbers

A wrong letter in a word is often obvious (*Nairbi*). A wrong number is not: *KES 15,000* typed as *KES 51,000* looks perfectly normal but could cause a big problem for an employer or client. With numbers:

- Type a little slower and check each group of digits.
- Read numbers in **chunks** of 3 or 4 digits ("0712 – 345 – 678").
- Compare the source and your typed version before moving on.

```exercise
Q: Fill in the finger that types the 8 on the number row.
The right ___ finger types 8.
A: middle
H: It is the same finger that types I and K.
```

```exercise
Q: On a US layout, complete the keys for the @ sign.
Shift + ___
A: 2
H: It is on the number row, typed by the left ring finger.
```

## Practice task

Find a page with lots of numbers, such as a supermarket receipt, a school fees structure or a price list. Type it into a document exactly as it appears, including KES amounts, dates and symbols. Then compare line by line with the original and count your number mistakes. Repeat tomorrow and try to reduce that count.
