Well done for reaching the end of the course. You started with posture and the home row, learned every letter row, numbers and symbols, measured your speed and accuracy, built a daily routine, picked up time-saving shortcuts and saw how typing leads to real work. This short lesson pulls it all together and then tests what you have learned.

## The key ideas in one table

| Topic | Remember |
|---|---|
| Posture | Feet flat, back supported, elbows about 90 degrees, wrists straight, eyes on the screen |
| Home row | A S D F and J K L ; with thumbs on the space bar; find F and J by their bumps |
| Finger columns | Each finger owns its column; index fingers cover two |
| Shift | Use the opposite hand's little finger |
| Numbers | Same columns, longer reach; use the keypad for number-heavy work |
| Accuracy first | Below 95%? Slow down |
| WPM | (characters / 5) / minutes; net WPM subtracts uncorrected errors per minute |
| Practice | 15–20 minutes daily beats long, rare sessions |
| Shortcuts | Learn three a week; Ctrl + S often |
| Work | Data entry and transcription reward accuracy; never pay to get a job |

## Your next 30 days

```text
Week 1: Daily routine, 20 minutes. Focus on accuracy (97%+).
Week 2: Add the numeric keypad or number row drills, 5 minutes a day.
Week 3: Push speed rounds; learn three new shortcuts.
Week 4: Take the one-minute test three times and compare with your
        first result. Try a short transcription or data-entry sample.
```

Typing skill fades if you stop completely, so keep a few minutes of practice in your week even after you reach your goal. Add your typing speed and accuracy to your CV once you are proud of them, for example "Touch typing: 45 WPM at 97% accuracy", and be ready to prove it in a test.

## Practice task

Take the one-minute test from lesson 6 three times and compare the average with the first result in your progress log. Write your current speed and accuracy in a line for your CV, and set your next target.
