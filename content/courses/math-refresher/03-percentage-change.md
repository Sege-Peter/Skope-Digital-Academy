"Fuel prices up 8%." "Sales down 16% this month." "Rent increased by 10%." Percentage change lets you compare changes fairly, whether the amounts are small or large.

## The formula

```text
Percentage change = (new − old) ÷ old × 100
```

A positive answer is an **increase**; a negative answer is a **decrease**. Always divide by the **old** (original) value.

### Worked example 1 – an increase

A 2 kg packet of maize flour went from KES 180 to KES 207.

```text
Change = 207 − 180 = 27
27 ÷ 180 = 0.15
0.15 × 100 = 15% increase
```

### Worked example 2 – a decrease

Kamau's shop made KES 50,000 in March and KES 42,000 in April.

```text
Change = 42,000 − 50,000 = −8,000
−8,000 ÷ 50,000 = −0.16
−0.16 × 100 = −16% → a 16% decrease
```

## Increasing or decreasing by a percentage

Use a **multiplier** – it is faster and avoids mistakes:

| Change | Multiplier | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Increase by 25% | × 1.25 | 1,200 × 1.25 = 1,500 |
| Increase by 8% | × 1.08 | 500 × 1.08 = 540 |
| Decrease by 20% | × 0.80 | 1,500 × 0.80 = 1,200 |
| Decrease by 5% | × 0.95 | 2,000 × 0.95 = 1,900 |

Increase by r% → multiply by (1 + r/100). Decrease by r% → multiply by (1 − r/100).

## Finding the original value (reverse percentage)

A restaurant bill of KES 3,300 already includes a 10% service charge. What was the food cost?

```text
Bill = original × 1.10
Original = 3,300 ÷ 1.10 = KES 3,000
```

> **Warning:** A common mistake is to take 10% *of 3,300* (330) and subtract it, giving 2,970. That is wrong, because the 10% was calculated on the original 3,000, not on 3,300.

## Up then down is not "back to start"

A phone costs KES 10,000. The price rises 20%, then falls 20%:

```text
10,000 × 1.20 = 12,000
12,000 × 0.80 =  9,600
```

The final price is KES 9,600, not 10,000 – the 20% decrease was taken from a bigger number.

## Percent versus percentage points

If a loan's interest rate rises from 10% to 12%:

- it rose by **2 percentage points** (12 − 10), but
- it rose by **20 percent** in relative terms (2 ÷ 10 × 100).

News reports and banks sometimes mix these up – be clear which one you mean.

## Comparing growth fairly

| Business | Last month (KES) | This month (KES) | Increase (KES) | % change |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Akinyi's salon | 40,000 | 50,000 | 10,000 | +25% |
| Juma's hardware | 400,000 | 440,000 | 40,000 | +10% |

Juma's shop grew by more *shillings*, but Akinyi's salon grew faster in *percentage* terms.

## Practice task

Find two prices you remember from last year (bus fare, a 2 kg packet of flour, a data bundle, school fees) and their prices today. Calculate the percentage change for each. Which one rose fastest in percentage terms? Write one sentence explaining what that means for your budget.
