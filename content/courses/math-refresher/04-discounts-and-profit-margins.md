Whether you sell mitumba, phone accessories or your design services, you must know whether you are really making money. This lesson covers the maths every small business owner needs.

## Discounts

A discount reduces the price by a percentage.

```text
A shirt costs KES 1,800 with 25% off.
Discount     = 25% of 1,800 = 0.25 × 1,800 = 450
Sale price   = 1,800 − 450 = KES 1,350
Quick method = 1,800 × 0.75 = KES 1,350
```

### Two discounts in a row

A shop gives 20% off, then an extra 10% off at the till. That is **not** 30% off:

```text
Price KES 5,000
After 20% off: 5,000 × 0.80 = 4,000
After 10% off: 4,000 × 0.90 = 3,600
Total discount = 1,400 of 5,000 = 28%
```

## Profit

```text
Profit = Selling price − Cost price
```

If the answer is negative, it is a **loss**. Remember that "cost" should include *all* costs of getting the item to the customer – transport, packaging, M-Pesa charges – not just the buying price.

## Markup versus margin

Achieng buys a dress for KES 600 and sells it for KES 1,000. Profit = KES 400. There are two ways to express this as a percentage:

| Measure | Formula | Achieng's dress |
|---|---|---|
| **Markup** | profit ÷ **cost** × 100 | 400 ÷ 600 × 100 = **66.7%** |
| **Margin** | profit ÷ **selling price** × 100 | 400 ÷ 1,000 × 100 = **40%** |

Same profit, different percentages – because they divide by different numbers. Margin is always smaller than markup.

> **Warning:** If a supplier says "you'll make 50%", ask: 50% markup or 50% margin? Mixing them up can wipe out your expected profit.

## Setting a price from a target margin

You want a 40% margin on an item that costs KES 600:

```text
Price = cost ÷ (1 − margin)
      = 600 ÷ (1 − 0.40)
      = 600 ÷ 0.60
      = KES 1,000
```

Check: profit 400 ÷ price 1,000 = 40%. ✔

## Break-even point

Fixed costs (rent, a stall licence, internet) must be paid whether you sell or not. The **break-even point** is how many items you must sell to cover them.

```text
Break-even (units) = fixed costs ÷ profit per item
```

Kiprono pays KES 12,000 a month for his stall. He makes KES 400 profit on each pair of shoes.

```text
12,000 ÷ 400 = 30 pairs
```

Pair number 31 onwards is real profit for the month.

## A full worked example

Fatuma sells smoothies at Kisumu's lakeside. Each cup costs her KES 70 (fruit, milk, cup, straw) and she sells at KES 150. Her monthly blender stand rent is KES 6,400.

```text
Profit per cup  = 150 − 70 = KES 80
Margin          = 80 ÷ 150 × 100 = 53.3%
Break-even      = 6,400 ÷ 80 = 80 cups per month
Selling 200 cups: 200 × 80 − 6,400 = 16,000 − 6,400 = KES 9,600 profit
```

## Practice task

Pick a product you could sell (or already sell). List every cost per item, including transport and packaging. Decide a selling price and calculate profit, markup and margin. Then estimate your monthly fixed costs and find your break-even number. Is that number realistic for one month? If not, what would you change – price, costs or product?
