At work you will constantly be handed numbers in tables and charts – sales reports, fare lists, school results. This lesson helps you summarise numbers with averages and read tables and graphs accurately.

## The four quick measures

For the daily sales KES 1,200, 1,500, 900, 1,800, 1,600:

| Measure | How | Answer |
|---|---|---|
| **Mean** | Total ÷ count = 7,000 ÷ 5 | KES 1,400 |
| **Median** | Middle value after sorting: 900, 1,200, **1,500**, 1,600, 1,800 | KES 1,500 |
| **Mode** | Most frequent value | none – all different |
| **Range** | Largest − smallest = 1,800 − 900 | KES 900 |

> **Tip:** If one value is extreme (like one huge sale), the median describes a "typical" day better than the mean.

## Working backwards from an average

Because mean = total ÷ count, you can find the total: **total = mean × count**.

```text
A kiosk averaged KES 4,500 a day over 6 days.
Total = 4,500 × 6 = KES 27,000
```

**What score do I need?** Kevin's four test scores average 60. What must he score in the fifth test to average 65?

```text
Total needed for 5 tests = 65 × 5 = 325
Total so far             = 60 × 4 = 240
Fifth test               = 325 − 240 = 85
```

## Weighted averages

When quantities differ, you can't just average the prices. A shop buys 10 kg of beans at KES 120/kg and 30 kg at KES 100/kg:

```text
Wrong:  (120 + 100) ÷ 2 = 110
Right:  (10 × 120 + 30 × 100) ÷ (10 + 30)
      = (1,200 + 3,000) ÷ 40
      = 4,200 ÷ 40 = KES 105 per kg
```

The cheaper beans count more because there are more of them.

## Reading a table

Monthly income of a cyber café in Bungoma (KES):

| Month | Printing | Typing | Internet | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Jan | 12,000 | 8,000 | 15,000 | 35,000 |
| Feb | 14,000 | 6,500 | 16,500 | 37,000 |
| Mar | 18,000 | 7,000 | 14,000 | 39,000 |
| Apr | 21,000 | 5,000 | 13,000 | 39,000 |

Read it step by step:

1. **Check the title, headings and units** – this is KES per month.
2. **Look along rows and down columns** – printing rises every month; typing mostly falls.
3. **Calculate what matters** – printing grew from 12,000 to 21,000, an increase of 9,000 ÷ 12,000 × 100 = **75%**.
4. **Check totals** – Jan: 12,000 + 8,000 + 15,000 = 35,000 ✔

## Reading a graph

The same printing income as a bar chart:

```text
Printing income (KES thousands)
Jan | ############             12
Feb | ##############           14
Mar | ##################       18
Apr | #####################    21
    +-------------------------
      0    5    10   15   20
```

Questions to ask of any graph:

- What does each **axis** show, and in what **units**?
- Does the axis **start at zero**? If not, differences may look bigger than they are.
- What is the **trend** – up, down, flat or up-and-down?
- Is anything **unusual** – a sudden spike or drop?

> **Warning:** Adverts and social media posts sometimes use graphs with a cut-off axis to make a small change look dramatic. Always read the numbers, not just the shape.

```exercise
Q: Find the mean and median of 8, 3, 10, 5, 9.

```

```

```
