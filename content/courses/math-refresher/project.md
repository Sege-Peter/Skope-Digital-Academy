## The situation

Brian wants to start selling roasted groundnuts at the stage in Kitale and asks his aunt for a small loan. She says: "Show me the numbers on one page." In this project you will do the same for a **made-up business** (it can be groundnuts, mandazi, phone covers, braided hair, anything). You will work out costs, price, profit, break-even and what a loan would cost, and explain it in plain words so a peer can check every calculation.

> **Note:** Use made-up businesses and numbers. Do **not** share real bank, M-Pesa or loan details, ID numbers or addresses. Round figures such as KES 150 per kilo are fine as examples.

## What you will produce

A one-to-two page document with five parts:

1. **The business** – 2–3 sentences: what you sell, to whom, in which town.
2. **Cost per item** – a table with at least 5 cost lines (ingredients or stock, packaging, transport, and so on) and the total cost per item. Show how you got each figure.
3. **Price and profit** – your selling price, profit per item, **markup %** and **margin %**, each with the working shown. Add one discount offer (for example 10% off for 5 or more items) and show the new profit.
4. **Break-even** – list monthly fixed costs (at least 3), then calculate how many items you must sell to break even and say whether that is realistic.
5. **Loan or savings check** – use a made-up example rate. Compare simple and compound interest on KES 5,000 for 3 years, and write 2–3 sentences on what you learned. Add one simple equation, such as how many weeks of savings you need to buy a KES 6,000 item if you save KES 400 a week.

## Steps

1. Choose the business and list every cost, even small ones.
2. Do the calculations by hand or in a calculator or Google Sheet. Write each formula.
3. Check each answer by estimating it roughly first.
4. Add a 40–60 word summary: "My main message to the lender is…"
5. Re-check the arithmetic, then submit.

## What to submit

- Your pack in the text box **or** as a PDF, Word file, or clear photo of neat handwritten work (PNG or JPG) – max 5 MB.
- Optional: a link to a Google Sheet with your formulas.

## Tips

- Margin uses selling price as the base, markup uses cost. Do not mix them up.
- Show working. A wrong answer with clear working can be fixed; a right answer with none cannot be checked.
- Use realistic but clearly made-up rates and say so. Never present them as real bank rates.

```rubric
Cost per item | At least five cost lines with correct totals and working shown | 4
Price, profit, markup and margin | Profit, markup % and margin % correctly calculated with formulas, plus a discount example | 5
Break-even | Fixed costs listed and the break-even number correctly worked out with a realistic comment | 4
Interest and equation | Simple versus compound interest correctly compared and one equation set up and solved | 4
Clarity and summary | Neat layout, units (KES) used, a short plain-English summary and no real personal data | 3
```
