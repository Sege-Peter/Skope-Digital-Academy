Fractions, decimals and percentages are three ways of writing the same idea: **part of a whole**. Half a loaf, 0.5 of a loaf and 50% of a loaf are all the same amount of bread. Being able to switch between them is one of the most useful maths skills at work.

## The big conversion table

| Fraction | Decimal | Percentage |
|---|---|---|
| 1/2 | 0.5 | 50% |
| 1/4 | 0.25 | 25% |
| 3/4 | 0.75 | 75% |
| 1/5 | 0.2 | 20% |
| 1/10 | 0.1 | 10% |
| 1/8 | 0.125 | 12.5% |
| 1/3 | 0.333… | about 33.3% |
| 2/3 | 0.666… | about 66.7% |

Learn the first five by heart – they appear everywhere.

## How to convert

```text
Fraction → decimal:     divide top by bottom      3/8  = 3 ÷ 8   = 0.375
Decimal → percentage:   multiply by 100            0.375 × 100    = 37.5%
Percentage → decimal:   divide by 100              16% ÷ 100      = 0.16
Percentage → fraction:  put over 100, simplify     40% = 40/100   = 2/5
```

## Simplifying fractions

Divide the top (numerator) and bottom (denominator) by the same number until you can't go further:

```text
12/16  → divide both by 4 → 3/4
45/60  → divide both by 15 → 3/4
```

## Adding and subtracting fractions

The bottoms must match first:

```text
1/2 + 1/3 = 3/6 + 2/6 = 5/6
3/4 − 1/8 = 6/8 − 1/8 = 5/8
```

Example: Otieno spends 1/2 of his salary on rent and food and 1/3 on transport and family support. Together that is 5/6, so 1/6 is left for savings.

## Finding a fraction or percentage of an amount

Multiply the amount by the fraction or the decimal.

```text
3/5 of KES 2,000      = 2,000 ÷ 5 × 3 = 400 × 3 = KES 1,200
15% of KES 2,400      = 0.15 × 2,400              = KES 360
```

### The 10% trick

To find 10%, move the decimal point one place left. Build other percentages from it:

```text
10% of 2,400 = 240
 5% of 2,400 = 120   (half of 10%)
15% of 2,400 = 240 + 120 = 360
20% of 2,400 = 240 × 2  = 480
```

> **Tip:** Use the 10% trick to check a shop's "15% off" price or a commission in your head before you pay or sign.

## What percentage is one number of another?

Divide the part by the whole, then multiply by 100.

```text
Brian sold 18 of his 24 phone covers.
18 ÷ 24 = 0.75 → 75% sold

Fatuma scored 42 out of 60 in a test.
42 ÷ 60 = 0.7  → 70%
```

## Multiplying decimals with money

Price per kilo × kilos:

```text
Tomatoes at KES 120 per kg, buying 2.5 kg:
120 × 2.5 = 120 × 2 + 120 × 0.5 = 240 + 60 = KES 300
```

## Practice task

Look at your phone's data or airtime usage, or a family budget. Work out what **fraction** and **percentage** of the total goes to the biggest item (for example, "KES 1,500 of KES 6,000 on transport = 1/4 = 25%"). Then use the 10% trick to work out what saving 10% of that item each month would mean in shillings.
