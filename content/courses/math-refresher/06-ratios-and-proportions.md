Ratios compare quantities: how to share profits between partners, how much sand to mix with cement, or which pack size gives the best value. Proportion is the skill of scaling those comparisons up or down.

## What a ratio means

A ratio **a : b** says "for every *a* of the first, there are *b* of the second". If a class has 12 girls and 8 boys, the ratio of girls to boys is 12 : 8.

### Simplifying ratios

Divide both sides by the same number, just like fractions:

```text
12 : 8   → divide by 4  → 3 : 2
15 : 25  → divide by 5  → 3 : 5
```

## Sharing in a ratio

Wanjiku and Ochieng run a car wash together. Wanjiku put in less money, so they agree to share profit in the ratio 2 : 3. This month's profit is KES 45,000.

```text
Total parts = 2 + 3 = 5
One part    = 45,000 ÷ 5 = 9,000
Wanjiku     = 2 × 9,000 = KES 18,000
Ochieng     = 3 × 9,000 = KES 27,000
Check: 18,000 + 27,000 = 45,000 ✔
```

The same method works for three people. KES 60,000 shared 1 : 2 : 3 → 6 parts of 10,000 → KES 10,000, 20,000 and 30,000.

## Mixing ratios

A common concrete mix is cement : sand : ballast = 1 : 2 : 4. If a fundi uses 3 wheelbarrows of cement:

```text
Sand    = 3 × 2 = 6 wheelbarrows
Ballast = 3 × 4 = 12 wheelbarrows
```

## Direct proportion – the unitary method

Two quantities are in **direct proportion** if doubling one doubles the other (more kilos → more cost). Find the value of **one unit** first.

```text
4 kg of rice costs KES 640. What do 7 kg cost?
1 kg = 640 ÷ 4 = KES 160
7 kg = 160 × 7 = KES 1,120
```

## Inverse proportion

Sometimes more of one means *less* of the other. More workers → fewer days.

```text
6 workers take 10 days to build a fence. How long would 4 workers take?
Total work = 6 × 10 = 60 worker-days
4 workers  = 60 ÷ 4 = 15 days
```

## Finding the best buy

Compare the price for the **same quantity**:

| Pack | Price | Price per kg |
|---|---|---|
| 500 g sugar | KES 90 | 90 ÷ 0.5 = KES 180 |
| 2 kg sugar | KES 340 | 340 ÷ 2 = KES 170 |

The 2 kg pack is cheaper per kilo – as long as you will use it all and have the cash now.

> **Tip:** Many supermarkets show a "unit price" on the shelf label (for example per kg or per litre). Use it to compare quickly.

## Scales on maps and plans

A map scale of 1 : 50,000 means 1 cm on the map = 50,000 cm on the ground = 500 m.

```text
4 cm on the map = 4 × 500 m = 2,000 m = 2 km
```

## Practice task

Find two sizes of the same product at a shop or online (cooking oil, flour, soap or data bundles). Calculate the price per litre, kilo or GB for each and decide which is the best buy. Then imagine you and a friend start a business where you contribute KES 3,000 and they contribute KES 5,000. Write the profit-sharing ratio in its simplest form and calculate each person's share of a KES 12,000 profit.
