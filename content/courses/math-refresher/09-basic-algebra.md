Algebra sounds scary, but it is just a tidy way of solving problems where one number is unknown. "How many weeks until I can afford the phone?" "How many items must I sell?" Those are algebra questions.

## Letters stand for unknown numbers

A **variable** is a letter that stands for a number we don't know yet.

```text
Chapati costs KES 20 each and tea costs KES 50.
Cost of x chapatis plus one tea = 20x + 50
(20x means 20 × x)
If x = 3:  20 × 3 + 50 = 60 + 50 = KES 110
```

`20x + 50` is an **expression**. When we set it equal to something, it becomes an **equation**.

## Solving equations: keep the balance

An equation is like a weighing scale – whatever you do to one side, do to the other. Undo operations in reverse order.

```text
20x + 50 = 250
20x      = 200      (subtract 50 from both sides)
x        = 10       (divide both sides by 20)
Check: 20 × 10 + 50 = 250 ✔
```

More examples:

```text
5x − 7 = 33          3(x + 4) = 27          2x + 15 = x + 40
5x = 40              x + 4 = 9              2x − x = 40 − 15
x = 8                x = 5                  x = 25
```

> **Tip:** Always check your answer by putting it back into the original equation.

## From words to equations

Follow four steps:

1. **Name the unknown** – "let w = number of weeks".
2. **Write the equation** from the words.
3. **Solve** it.
4. **Answer in words, with units**, and check it makes sense.

### Example 1: saving for a phone

Achieng wants a phone costing KES 18,000. She has KES 4,500 and saves KES 1,500 a week.

```text
Let w = number of weeks
4,500 + 1,500w = 18,000
1,500w = 13,500
w = 9
Achieng needs 9 weeks.
```

### Example 2: comparing two offers

Two printers quote for printing a booklet:

- **Printer A:** KES 300 set-up fee + KES 20 per copy
- **Printer B:** KES 100 set-up fee + KES 30 per copy

When do they cost the same?

```text
Let c = number of copies
300 + 20c = 100 + 30c
200 = 10c
c = 20
```

At 20 copies both cost 300 + 20 × 20 = KES 700. For **more than 20 copies, Printer A is cheaper**; for fewer, Printer B is cheaper.

### Example 3: break-even with algebra

Fatuma's smoothie stand makes KES 80 profit per cup and pays KES 6,400 rent a month. Monthly profit P for n cups is:

```text
P = 80n − 6,400
Break-even means P = 0:  80n = 6,400  →  n = 80 cups
```

## Using formulas

A formula is an equation that is used again and again. You can rearrange it to find any part:

```text
Simple interest:  I = P × r × t
To find time:     t = I ÷ (P × r)
I = 3,000, P = 10,000, r = 0.10  →  t = 3,000 ÷ 1,000 = 3 years
```

## Practice task

Write your own "saving for something" equation: the item you want, its price, what you have now and what you can realistically save per week. Solve it to find the number of weeks. Then write a second equation for a faster plan (for example saving KES 200 more each week) and compare the two answers.
