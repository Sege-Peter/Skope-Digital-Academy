Good number sense means you can read big numbers confidently, round them sensibly and calculate in the right order. These basics prevent the costly mistakes that happen at a till, in a budget or on an invoice.

## Place value and big numbers

Every digit's position tells you its value. Take **KES 3,482,750**:

| Millions | Hundred thousands | Ten thousands | Thousands | Hundreds | Tens | Ones |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3 | 4 | 8 | 2 | 7 | 5 | 0 |

You read it as "three million, four hundred and eighty-two thousand, seven hundred and fifty shillings".

Useful shortcuts you will see in news and business:

| Written | Means |
|---|---|
| KES 5K or 5,000 | five thousand |
| KES 250,000 | a quarter of a million |
| KES 1.2M | 1,200,000 (one point two million) |
| KES 3.5 billion | 3,500,000,000 |

> **Tip:** Commas every three digits make big numbers easy to read. Write KES 1,250,000, not KES 1250000.

## Rounding

Rounding gives a simpler number that is "close enough". Look at the digit just *after* the place you are rounding to: **5 or more → round up; 4 or less → keep it**.

```text
Round 47,382 to the nearest thousand:
  thousands digit = 7, next digit = 3  → keep → 47,000

Round 47,382 to the nearest hundred:
  hundreds digit = 3, next digit = 8   → round up → 47,400

Round KES 1,849.65 to the nearest shilling:
  next digit after the ones = 6        → round up → KES 1,850
```

## Order of operations – BODMAS

When a calculation has several steps, do them in this order:

| Letter | Means | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **B** | Brackets | ( ) first |
| **O** | Orders (powers, roots) | 3² = 9 |
| **D M** | Division and Multiplication, left to right | |
| **A S** | Addition and Subtraction, left to right | |

```text
5 + 3 × 4      = 5 + 12      = 17     (not 32!)
(5 + 3) × 4    = 8 × 4       = 32
20 − 8 ÷ 2 + 1 = 20 − 4 + 1  = 17
```

### A worked example

Wanjiru buys 3 loaves of bread at KES 65 each and 2 packets of milk at KES 60 each. She pays with KES 500. Her change:

```text
Change = 500 − (3 × 65 + 2 × 60)
       = 500 − (195 + 120)
       = 500 − 315
       = KES 185
```

Notice the brackets: the *whole* shopping total is subtracted from 500.

## Negative numbers

Negative numbers show amounts below zero – a loss, a debt or a temperature below freezing.

- A shop with income of KES 40,000 and costs of KES 46,000 made a result of 40,000 − 46,000 = **−6,000** (a loss of KES 6,000).
- If you owe KES 500 on Fuliza and then deposit KES 800, your balance becomes −500 + 800 = **300**.

```exercise
Q: Round 47,382 to the nearest ten thousand.
___
A: 50000 / 50,000
H: Look at the thousands digit.
```

```exercise
Q: Calculate using BODMAS.
(20 − 8) ÷ 4 + 2 × 3 = ___
A: 9
```

```exercise
Q: Write KES 1.2M in full and a loss of KES 6,000 as a signed number.
1.2M = ___ ; loss = ___
A: 1200000 / 1,200,000 | -6000 / -6,000 / −6000 / −6,000
```

## Practice task

Take a real receipt (supermarket, chemist or hardware). Without a calculator, round each item to the nearest 10 shillings and add them up. Then compare your estimate with the receipt total. How far off were you? Finally, write the total in words as you would on a cheque.
