Interest is the price of money. When you borrow, you pay interest; when you save or invest, you earn it. Understanding how it grows can save you from expensive loans and help your savings work harder.

## Key words

| Word | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Principal (P)** | The amount borrowed or saved at the start |
| **Rate (r)** | The interest percentage per period (per year or per month) |
| **Time (t or n)** | The number of periods |
| **Interest (I)** | The extra money paid or earned |
| **Amount (A)** | Principal + interest |

> **Note:** Always check whether a rate is **per month** or **per year** (p.a. = per annum). 5% per month is far more expensive than 5% per year.

## Simple interest

Interest is calculated on the **original principal only**.

```text
I = P × r × t        (r as a decimal)
A = P + I
```

**Worked example:** Brian borrows KES 20,000 from a chama at 10% per year simple interest for 3 years.

```text
I = 20,000 × 0.10 × 3 = KES 6,000
A = 20,000 + 6,000    = KES 26,000
```

## Compound interest

Interest is added to the balance, and next period's interest is calculated on the **new, bigger balance** – "interest on interest".

```text
A = P × (1 + r)ⁿ
I = A − P
```

**Worked example:** The same KES 20,000 at 10% per year, compounded yearly, for 3 years:

| Year | Start balance | Interest (10%) | End balance |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 20,000 | 2,000 | 22,000 |
| 2 | 22,000 | 2,200 | 24,200 |
| 3 | 24,200 | 2,420 | 26,620 |

Using the formula: 20,000 × 1.10³ = 20,000 × 1.331 = **KES 26,620**. Compound interest gave KES 620 more than simple interest.

## Compounding working for you: savings

Grace saves KES 10,000 in an account earning 8% per year, compounded yearly, and leaves it for 5 years.

```text
A = 10,000 × 1.08⁵ = 10,000 × 1.4693… ≈ KES 14,693
```

She earned about KES 4,693 without adding a shilling. Start early – time is the most powerful part of the formula.

### The Rule of 72

A quick way to estimate how long money takes to **double**:

```text
Years to double ≈ 72 ÷ annual rate (in %)
At 8%:  72 ÷ 8  = about 9 years
At 12%: 72 ÷ 12 = about 6 years
```

## Compounding working against you: short-term loans

Some loan apps charge interest per month and add fees when you roll a loan over. Imagine (as an example only) a loan that grows by 10% every month it is not repaid:

```text
KES 5,000 × 1.10¹² ≈ KES 15,692 after one year
```

The debt more than *triples*. Before borrowing, ask for the **total amount to repay** and the deadline in writing, compare with a SACCO or bank, and borrow only what you can repay on time.

> **Warning:** Rates and fees differ between lenders and change over time. Always check the current total cost with the lender, and be wary of anyone who asks for an upfront "processing fee" before giving you a loan – that is a common scam.

## Monthly rates and annual rates

A rate of 2% per month is *not* the same as 24% per year once compounding is included: 1.02¹² ≈ 1.268, so it is about **26.8% per year**. Compare loans on the same basis.

```exercise
Q: Find the simple interest on KES 15,000 at 12% per year for 2 years.
KES ___
A: 3600 / 3,600
```

```exercise
Q: KES 10,000 is saved at 10% per year compound interest for 2 years. What is the final amount?
KES ___
A: 12100 / 12,100
H: 10,000 × 1.1 × 1.1
```

```exercise
Q: Using the Rule of 72, about how many years does money take to double at 6% per year?

```

```exercise
Q: A KES 8,000 loan charges 5% simple interest per month for 3 months. What is the total to repay?

```
