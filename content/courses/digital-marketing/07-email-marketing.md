Social media platforms decide who sees your posts. An **email list** is different: the business owns the list of people who asked to hear from it. Email works especially well for schools, colleges, NGOs, online shops, hotels, training centres and professional services.

## Permission first

Only email people who **agreed** to receive emails from the business. Ways to collect emails with permission:

- A sign-up form on the website or a link in the social media bio.
- A paper or tablet sign-up sheet at the shop counter or an event, with a clear tick box.
- Offering something useful in exchange, such as a free price guide, a recipe booklet or a revision timetable.

> **Warning:** Never buy email lists or copy emails from WhatsApp groups. It damages the business's reputation, gets emails marked as spam, and may break the law. Kenya's Data Protection Act controls how personal data is collected and used for direct marketing – see odpc.go.ke for guidance.

## Email marketing tools

Instead of sending from a normal Gmail account, businesses use email marketing tools such as Mailchimp, Brevo or MailerLite. Many have a free plan for small lists (check each tool's current limits). These tools:

- Store your list and handle sign-up forms.
- Provide templates that look good on phones.
- Add an **unsubscribe** link automatically.
- Show results: who opened, who clicked.

## Types of emails

| Email | When to send | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Welcome | Right after someone signs up | "Karibu! Here is your free size guide." |
| Newsletter | Regularly (e.g. monthly) | Tips, news, new products |
| Promotion | For an offer or launch | "Back-to-school bundle this week" |
| Reminder | Before a date | "Registration closes Friday" |

## Parts of a good email

```text
From name:     Kiprono at Rift Valley Tutors
Subject line:  Your KCSE maths revision plan for March
Preview text:  4 weekly topics + free past paper inside
Body:          Short greeting, one main message, 2-3 short paragraphs
Button (CTA):  Download the free plan
Footer:        Business address, contact, unsubscribe link
```

The **subject line** decides whether the email is opened. Keep it short, clear and specific. Avoid ALL CAPITALS and lots of exclamation marks – they look like spam.

```exercise
Q: Fill in the two parts of an email described.
The line people see before opening is the ___ line. The link that lets people stop receiving emails is the ___ link.
A: subject | unsubscribe
```

## Measuring email results

Email tools report numbers such as:

- **Open rate** – the percentage of delivered emails that were opened. (Some phones and apps affect how accurately opens are counted, so treat it as a rough guide.)
- **Click rate** – the percentage who clicked a link. This is usually a more reliable sign of interest.
- **Unsubscribes** – people who left the list. A few is normal; many means the content or frequency is wrong.

If 400 people received an email and 20 clicked the button, the click rate is 20 ÷ 400 × 100 = 5%.

```exercise
Q: An email was delivered to 500 people and 25 clicked the link. What is the click rate in percent?
Click rate = 25 ÷ 500 × 100 = ___%
A: 5
```

## Tips for beginners

1. Send on a regular schedule so readers know what to expect.
2. One main message and one main button per email.
3. Test on your phone before sending to the list.
4. Personalise: most tools can insert the reader's first name.

## Practice task

For your practice business, write a **welcome email**: a from name, subject line, preview text, a body of under 120 words, one button text, and a footer. Ask two friends whether they would open it based on the subject line alone.
