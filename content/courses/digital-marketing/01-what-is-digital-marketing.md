Every business needs customers. **Marketing** is everything a business does to get the right people to notice it, trust it and buy from it. **Digital marketing** is doing that work online: on social media, on WhatsApp, through email, on Google and on websites.

In this course you will learn the skills that small businesses in Kenya are looking for, and how to turn those skills into an income.

## Marketing is more than "posting"

Many people think marketing means posting a photo on Facebook and hoping for the best. Real marketing starts earlier, with a few questions:

1. **Who** are we trying to reach?
2. **What** problem do we solve for them?
3. **Where** do those people spend time online?
4. **What** do we want them to do next (call, visit, order, book)?
5. **How** will we know if it worked?

When you can answer these five questions for a business, your posts stop being random and start bringing results.

## Traditional vs digital marketing

| | Traditional marketing | Digital marketing |
|---|---|---|
| Examples | Radio adverts, posters, flyers, newspaper ads | Facebook posts, TikTok videos, WhatsApp status, Google search ads, email |
| Cost to start | Often high (printing, airtime) | Can start free with organic posts |
| Targeting | Broad: everyone who hears or sees it | Can target by location, age and interests |
| Measuring | Hard to know who responded | Platforms show views, clicks and messages |
| Speed | Takes time to print or book | A post can go live in minutes |

Traditional marketing still works. A clear signboard outside a salon in Kakamega is marketing. But digital tools let even a tiny business reach people beyond its street.

## The main digital channels

- **Social media** – Facebook, Instagram, TikTok, X and LinkedIn. Good for showing products and building a following.
- **Messaging** – WhatsApp Business. In Kenya, many sales are closed in a WhatsApp chat.
- **Search** – Google. People search when they are ready to buy ("plumber near me Thika").
- **Email** – useful for schools, NGOs, online shops and professional services.
- **Websites** – a home base the business owns, where all the other channels can point.

## Organic vs paid

**Organic** marketing is free to post: your normal posts, reels and status updates. **Paid** marketing means paying a platform to show your content to more people, such as Facebook ads or Google ads. Most small businesses should first learn to post well organically, then add small paid campaigns once they know what their customers respond to.

> **Tip:** You do not need a big budget to practise. Offer to manage the page of a relative's shop or a church youth group. Real practice is worth more than theory.

## A simple example

Fatuma sells second-hand shoes (mitumba) in Nakuru. Before, she posted random photos on her personal Facebook. After planning, she knows her buyers are mostly young working women in Nakuru town, so she posts clear photos with sizes and prices every morning, replies quickly on WhatsApp, and tells buyers where to collect. Same phone, same shoes – but now there is a plan.
