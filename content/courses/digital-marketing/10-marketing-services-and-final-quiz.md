You now have the core skills of a beginner digital marketer. This final lesson shows how to turn them into paid work with local businesses, then reviews the course with a quiz.

## Who needs your help?

Many small businesses know they should be online but have no time or skills. Good first clients include salons and kinyozis, restaurants and cafés, boutiques, hardware shops, private schools and tuition centres, churches, clinics and chemists, event companies and car wash businesses.

## Packaging your services

Clients understand **packages** better than hourly rates. Example packages:

| Package | What it could include |
|---|---|
| Starter setup (one-off) | Set up or fix Facebook Page, Instagram, WhatsApp Business and Google Business Profile |
| Monthly social media | Content calendar, a set number of posts per week, replying to comments, monthly report |
| Content only | Photos, short videos and captions the owner posts themselves |
| Ad management | Planning and running paid ads; the ad budget is paid separately by the client |

## Pricing (examples only)

Prices vary a lot by town, client size and your experience. As a beginner you might charge a modest one-off fee for a setup and a monthly fee for management – for example, some beginners start somewhere in the low thousands of shillings for a setup and raise prices as they gain results and reviews. Research what others charge in your area, start fair, and increase as your portfolio grows. Always agree the price, the number of posts and what is **not** included **in writing** before you start.

> **Tip:** Ask for part of the payment upfront (for example half) and the rest on delivery, paid to an M-Pesa number in your own name so records are clear.

## Getting your first clients

1. **Build proof.** Manage your practice business's pages for free for a month and record the results.
2. **Show a before/after.** Screenshot the profile before and after your work.
3. **Approach in person.** Visit businesses near you with a one-page offer. Point out one quick improvement you could make for them.
4. **Ask for referrals and testimonials** from every happy client.
5. **Market yourself.** Your own pages should show your skills.

> **Warning:** As a freelancer, be careful of "clients" who ask you to pay to join their agency, buy their course before getting work, or log in to accounts using codes they send you. Never share a client's passwords with anyone, and return access when the contract ends.

## Course wrap-up

Over four weeks you learned to: understand a target audience and write a value proposition; set up and use Facebook, Instagram, TikTok and WhatsApp Business; plan content with pillars and a calendar; write copy with AIDA and clear calls to action; run permission-based email marketing; improve local visibility with SEO and Google Business Profile; and measure results that matter. Keep practising on real businesses – skills grow with use.

## Practice task

Write a **one-page service offer** for a real business near you: the three problems you noticed on their pages, the package you recommend, what is included, the timeline and how they can contact you. Do not send it until you have checked every detail twice.
