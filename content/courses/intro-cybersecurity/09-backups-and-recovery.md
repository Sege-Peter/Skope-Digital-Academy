No protection is perfect. Phones get stolen, laptops fall, flash disks die and accounts get hacked. People who recover quickly are the ones who **prepared backups** and **know what to do** in the first hour.

## Backups

A **backup** is a separate copy of your important files that you can restore if the original is lost, damaged or locked by ransomware.

### The 3-2-1 rule

| Number | Meaning | Example for a small business |
|---|---|---|
| **3** copies | The original plus two backups | Files on the office PC + two copies |
| **2** different types of storage | Not all on the same kind of device | External hard drive + cloud storage |
| **1** copy off-site | Stored away from the office | Cloud (Google Drive, OneDrive) or a drive kept at home |

### Backup options

- **Phone:** turn on Google backup (Android) or iCloud backup (iPhone) for contacts, photos and settings. Turn on **WhatsApp chat backup** (**Settings** › **Chats** › **Chat backup**).
- **Computer:** use OneDrive, Google Drive or Windows' built-in backup tools, plus an external hard drive.
- **Business records:** keep M-Pesa statements, receipts and customer lists in the cloud as well as on the office computer.

> **Warning:** A backup drive that stays plugged in all the time can be encrypted by ransomware along with everything else. Plug it in, back up, then **unplug it**.

> **Tip:** A backup is only useful if it works. Every few months, try restoring one file to make sure you can.

## Signs an account has been hacked

- You cannot log in – the password or recovery email has been changed.
- Friends receive messages or posts you did not send.
- You get "new sign-in" or "password changed" alerts you did not trigger.
- Unknown transactions appear in M-Pesa or bank statements.

## What to do after being hacked

Act quickly but calmly. Work from a **different, clean device** if you suspect yours is infected.

1. **Secure your email first.** Use the provider's official account recovery page (for example Google's account recovery) to get back in, then change the password.
2. **Change passwords** on the hacked account and any account that used the same password.
3. **Turn on 2FA** and check that the recovery phone number and email are yours.
4. **Sign out other sessions.** Most services list "Where you're logged in" or "Your devices" – sign out anything you do not recognise.
5. **Check for changes** – forwarding rules in email, new linked devices in WhatsApp (**Settings** › **Linked devices**), unknown apps with access.
6. **Warn your contacts** not to send money or click links from your account.
7. **Call your bank or provider** immediately if money or financial apps are involved, to block and reverse what they can.
8. **Scan your devices** for malware.

## Keeping evidence

Before deleting anything, collect evidence. Investigators need it.

- Screenshots of scam messages, showing the sender's number or profile.
- Transaction messages and M-Pesa codes.
- Dates and times of what happened.
- Links and usernames of fake accounts.

## Reporting incidents

| Where | What to report |
|---|---|
| Your bank, Sacco or mobile money provider | Unauthorised transactions – report as fast as possible |
| The nearest police station / DCI | Fraud, extortion, identity theft – ask for an OB (Occurrence Book) number |
| National KE-CIRT/CC (ke-cirt.go.ke), under the Communications Authority of Kenya | Cyber incidents affecting individuals and organisations |
| The platform (Facebook, WhatsApp, TikTok…) | Fake and hacked accounts, harassment |
| Office of the Data Protection Commissioner (odpc.go.ke) | Misuse of your personal data by an organisation |

> **Note:** Contact details for these organisations can change. Always get them from their official websites, never from a link in a suspicious message.

## Incident reporting at work

If you work in an office or cyber café and something looks wrong – a strange pop-up, a clicked phishing link, a missing laptop – **report it to your supervisor or IT person immediately**. Many people hide mistakes out of embarrassment, which gives attackers more time. A good report says **what** happened, **when**, **which device or account**, and **what you have done so far**.

## Practice task

Set up an automatic backup today: turn on phone backup and WhatsApp chat backup. Then write a one-page "If I get hacked" plan listing your important accounts, where your backup codes are, and the numbers of your bank and network provider's customer care (taken from their official websites).
