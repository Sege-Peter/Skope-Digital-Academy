Every day you visit websites, download files and connect to networks. A few simple habits make each of these much safer – whether you are on your own phone, a friend's laptop or a cyber café computer in town.

## Reading web addresses

Before you type a password or pay on a site, check the address bar.

| Part | Example | What to check |
|---|---|---|
| Protocol | `https://` | Should be **https**, not http |
| Domain | `ecitizen.go.ke` | Must be the organisation's real domain |
| Path | `/login` | Less important |

The padlock or `https://` means the connection is **encrypted** – others on the network cannot read what you send. It does **not** prove the website is honest. Scam sites can have a padlock too, so the **domain** is what matters.

> **Tip:** Kenyan government websites end in **.go.ke**. Bookmark the real websites you use often (your bank, eCitizen, KRA, your college portal) and open them from your bookmarks instead of from links in messages.

## Safe browsing habits

- **Type or bookmark** important addresses; do not trust search adverts blindly – criminals sometimes pay for ads that imitate real sites.
- **Keep your browser updated** (Chrome, Edge, Firefox and Safari update themselves if allowed).
- **Ignore scary pop-ups** saying "Your phone has 5 viruses! Click to clean." Close the tab.
- **Download only from official sources** – the software maker's own website or an official app store.
- **Be careful with browser extensions.** Install only well-known ones you really need.
- **Check before you pay** online: look for reviews, a physical address and real contact details. Prefer pay-on-delivery for unknown sellers.

## Using shared and cyber café computers

Shared computers are risky because the next user can see what you leave behind, and some may have malware.

1. Use a **private/incognito window** (**Ctrl + Shift + N** in Chrome and Edge, **Ctrl + Shift + P** in Firefox).
2. **Never** tick "Remember me" or "Save password".
3. **Sign out** of every account when you finish, then close the browser.
4. Do not leave your documents in Downloads or on the Desktop – delete them.
5. Avoid online banking on shared computers if you can; use your own phone and mobile data instead.

> **Note:** Incognito mode only stops the browser from saving your history and cookies after you close it. It does not hide you from the network owner or protect you from malware on that computer.

## Public Wi-Fi

Free Wi-Fi in hotels, restaurants, malls and matatus is convenient but comes with risks:

| Risk | What it means |
|---|---|
| Fake hotspot ("evil twin") | A criminal creates a network named like the real one, e.g. *Cafe_Free_WiFi* |
| Snooping | On unencrypted sites, others on the network may see your traffic |
| Unsafe sharing | File sharing turned on lets others on the network see your shared folders |

Stay safer on public Wi-Fi:

- **Ask staff** for the exact network name before connecting.
- Only use **https** websites, and avoid banking and sensitive logins – use mobile data for those.
- On Windows, choose **Public network** when asked, which turns off file and printer sharing.
- Turn off **auto-connect** for open networks so your phone does not join them silently.
- A reputable **VPN** encrypts all your traffic on untrusted networks. Avoid unknown "free VPN" apps, which may collect and sell your data.

> **Warning:** If a Wi-Fi login page asks for your email password, M-Pesa PIN or ID number, disconnect. A genuine hotspot may ask for a phone number or voucher code, but never your PIN.

## Practice task

On your phone, open the Wi-Fi settings and **forget** any public networks you no longer use. Then bookmark the real websites of three services you use (for example your bank, eCitizen and your email) so you never need to search for them again.
