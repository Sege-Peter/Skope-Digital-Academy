Banks, telcos, Saccos, government agencies, hospitals and online businesses across East Africa all need people who can protect their systems and data. Cybersecurity is a growing field, and many roles start with exactly the awareness skills you have built in this course.

## Roles in cybersecurity

| Role | What they do |
|---|---|
| IT support / help desk technician | Fixes users' computers and accounts – a common entry point into security |
| Security awareness trainer | Teaches staff to spot phishing and follow safe habits |
| SOC (Security Operations Centre) analyst | Watches alerts, investigates suspicious activity and escalates incidents |
| Fraud analyst | Detects suspicious transactions at banks, Saccos and mobile money providers |
| Network / systems administrator | Configures and secures servers, networks and user accounts |
| Governance, risk and compliance officer | Writes policies and checks that organisations follow laws like the Data Protection Act |
| Penetration tester (ethical hacker) | Tests systems **with written permission** to find weaknesses before criminals do |
| Digital forensics investigator | Collects and analyses evidence after incidents |

> **Warning:** Ethical hacking is only legal with clear, written permission from the system owner. Practising on systems you do not own breaks the law (see the Computer Misuse and Cybercrimes Act, 2018) – use legal practice labs instead.

## A realistic path for a school leaver

1. **Build foundations.** Learn how computers, operating systems and networks work. Our *Networking Basics* course is a good next step.
2. **Get an entry IT job or attachment** – help desk, cyber café technician, school IT assistant. Real experience matters.
3. **Study for a recognised beginner certification.** Examples include **CompTIA Security+**, **ISC2 Certified in Cybersecurity (CC)** and the **Google Cybersecurity Certificate**. Costs and offers change, so check the official websites; some organisations run free or sponsored training programmes.
4. **Use free learning platforms** such as Cisco Networking Academy (netacad.com), which offers free introductory cybersecurity courses.
5. **Practise legally** in safe labs and beginner "capture the flag" (CTF) challenges designed for learning.
6. **Join communities** – local tech meet-ups, university security clubs and online groups – and share what you learn.
7. Consider a **diploma or degree** in IT, computer science or information security as you grow.

> **Note:** Be wary of anyone selling "guaranteed hacking jobs" or "hacking courses" on social media. Learn from recognised institutions and official certification bodies.

## Start earning with what you know now

Even as a beginner, you can offer **security check-ups** to family, friends and small businesses: turning on 2FA, setting phone locks, setting up backups and teaching staff to spot M-Pesa scams. Always explain what you are changing and never ask to keep anyone's passwords.

## Practice task

Do a free 30-minute "security check-up" for one family member or a small business near you. Use a checklist: screen lock, SIM PIN, 2FA on email and WhatsApp, backups, app updates and a conversation about the "never share your PIN or OTP" rule.
