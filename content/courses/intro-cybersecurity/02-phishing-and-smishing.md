**Phishing** is when a criminal sends a fake message that pretends to come from a trusted organisation, hoping you will click a link, open an attachment or hand over information. The name comes from "fishing": they throw out bait and wait for someone to bite.

| Type | Channel | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Phishing | Email | "Your Gmail storage is full. Log in here to avoid losing your emails." |
| Smishing | SMS | "Your parcel is held. Pay KES 150 delivery fee at this link." |
| Vishing | Voice call | "This is your bank's security team. Read me the code we just sent." |
| WhatsApp / social media scams | Chat apps | A "friend" says they changed numbers and urgently need money |

## Anatomy of a fake message

Here is an invented example of a smishing message:

```text
From: +254 7XX XXX XXX
KRA NOTICE: You have an unclaimed tax refund of KES 23,450.
Claim before midnight or it will be cancelled:
http://kra-refund-portal.example.co
```

Red flags:

1. **Sender** – a normal mobile number, not an official sender name.
2. **Urgency** – "before midnight".
3. **Too good to be true** – money you did not expect.
4. **Strange link** – the real KRA website is **kra.go.ke**. Any other domain, even one containing "kra", is not KRA.

> **Warning:** Criminals register addresses that look almost right: `safaricom-care.com`, `equity-bank-ke.net`, `kcb.co.ke.login-help.info`. Read the part just before the first single `/` – in that last example the real domain is `login-help.info`, not KCB.

## Common phishing red flags

- Generic greetings like *Dear Customer* or *Dear user*.
- Spelling and grammar mistakes (though some scams are now well written, so do not rely on this alone).
- Requests for your **PIN, password, OTP (one-time code) or ID photos**.
- Attachments you were not expecting, especially `.zip`, `.exe`, `.apk` or documents asking you to "enable content".
- Links that are shortened or hidden behind buttons. On a computer, hover over a link to see where it really goes; on a phone, long-press it.
- Job offers that need a "registration fee", "medical fee" or "uniform fee" first.

## Fake job and loan offers

School leavers are a common target. Typical scams include:

- Overseas jobs that ask you to pay for a visa or medical test through an agent's personal M-Pesa number. Before applying, check the recruitment agency is licensed with the National Employment Authority (nea.go.ke).
- "Data entry" or "online typing" work that needs an activation fee.
- Instant loan apps or messages that ask for an upfront "processing fee".

> **Remember:** A real employer pays you. If you must pay to get a job, treat it as a scam until proven otherwise.

## What to do with a suspicious message

1. **Do not click, reply or call back** using details in the message.
2. **Verify independently** – type the official website yourself, use the official app, or call the number printed on your card or on the organisation's official website.
3. **Report it.** Safaricom subscribers can forward a scam SMS to **333**. Report phishing emails using your email app's *Report phishing* or *Report spam* option.
4. **Delete it** and warn family members, especially older relatives.
