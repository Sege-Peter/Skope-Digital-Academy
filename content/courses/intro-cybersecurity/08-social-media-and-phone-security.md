Your phone holds your M-Pesa, bank apps, photos, chats and the codes that protect your other accounts. Your social media profiles hold your life story. Both deserve careful protection.

## What not to post

Criminals collect small details from many posts to build a picture of you – for SIM-swap fraud, fake accounts or burglary.

| Avoid posting | Why |
|---|---|
| Photos of your ID, passport, KCSE certificate or bank card | Used for identity theft and loan fraud |
| Boarding passes, tickets or bills with barcodes | Contain personal and booking details |
| Your exact home location and daily routine | Shows when the house is empty |
| "I'm travelling to Mombasa for two weeks!" while still away | Same – post after you return |
| Answers to common security questions (first school, mother's maiden name) | Help attackers reset your accounts |
| Screenshots showing your phone number and M-Pesa balance | Make you a target |

> **Tip:** Before posting, ask: *Would I be comfortable if a stranger, a future employer and a fraudster all saw this?* Employers in Kenya do look at applicants' social media.

## Privacy settings

Every platform lets you control who sees your content. Menus change often, but look for **Settings** › **Privacy** (or **Accounts Centre** for Facebook and Instagram).

- **Facebook:** limit who can see your posts, friends list, phone number and email; review where you are tagged.
- **Instagram:** consider a **private account** for personal photos; control who can message you.
- **TikTok:** set your account to private if you do not post for business; control who can duet, comment and download your videos.
- **WhatsApp:** **Settings** › **Privacy** – choose who sees your profile photo, *About*, last seen and status; set **Groups** so strangers cannot add you to scam groups.
- **X (Twitter):** protect your posts if your account is personal; turn off location tagging.

## Fake accounts and impersonation

Scammers copy someone's photos and name to create a duplicate account, then message their friends asking for money. If this happens to you:

1. Use the platform's **Report** option and choose "Pretending to be me / someone I know".
2. Ask friends to report it too.
3. Post a short warning on your real account.

Also be careful of strangers who quickly build a romantic or business friendship online and then ask for money, crypto investment or intimate photos. That is a common pattern of **romance and sextortion scams**. Never send money or intimate content to someone you have not met and verified; if you are threatened, do not pay – keep the evidence, block, and report to the platform and the police.

## Securing your phone

| Setting | What to do |
|---|---|
| Screen lock | Use a 6-digit PIN, pattern of at least 6 points, or fingerprint/face – never "None" or "Swipe" |
| SIM PIN | Turn on a SIM PIN so a thief cannot put your SIM in another phone without it |
| Find my device | Turn on **Find My Device** (Android) or **Find My** (iPhone) to locate, lock or erase a lost phone |
| Updates | Install system and app updates promptly |
| App locks | Use the fingerprint or PIN lock inside M-Pesa, banking and WhatsApp |
| Notifications | Hide message content on the lock screen so OTP codes are not visible to anyone holding your phone |
| Note your IMEI | Dial `*#06#` and write the number down; it helps police and your network identify a stolen phone |

> **Warning:** Before selling or giving away a phone, back it up, sign out of your Google or Apple account, then do a **factory reset**. Otherwise the new owner may access your accounts and photos.

## If your phone is lost or stolen

1. Call your network provider from another phone to **block the SIM**.
2. Use Find My Device / Find My to **lock** or **erase** it.
3. Change your email and social media passwords from another device.
4. Report to the nearest police station and keep the reference (OB) number.
5. Get a replacement SIM from an official shop with your ID.
