## The situation

Achieng runs "Achieng's Mini Mart", a made-up shop in Kisumu with one assistant, Brian. They use M-Pesa for payments, WhatsApp for orders and one shared phone for the shop's Facebook page. Last month a customer was nearly tricked by a fake "wrong number" message. Achieng asks you to make a **simple security pack** she can print and stick in the shop: a checklist for protecting accounts and phones, and a guide to spotting scam messages. This project is defensive only: you will help people stay safe, not test or attack anyone's real accounts.

> **Note:** Do not include anyone's real PIN, OTP, password, ID number or phone number. Use made-up examples such as "07XX XXX XXX" and never ask a peer for these either.

## What you will produce

One document (text box, or a PDF/Word file) with three parts:

1. **Security checklist** – 10–12 tick-box items for the shop, grouped under headings such as Phone, Accounts, M-Pesa and Backups. Each item is one short action (e.g. "Turn on 2-step verification for WhatsApp").
2. **Scam-spotting guide** – write 3 made-up scam messages (SMS or WhatsApp, 1–3 lines each, with no real names or numbers). Under each one, list the red flags and say what to do.
3. **If something goes wrong** – 5 short steps Achieng and Brian follow if they think an account is hacked or money was sent to a scammer, including who to call (write "the official customer care number from the provider's website" – do not invent numbers).

Add a 3-line "Golden rules" box at the top, including "never share your PIN or OTP".

## Steps

1. Re-read the lessons on phishing, M-Pesa fraud, passwords, two-factor authentication and backups.
2. List risks for the shop, then turn each into a short checklist action.
3. Invent your scam messages from the patterns you learned and mark the red flags.
4. Write the "if something goes wrong" steps in order.
5. Ask someone to read it and say if anything is unclear. Fix it.

## What to submit

- Your pack in the text box **or** as a PDF/Word file (max 5 MB), or a Google Docs link set to view access.

## Tips

- Use plain words a busy shop assistant can follow in a minute.
- One action per checklist line – not "set up everything".
- Do not claim to know what a bank or network will do; say "contact them".

```rubric
Security checklist | Ten or more clear, practical, correctly grouped actions that fit a small shop | 6
Scam examples and red flags | Three realistic made-up scams, each with accurate red flags and the right response | 6
If something goes wrong | Five steps in a sensible order that point to official help | 4
Presentation and safety | Easy to read, correct spelling, golden rules included and no real personal data | 4
```
