Kenya is one of the most connected countries in Africa. We pay with M-Pesa, apply for jobs online, chat on WhatsApp, run businesses on Instagram and access government services through eCitizen. All of this is convenient – and it also attracts criminals who want our money, our accounts and our personal information.

**Cybersecurity** is the practice of protecting devices, accounts, networks and information from theft, damage and misuse. You do not need to be a computer expert to practise it. Most attacks against ordinary people succeed because of simple mistakes that you will learn to avoid in this course.

## What attackers want

| Target | Why they want it | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Money | Direct theft | Tricking you into sending M-Pesa or revealing your PIN |
| Accounts | To scam your contacts or sell the account | A hacked WhatsApp asking your friends for "urgent" money |
| Personal data | To impersonate you or take loans in your name | ID number, date of birth, photos of your ID |
| Devices | To use them for further attacks or to lock your files | Malware on a phone or office computer |

## Most attacks target people, not machines

Movies show hackers typing green code at lightning speed. In real life, most fraud starts with a message or a phone call. The criminal pretends to be someone you trust – a bank, Safaricom, a relative, an employer – and persuades you to do something: click a link, share a code, send money or install an app. This is called **social engineering**.

Social engineering works by creating strong feelings:

- **Urgency** – "Your account will be closed in 2 hours!"
- **Fear** – "Police are looking for you."
- **Greed or excitement** – "You have won KES 50,000!"
- **Helpfulness or sympathy** – "Mum, I'm stuck, please send money to this number."

> **Tip:** When a message makes you feel rushed, scared or very excited, **stop**. That feeling is a warning sign. Take time to check before you act.

## The CIA triad

Security professionals describe their goals with three words, known as the **CIA triad** (nothing to do with the spy agency):

| Goal | Meaning | Everyday example |
|---|---|---|
| **Confidentiality** | Only the right people can see information | Nobody else reads your messages or knows your PIN |
| **Integrity** | Information is accurate and not secretly changed | A customer's M-Pesa statement has not been edited |
| **Availability** | Information and systems work when needed | The shop's computer and files are there on Monday morning |

Every protection you learn in this course supports at least one of these goals.

## Kenyan laws you should know about

Kenya has laws that deal with online crime and personal data:

- The **Computer Misuse and Cybercrimes Act, 2018** makes offences such as unauthorised access to computers, computer fraud and identity theft illegal.
- The **Data Protection Act, 2019** gives people rights over their personal data and sets duties for organisations that collect it. The **Office of the Data Protection Commissioner** (odpc.go.ke) oversees it.

> **Note:** Laws are amended over time. For exact details, read the current text on official sources such as kenyalaw.org rather than relying on summaries.

These laws also mean that "testing" or breaking into someone else's account or system without permission is a crime – even out of curiosity.

## Practice task

Look through your SMS inbox, WhatsApp and email for the last month. Find **one message** that you now suspect was a scam or that tried to create urgency, fear or excitement. Write down: who it pretended to be, what it wanted you to do, and which feeling it used.
