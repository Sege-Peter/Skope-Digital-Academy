## The situation

Achieng has just finished school in Kisumu. She wants to start a small business, but she has only a little money and no idea if people will buy. Instead of spending everything on day one, she writes a **30-day test plan**: one idea, a few real customer conversations, a simple price, one marketing post and a way to record money. In this project you will do the same for an idea of your own (a real idea is fine, but the numbers can be guesses).

> **Note:** Use made-up details wherever privacy matters. When you describe customers you talked to, write "Customer 1, a woman selling vegetables" – never real names, phone numbers, ID numbers or M-Pesa numbers.

## What you will produce

One document (text box, or a PDF/Word file) with five parts:

1. **The idea** – 60–100 words: the problem you solve, who pays, and where you will sell.
2. **Customer test** – a table with five rows (Customer 1 to 5): the question you asked and what they said, in one line each. Then 2–3 sentences on what you learned.
3. **One-page business model** – a small table with these rows: customers, problem, your offer, how you reach people, income, main costs.
4. **Price and one good day** – a start-up list of at least five items (must-have or nice-to-have) with example prices in KES, a cost-plus price for one product or service, and a "one good day" sum (customers x price – costs = profit). Show your working.
5. **30-day action plan** – one offer post (40–70 words: what, for whom, how much, how to order) and a four-week list of actions, including how you will record money in and out.

## Steps

1. Re-read the lessons on validating an idea, the business model, pricing and marketing.
2. Choose one idea. Write it down in plain words.
3. Talk to five people (or ask five friends to act as customers) using open questions, not "Would you buy this?".
4. Fill in the model table and the price sums. Check your arithmetic twice.
5. Write the offer post and the 30-day list.
6. Read everything aloud, fix mistakes and submit.

## What to submit

- Your whole plan in the text box **or** as a PDF/Word file (max 5 MB).
- Optional: a link to a photo or Google Doc of your notebook pages.

## Tips

- Prices can be your honest guesses – say they are examples and where you would check real ones.
- A plan that says "I will test with KES 500" is better than one that needs big capital.
- Do not copy a business you saw online word for word; use your own area and your own customers.
- If a customer says no, write it down – that is useful information, not failure.

```rubric
Idea and customer test | A clear problem and buyer, five customer notes with honest lessons learned | 5
Business model | The model table is complete and every row fits the idea | 4
Price and profit working | Start-up list and cost-plus price shown step by step, and the one-good-day sum is correct | 5
Offer post and 30-day plan | The post says what, for whom, how much and how to order; the plan has specific weekly actions and a money record | 4
Presentation | Easy to read, correct spelling, no real personal data | 2
```
