A **business model** is a short description of how your business creates value for customers and how it makes money. You do not need a 30-page business plan to start. You need clear answers to a few questions.

## The one-page business model

| Question | Kiprono's phone-charging stall (Kericho market) |
|---|---|
| **Customers** – who pays? | Traders and shoppers at the market without power |
| **Problem** – what do they need? | A charged phone to receive M-Pesa payments and calls |
| **Offer** – what do you give them? | Safe phone charging while they shop, KES 20 per phone |
| **Channel** – how do they find and reach you? | A stall at the market gate, signboard, word of mouth |
| **Resources** – what do you need? | Solar panel, battery, charging cables, a lockable box |
| **Costs** – what do you spend money on? | Market fee, cable replacements, his own lunch money is *not* a business cost |
| **Revenue** – how does money come in? | Cash or M-Pesa per charge; extra income from selling cables |

If you can fill this table for your idea, you understand your business better than many people who have been trading for years.

## Ways small businesses make money

1. **Selling items** – buy at one price, sell at a higher one (shop, mitumba).
2. **Charging for a service** – per job, per hour or per item (repairs, tutoring).
3. **Subscriptions or regular orders** – the same customer pays every week or month (daily milk delivery, monthly cleaning).
4. **Commission** – you earn a share for connecting a buyer and a seller (a house agent).

Regular orders are powerful because you can predict your income.

## Revenue, costs and profit

Three words you will use throughout this course:

- **Revenue** (or sales) – all the money customers pay you.
- **Costs** – the money you spend to run the business.
- **Profit** – what is left: `Profit = Revenue − Costs`.

```text
Kiprono, one market day
Revenue: 30 phones x KES 20 = KES 600
Costs:   market fee KES 100 + cable wear KES 50 = KES 150
Profit:  KES 600 − KES 150 = KES 450
```

> **Note:** If costs are bigger than revenue, the result is a **loss**. A business that keeps making losses is using up money instead of earning it.

## Know your competitors

Ask: who else solves this problem, how much do they charge, and why would someone choose you? Your reason might be location, speed, quality, friendliness or a lower price – but avoid competing on price alone, because it squeezes your profit.

```exercise
Q: Kiprono charges 25 phones at KES 20 each. What is his revenue?
Revenue = KES ___
A: 500
```

```exercise
Q: Fill in the formula.
Profit = ___ − ___
A: revenue / sales | costs / expenses
```
