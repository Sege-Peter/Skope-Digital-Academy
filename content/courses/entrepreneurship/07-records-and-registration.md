Two things separate a hobby from a real business: **records** that show how the business is doing, and the **legal steps** that let it operate openly and grow.

## Why keep records?

Without records you are guessing. With them you can answer:

- Am I actually making a profit, or just handling a lot of cash?
- Which products sell best?
- Who owes me money, and whom do I owe?
- Can I show a SACCO or bank that my business is real?

## A simple daily record

A notebook or a free spreadsheet is enough to start. Write every sale and every expense on the day it happens.

| Date | Details | Money in (KES) | Money out (KES) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3 Mar | Sold 40 chapatis | 1,200 | |
| 3 Mar | Flour and oil | | 600 |
| 3 Mar | Charcoal | | 150 |
| 4 Mar | Sold 35 chapatis | 1,050 | |
| 4 Mar | Beans | | 300 |
| **Total** | | **2,250** | **1,050** |

Profit for the two days: 2,250 − 1,050 = **KES 1,200**.

> **Tip:** Use a separate M-Pesa line or a Till/Pochi la Biashara for business so your statement becomes an automatic record. Never mix school fees or personal shopping with business money.

## Registering a business in Kenya – the general steps

Registration lets you open a business bank account, apply for tenders, sign contracts and build trust. The exact requirements, forms and fees change from time to time, so always follow the official sources rather than a friend's advice or an old blog post.

1. **Get a KRA PIN** if you do not have one – done online through iTax on [kra.go.ke](https://www.kra.go.ke). You will need your national ID.
2. **Choose a business structure.** The common choices for beginners are a *business name* (sole proprietorship or partnership) or a *private limited company*. A business name is simpler; a company is a separate legal person and has more duties.
3. **Search and reserve a name** and then **register** through the Business Registration Service on [eCitizen](https://www.ecitizen.go.ke) (see also [brs.go.ke](https://brs.go.ke)).
4. **Get the county business permit** (often called a single business permit) from your county government. Each county sets its own process and charges.
5. **Check sector licences** – food businesses, transport, health products and others may need extra permits or inspections.
6. **Understand your tax duties** – check kra.go.ke for the taxes that apply to your business type and size, and for filing deadlines.

> **Warning:** Only pay registration fees through the official eCitizen or KRA systems. Be careful with "agents" who ask you to send money to a personal number to "fast-track" registration.

> **Note:** This lesson gives general steps, not legal advice. For anything unclear, ask at a Huduma Centre or consult a qualified accountant or lawyer.
