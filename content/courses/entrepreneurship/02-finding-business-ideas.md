Good business ideas rarely arrive as a flash of genius. They usually come from paying attention to everyday problems, complaints and gaps in your own area.

## Where ideas come from

| Source | Questions to ask | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Complaints | What do people around me keep complaining about? | "There is no one to fix phone screens near our school." |
| Your skills | What can I do that others pay for? | Braiding, baking, graphic design, maths tutoring |
| Things you see selling fast | What runs out quickly in local shops? | Smocha, airtime, sanitary pads near a hostel |
| Changes | What is new in my area? | A new estate needs cleaners, water delivery and gas refills |
| Waste | What is thrown away that someone could use? | Plastic bottles, offcuts from tailors, maize stalks |

## Write many ideas first

Do not judge your ideas too early. Spend 15 minutes listing at least **ten** ideas, even silly ones. Quantity first, quality later.

```text
My idea list (Brian, Eldoret)
1. Wash school shoes on Sunday evenings
2. Sell chapati and beans to construction workers
3. Charge phones at the market (solar panel)
4. Design posters for churches on Canva
5. Deliver gas cylinders by bicycle
6. Sell second-hand textbooks
...
```

## Choose with a simple score

Score each idea from 1 (poor) to 5 (good) on four questions, then add the scores.

| Idea | People need it? | I can do it now? | Low start-up cost? | I enjoy it? | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Chapati for workers | 5 | 4 | 4 | 3 | 16 |
| Canva posters | 3 | 5 | 5 | 5 | 18 |
| Gas delivery | 4 | 2 | 1 | 3 | 10 |

Brian's best options are the posters (18) and the chapati (16). Gas delivery needs a lot of capital and safety knowledge, so it waits.

> **Tip:** The best first business is often one where you already have the skill and most of the tools. That keeps your risk low.

## Ideas to be careful with

> **Warning:** Avoid "business opportunities" where you must pay a joining fee and then recruit other people to earn. That is usually a pyramid scheme, not a business. A real business earns from selling something useful to customers.

Also be careful with ideas that need a licence you cannot yet get (for example selling medicine or alcohol), or that depend on a single customer.

## Practice task

Take a notebook and list **ten business ideas** you could start in your area. Score your top five using the four questions in the table above. Circle the idea with the highest total – you will test it in Week 2.
