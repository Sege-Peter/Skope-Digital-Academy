An **entrepreneur** is someone who spots a need, organises the money, time and people to meet it, and takes the risk of running the business. A **business** is simply a way of solving a problem for people who are willing to pay for the solution.

You do not need a big office, a loan or a fancy name to be an entrepreneur. The mama mboga at the stage, the boda boda rider who delivers parcels, and the student who edits photos for weddings are all entrepreneurs.

## Why it matters for school leavers

Formal jobs are limited, and many employers ask for experience. A small business lets you:

- earn money while you wait for college or a job,
- build experience you can show later,
- learn skills such as selling, negotiating and managing money,
- create work for other people as you grow.

> **Note:** Entrepreneurship is not a guaranteed way to get rich. Many small businesses earn modest amounts, and some fail. This course is about starting carefully so that a failure costs you little and teaches you a lot.

## Traits that help

Nobody is born an entrepreneur. These habits can be learned:

| Habit | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| Curiosity | Asking "why do people complain about this?" |
| Discipline | Opening on time even when sales are slow |
| Honesty | Delivering what you promised, at the price you promised |
| Learning from failure | Changing what did not work instead of giving up |
| Careful with money | Keeping business money separate and tracking every shilling |

## Types of small businesses

Most beginner businesses fall into one of these groups:

1. **Products** – selling goods you buy or make (eggs, second-hand clothes, liquid soap).
2. **Services** – selling your time and skill (hair, phone repair, CV writing, tutoring).
3. **Online businesses** – services or products sold through WhatsApp, Instagram, TikTok or a website.

Services usually need less starting money because you are not buying stock. Products can grow faster once you find steady buyers.

## A short story

Achieng finished Form Four in Kisumu. She noticed that parents in her estate struggled to find someone to plait their daughters' hair on Saturday mornings. She started with a comb, some extensions and a plastic chair outside her mother's house. Six months later she had regular customers and was teaching a friend to help her. She did not start with a business plan – she started with a problem she could see.
