When you search Google for "laptop repair Nairobi CBD", some of the results at the top or bottom of the page are marked **Sponsored**. Those are **search ads**. A business paid to appear there for that search. This course teaches you how these ads work and how to measure whether they bring real customers.

## Why search ads are special

On social media, people are scrolling for entertainment, and ads interrupt them. On Google, people are **actively looking** for something. Someone searching "emergency plumber Thika" has a problem right now. That **intent** is why search ads can work well even for small businesses.

## Google Ads in one paragraph

**Google Ads** is Google's advertising platform. A business creates an account, chooses the searches it wants to appear for (keywords), writes ads, sets a budget, and tells Google where to send people (a landing page, a call, or directions). Google then shows the ads to people whose searches match, in the locations the business chose.

## Pay per click (PPC)

For most search campaigns, the advertiser pays when someone **clicks** the ad, not when it is merely shown. This is called **pay-per-click (PPC)**. The amount paid for a click is the **cost per click (CPC)**.

```text
Ad shown 1,000 times  -> impressions = 1,000
People clicked 40 times -> clicks = 40
Total cost KES 2,000  -> average CPC = 2,000 ÷ 40 = KES 50
```

These numbers are only an example. Real costs depend on the industry, location, competition and ad quality.

## The ad auction

Every time someone searches, Google runs a quick **auction** among advertisers whose keywords match. The winner is not simply whoever pays the most. Google combines several factors, including:

- **The bid** – the most the advertiser is willing to pay for a click.
- **Ad quality** – how relevant the ad and the landing page are to the search, and how likely people are to click.
- **Context** – the searcher's location, device, time of day and the search itself.
- **Ad assets** – extra information such as phone numbers or links that make the ad more useful.

This means a small business with a very relevant ad and a good landing page can sometimes appear above a bigger competitor that bids more but has a weaker ad.

> **Note:** Google publishes help articles explaining the auction and quality. The exact formula is not public, so be wary of anyone who claims to "know the secret".

## Types of campaigns

Google Ads offers several campaign types, such as **Search** (text ads on search results), **Display** (image ads on websites and apps), **Video** (ads on YouTube), **Shopping** (product listings) and automated campaign types that mix several placements. This course focuses on **Search**, the best place for beginners to start.

## Search ads vs SEO

| | Search ads | SEO (organic) |
|---|---|---|
| Cost | Pay for each click | Free clicks, but takes effort |
| Speed | Can show within a day of approval | Usually takes weeks or months |
| Stops when | The budget runs out or you pause | Continues while rankings hold |
| Label | Marked "Sponsored" | No label |

Many businesses use both: ads for quick results, SEO for long-term visibility.
