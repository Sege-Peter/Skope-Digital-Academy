**Keywords** are the words and phrases an advertiser chooses so their ads can show when people search for related things. The search a person actually types is called a **search term** (or query). Choosing good keywords is the most important skill in search advertising.

## Thinking like the customer

Imagine Otieno runs a driving school in Kisumu. People who might need him could search:

- driving school Kisumu
- learn to drive Kisumu
- driving lessons near me
- driving school fees Kisumu
- how to get a driving licence in Kenya

Not every search is equally valuable. "Driving school Kisumu" shows strong intent to enrol. "How to get a driving licence in Kenya" is more about information – the person may not be ready to pay yet.

## Types of intent

| Intent | Example search | Value for ads |
|---|---|---|
| Buying / action | "book driving lessons Kisumu" | High – ready to act |
| Comparing | "best driving school Kisumu" | Good – choosing a provider |
| Information | "what is a provisional licence" | Lower – still learning |
| Navigational | "Otieno driving school contacts" | Already looking for that business |

Beginners should focus budget on **action** and **comparing** searches.

## Finding keyword ideas

- Ask the business owner what customers say when they call.
- Type a word into Google and look at the autocomplete suggestions and "related searches".
- Use Google's **Keyword Planner** (inside Google Ads), which suggests keywords and gives rough estimates of searches and costs. Treat these as estimates, not promises.
- Include locations: town names, estates and "near me".

## Match types

A **match type** controls how closely a search must match your keyword before your ad can show. Google currently offers three:

| Match type | How you write it | What it does (simplified) |
|---|---|---|
| **Broad match** | driving lessons | Can show for searches related to the meaning, even without the same words. Widest reach, least control |
| **Phrase match** | "driving lessons" | Shows when the search includes the meaning of the phrase, such as "cheap driving lessons in Kisumu" |
| **Exact match** | [driving lessons] | Shows for searches with the same meaning as the keyword. Narrowest reach, most control |

> **Note:** Google has changed how match types behave several times over the years. Read the current definitions in the Google Ads Help Centre before running a real campaign.

## Negative keywords

**Negative keywords** stop your ad from showing for searches you do not want. They save money.

For Otieno's paid driving school, good negatives might be:

```text
free
jobs
vacancies
salary
pdf
```

Someone searching "driving school jobs Kisumu" wants employment, not lessons. Adding "jobs" as a negative keyword prevents paying for that click.

## Search terms report

After a campaign runs, Google Ads shows a report of the actual **search terms** that triggered your ads. Check it regularly. You will find new good keywords to add, and irrelevant searches to block with negatives.

## Practice task

Choose a local business (for example a hardware shop in Kitale or a cake baker in Thika). Write **10 keywords** grouped into "action", "comparing" and "information" intent, mark the 5 you would advertise on, and write **5 negative keywords** with a reason for each.
