Advertising spends real money – often the hard-earned money of a small business owner. Understanding budgets and bidding helps you spend it carefully and explain results honestly.

## Daily budget

In Google Ads, you usually set an **average daily budget** for each campaign. Google spreads spending across the month. On some days it may spend more than the daily amount and on others less, but it aims to keep the monthly total within a limit linked to your daily budget. Read the current rules in the Google Ads Help Centre so you can explain them to clients.

A simple way to plan:

```text
Monthly amount the client can afford ÷ about 30 days = daily budget
Example: KES 15,000 ÷ 30 = KES 500 per day
```

This is an example, not a recommendation. Every business must choose an amount it can afford to lose while testing.

## Bids

A **bid** is the most you are willing to pay for a click (or for another result, depending on the strategy). In the auction you learned about in [How Search Ads Work](how-search-ads-work), the bid is combined with ad quality and other factors. Often you pay **less** than your maximum bid.

## Bidding strategies

Google offers different strategies. They fall into two families:

| Family | Examples | How it works |
|---|---|---|
| **Manual** | Manual CPC | You set the maximum bid per click yourself. Lots of control, more work |
| **Automated (smart)** | Maximise clicks, maximise conversions, target cost per action, target return on ad spend | Google sets bids automatically for each auction based on your goal |

Automated strategies that aim for conversions need **conversion tracking** (see [Google Analytics Concepts](analytics-concepts)) and usually work better once a campaign has collected some conversion data. Many beginners start with a simple strategy, learn from the results, then move to a conversion-focused strategy.

> **Note:** Strategy names and options change from time to time. Learn the idea behind each one – clicks, conversions, cost per result, return – and you will recognise them even if the names shift.

## Key money metrics

| Metric | Formula | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **CPC** | Cost ÷ clicks | Average price of a click |
| **Conversion rate** | Conversions ÷ clicks × 100 | Share of clicks that became enquiries or sales |
| **Cost per conversion (CPA)** | Cost ÷ conversions | What one result cost |
| **ROAS** | Revenue from ads ÷ ad cost | Shillings earned per shilling spent |

## Worked example

Kiprono's tuition centre in Eldoret spent KES 6,000 in a month on search ads.

```text
Clicks: 150            CPC = 6,000 ÷ 150 = KES 40
Enquiry forms: 12      Conversion rate = 12 ÷ 150 × 100 = 8%
                       Cost per enquiry = 6,000 ÷ 12 = KES 500
```

Is KES 500 per enquiry good? That depends on the business. If one in three enquiries enrols and a student pays fees worth many times that, it may be excellent. If the margin is tiny, it may be too expensive. **The right cost depends on the value of a customer.**

```exercise
Q: A campaign cost KES 4,000 and got 200 clicks. What was the average CPC?
CPC = KES ___
A: 20
```

```exercise
Q: The same campaign got 10 conversions from 200 clicks. Find the conversion rate and the cost per conversion (cost was KES 4,000).
Conversion rate = ___%   Cost per conversion = KES ___
A: 5 | 400
```

## Budget safety habits

1. Start small and test for long enough to learn (not just one day).
2. Use location targeting and negative keywords to avoid waste.
3. Check the account several times a week in the first weeks.
4. Agree the maximum monthly spend with the client **in writing**.
5. The client should pay Google directly with their own billing details, so the money trail is clear.

> **Warning:** Scammers sell "cheap Google Ads credit" or ask you to pay into personal numbers for "ad coupons". Only pay through the official Google Ads billing inside the account.

## Practice task

A client says: "I can spend KES 9,000 a month." Calculate the daily budget. Then, assuming an average CPC of KES 30 (an example figure), estimate how many clicks the budget could buy in a month. If 6% of those clicks become customers, how many customers is that? Write your answers and explain why the real numbers may differ.
