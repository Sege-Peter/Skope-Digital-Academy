When you manage ads and analytics, you handle other people's money and data. Clients and their customers must be able to trust you. Ethical work is not only "the right thing" – it is also how you keep clients for years and get referrals.

## Honest promises

Be careful with what you promise a client:

- **Never guarantee** "first position on Google" or a fixed number of sales. Nobody controls the auction or the customer's decision.
- Explain that the first weeks are a **testing period**.
- Share both good and bad results.
- Be clear about what is your fee and what is the **ad spend** paid to Google.

## Honest ads

Ads must not mislead customers. Avoid:

- Fake discounts ("was KES 5,000, now KES 2,000" when it was never KES 5,000).
- Claims you cannot prove ("Kenya's number one school").
- Hiding important conditions, such as extra charges.
- Advertising products or services that are illegal or not allowed by Google's policies – for example counterfeit goods or misleading financial offers.

Kenya's consumer protection rules also apply to advertising. If a client wants something that feels dishonest, say no and explain why.

## Click fraud and competitor tricks

**Click fraud** is clicking ads on purpose to waste someone's money – for example repeatedly clicking a competitor's ads. It is dishonest, it harms other businesses, and Google has systems to detect invalid clicks. Never do it, never pay anyone to do it, and never accept "clients" who ask for it.

Also avoid using a competitor's **trademarked** business name in your ad text without permission; Google has trademark policies about this.

## Data and privacy

- Use Analytics to understand **groups of visitors**, not to spy on individuals.
- Do not collect more personal data than needed in forms.
- Make sure the website has a clear privacy notice.
- Follow Kenya's Data Protection Act – the Office of the Data Protection Commissioner (odpc.go.ke) publishes guidance for businesses.

## Account access and ownership

This is a common source of disputes:

1. The **client should own** the Google Ads and Analytics accounts, with their own billing.
2. You should be **added as a user** with the access level you need, not given the owner's password.
3. When the contract ends, the client removes your access and keeps all the data.
4. Keep client accounts secure with strong passwords and two-step verification.

> **Warning:** Never share login codes sent by SMS or email. Be suspicious of messages pretending to be "Google Ads Support" asking you to log in through a link or pay a fee to "unlock" an account. Go to the official site yourself instead of clicking links.

## Your learning path

To grow in this field:

- Study Google's free official training. **Google Skillshop** offers free courses and certifications for Google Ads and Analytics – a recognised way to show your skills.
- Practise with Google's **demo Analytics account** (if available), which lets you explore real reports without your own website.
- Offer a small, low-risk first project to a local business, with a clear written agreement.
- Keep a simple portfolio: the problem, what you did, and the results (with the client's permission).

## Practice task

Write a short **client agreement checklist** (8–10 points) you would go through before starting any ads project. Include: who owns the account, the monthly ad spend limit, your fee and when it is paid, reporting frequency, what you cannot guarantee, and how access is removed at the end.
