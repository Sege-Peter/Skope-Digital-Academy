A messy Google Ads account wastes money because ads do not match searches well. A tidy structure makes ads more relevant, makes reports easier to read and helps you control the budget.

## The three levels

Think of a Google Ads account like a filing cabinet.

| Level | Like a... | What you set here |
|---|---|---|
| **Account** | The whole cabinet | Business details, billing, users and access |
| **Campaign** | A drawer | Budget, locations, languages, bidding approach, campaign type, schedule |
| **Ad group** | A folder in the drawer | A tight group of related keywords and the ads written for them |

Inside each ad group are **keywords** and **ads**. The rule of thumb is: **one theme per ad group**, so that the ad can speak directly to the keywords.

## An example structure

Fatuma owns a cleaning company in Mombasa offering home cleaning, office cleaning and sofa/carpet cleaning.

```text
Account: Fatuma Cleaning Services
|
+-- Campaign: Mombasa - Search (budget, location: Mombasa County)
    |
    +-- Ad group: Home cleaning
    |     Keywords: house cleaning Mombasa, home cleaners Mombasa, ...
    |     Ads: "Trusted Home Cleaners in Mombasa"
    |
    +-- Ad group: Office cleaning
    |     Keywords: office cleaning services Mombasa, commercial cleaners ...
    |     Ads: "Office Cleaning - Before Work Hours"
    |
    +-- Ad group: Sofa and carpet
          Keywords: sofa cleaning Mombasa, carpet cleaning Nyali, ...
          Ads: "Sofa & Carpet Cleaning at Your Home"
```

Someone searching "sofa cleaning Nyali" sees an ad about sofa cleaning, not a general ad about all services. That relevance tends to improve click rates and ad quality.

## When to create separate campaigns

Because budget and location are set at the **campaign** level, create separate campaigns when you need different settings:

- **Different budgets** – the owner wants to spend more on office cleaning because those contracts are bigger.
- **Different locations** – a business with branches in Nairobi and Nakuru.
- **Different goals** – one campaign for calls, another for website enquiries.
- **Different languages** – for example English and Kiswahili ads, if the business serves both audiences.

## Key campaign settings

- **Goal** – what you want: website enquiries, calls, store visits, sales.
- **Locations** – target only areas the business actually serves. A Kisumu delivery service should not pay for clicks from Garissa.
- **Ad schedule** – show ads when the business can respond, for example during working hours if the goal is phone calls.
- **Networks** – for a beginner search campaign, many advertisers choose to show only on Google Search at first, so results are easier to understand. Check what the current settings allow.

> **Tip:** Before building anything in the real platform, draw the structure on paper or in a spreadsheet. It is much easier to fix a plan than a live account.

## Practice task

Pick a business with at least three different services (for example a cyber café that offers printing, KRA PIN and eCitizen services, and typing). Draw its account structure like the example: one campaign, three ad groups, and at least four keywords per ad group. Then decide whether any service needs its own separate campaign, and write why.
