Reports are only useful if they lead to a decision: keep, change, pause or scale up. In this lesson you will read simple Google Ads and Analytics figures and turn them into clear advice a business owner can understand.

## Always compare

A single number means little on its own. "We got 300 clicks" – compared with what? Useful comparisons:

- **This period vs last period** – this month vs last month.
- **Campaign vs campaign** or **ad group vs ad group**.
- **Against the goal** – the cost per enquiry the client can afford.
- **Device vs device** – mobile vs desktop.

Choose date ranges of the same length, and remember seasons: school openings, holidays and month-end can change behaviour a lot in Kenya.

## Reading a Google Ads table

Here is an invented example for a Nakuru catering business:

| Ad group | Impr. | Clicks | CTR | Cost (KES) | Conv. | Cost/conv. (KES) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Wedding catering | 4,000 | 160 | 4% | 6,400 | 8 | 800 |
| Office lunch | 2,500 | 50 | 2% | 1,500 | 6 | 250 |
| Cheap catering | 6,000 | 240 | 4% | 4,800 | 1 | 4,800 |

What does it tell us?

- **Office lunch** has the lowest cost per conversion. It may deserve more budget.
- **Wedding catering** costs more per enquiry, but weddings are bigger jobs, so it may still be very profitable. Ask the owner.
- **Cheap catering** gets many clicks but almost no enquiries. Check the search terms report – people searching "cheap" may want prices the business cannot offer. Consider pausing it or adding negatives.

```exercise
Q: Use the table. Which ad group has the lowest cost per conversion, and what is that cost?
Ad group: ___ lunch   Cost per conversion: KES ___
A: office | 250
```

## Reading an Analytics traffic report

Example: visits to the same caterer's website in one month.

| Source / medium | Sessions | Engagement rate | Key events |
|---|---|---|---|
| google / cpc | 450 | 62% | 15 |
| google / organic | 300 | 70% | 9 |
| facebook / social | 500 | 35% | 3 |
| (direct) / (none) | 120 | 55% | 4 |

Observations:

- Facebook sends the most sessions but has a low engagement rate and few key events. The posts may attract curious people rather than buyers, or the link may go to the wrong page.
- Google Ads and organic search bring fewer but more engaged visitors – people searching with intent.

## Common mistakes in reading data

1. **Judging too early.** A few days of data can mislead; wait for enough clicks.
2. **Chasing clicks instead of results.** High CTR with zero conversions is not success.
3. **Ignoring tracking problems.** If conversions suddenly drop to zero, check whether the tracking tag broke before blaming the ads.
4. **Confusing correlation with cause.** Sales rose in December – was it the ads, or the holiday season?

## Writing the summary

Business owners are busy. Lead with the result, explain simply, and recommend an action:

```text
Summary – March (Search campaign)
Spent KES 12,700 and received 15 enquiries (average KES 847 each).
Best performer: Office lunch ads – KES 250 per enquiry.
Problem: "cheap catering" searches used KES 4,800 for 1 enquiry.
Recommendation: pause "cheap catering", move part of its budget to office lunch,
and add "cheap" and "free" as negative keywords. Review again in 2 weeks.
```

> **Tip:** Never hide bad results. Explaining what went wrong and how you will fix it builds more trust than a report full of only good news.
