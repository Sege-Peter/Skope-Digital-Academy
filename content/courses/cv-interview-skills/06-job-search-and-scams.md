Now that your CV is ready, where do you find jobs? And how do you avoid the many fake "jobs" that target young people in Kenya? This lesson covers both.

## Where to look

| Source | Good for |
|---|---|
| Company websites and official social media pages | Supermarkets, banks, hotels, NGOs often post vacancies under "Careers" |
| LinkedIn Jobs | Office, sales, tech and NGO roles |
| Newspapers (print and online) | Government and county jobs, large companies |
| Public Service Commission and county government websites | Government jobs and internships |
| Your network | Relatives, teachers, church members, former classmates |
| Walking in | Shops, salons, hotels and restaurants in your town |

> **Tip:** Many entry-level jobs are never advertised. Tell people you trust that you are looking, and what you can do. "I am looking for work as a cashier or shop attendant. I am good with M-Pesa and customers" is easy for people to remember and pass on.

## Organise your search

Keep a simple table (paper, Google Sheets or Excel) of every application:

| Date | Company | Job | How applied | Follow-up date | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3 Mar | Tumaini Supermarket, Thika | Cashier | Email | 17 Mar | Waiting |
| 5 Mar | Bidii Hardware | Sales assistant | Walk-in | 12 Mar | Interview 14 Mar |

Aim for a steady number of good, tailored applications each week rather than sending the same CV to a hundred places in one day.

## Spotting job scams

Job scams are common and they target people who are eager to work. Learn these warning signs.

**Red flags:**

- They ask you to pay a "registration fee", "training fee", "medical fee", "uniform fee" or "processing fee" before you start. **Genuine employers do not charge you to get a job.**
- The pay is unusually high for very little work ("Earn KES 5,000 a day typing from your phone").
- You are "hired" without any interview, or the interview happens only on WhatsApp or Telegram.
- They ask for your M-Pesa PIN, bank PIN, passwords or a one-time code (OTP). **Never share these with anyone.**
- The email comes from a free address (like `@gmail.com`) but claims to be a big company or the UN.
- They pressure you: "Only 3 slots left, pay today!"
- Overseas jobs where the agent cannot show proper registration, or wants to keep your passport.

> **Warning:** Before accepting an overseas job, check that the recruitment agency is registered with Kenya's National Employment Authority, using the official government website. Never hand over your passport or pay cash to an unverified agent.

## How to check if a job is real

1. Search the company name plus "scam" or "fraud" online.
2. Visit the company's **official** website or verified page and see if the job is listed there.
3. Call the company using the number on its official website, not the number in the advert.
4. Ask someone you trust to look at the offer.
5. If anything feels wrong, walk away. A real opportunity will still be real tomorrow.

If you are scammed, report it to the police and to your mobile money provider as soon as possible, and warn friends so they are not caught too.
