You have just finished school. Maybe you have your KCSE results, maybe you are waiting for college, or maybe you want to start earning straight away. Either way, sooner or later someone will ask you for a **CV** and then invite you to an **interview**. This course shows you how to do both well, even if you have never had a formal job.

## What is a CV?

A **CV** (curriculum vitae) is a short document that tells an employer who you are, what you can do and how to contact you. In Kenya the words *CV* and *resume* are used for the same thing.

Your CV has one job: to get you **invited to an interview**. It does not need to tell your whole life story. It needs to make the reader think, "This person could do the job. Let us talk to them."

## How employers read CVs

A supermarket in Nakuru advertising for a cashier may receive more than 200 CVs. The manager will not read each one slowly. Most CVs get a quick scan of under a minute. In that time the reader looks for:

- Is this person's name and phone number easy to find?
- Does the CV match the job advertised?
- Is it neat, with no spelling mistakes?
- Is there any proof they can be trusted and can learn?

If the answer to these is "yes", your CV goes into the **shortlist** pile.

## "But I have no experience"

Almost every school leaver worries about this. The good news: employers hiring for entry-level jobs **know** you are young. They are not expecting ten years of experience. They are looking for signs that you are:

| Quality | Where you can show it |
|---|---|
| Reliable | Good attendance, prefect or class rep role, helping in a family business |
| Willing to learn | Short courses (like this one), self-taught computer skills |
| Good with people | Church youth group, sports team, selling at the market |
| Honest | Referees who know you, a clean and truthful CV |
| Able to communicate | A well-written CV and cover letter |

Helping at your aunt's shop in Kisumu, keeping records for your chama, coaching younger children in football or running a small mtumba business all count. You just need to describe them well. We will learn how in the next lessons.

> **Tip:** Think of "experience" as anything where you were responsible for something and got a result. That includes unpaid work and volunteering.

## The basic rules of a good CV

1. **Short** – one page is perfect for a school leaver; two pages at most.
2. **Clear** – simple headings, plenty of white space, one easy font.
3. **Honest** – never invent grades, jobs or certificates. Employers check, and lying can cost you the job later.
4. **Targeted** – adjust it slightly for each job you apply for.
5. **Error-free** – ask someone to read it before you send it.

## Practice task

Take a sheet of paper or open a note on your phone. Write down **every** activity you have done in the last three years where you were responsible for something: school roles, clubs, sports, church, family business, casual work, volunteering, online courses. Aim for at least eight items. Do not judge them yet; you will use this list in the next two lessons.
