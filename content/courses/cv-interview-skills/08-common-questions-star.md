Most interviewers ask a similar set of questions. If you prepare for them, you will not be caught off guard. This lesson gives you a simple method for answering and some model answers to adapt (never copy them word for word; use your own true stories).

## The STAR method

Many questions start with "Tell me about a time when…". These are called **behavioural questions**. Answer them with **STAR**:

| Letter | Meaning | What to say |
|---|---|---|
| S | Situation | Where and when it happened |
| T | Task | What you needed to do |
| A | Action | What **you** did, step by step |
| R | Result | What happened in the end, ideally with a number or something you learned |

Spend most of your time on **Action** and **Result**. Keep the whole answer to about one or two minutes.

## Model answer: "Tell me about a time you dealt with a difficult customer."

```text
S: During the December holidays I was helping at my mother's
   cereals shop in Kitale.
T: A customer came back angry, saying the beans we sold him had
   weevils.
A: I listened without interrupting, apologised, and checked the bag
   with him. They were from an old batch, so I replaced them with
   fresh beans and told my mother so we could check the rest of the
   stock.
R: He left happy and still buys from us. We also started checking
   every batch before selling it.
```

## "Tell me about yourself."

This is usually the first question. It is **not** a request for your life history. Use a simple formula: **present – past – future**.

```text
"I recently finished my KCSE at Kisii School, where I enjoyed
Business Studies and served as dining hall captain. For the last
year I've helped in my uncle's electronics shop, serving customers
and handling M-Pesa payments. I'm now looking for a sales role
where I can use those customer skills and keep learning, which is
why this position at your shop interests me."
```

## Other common questions

| Question | What they really want to know | How to answer |
|---|---|---|
| Why do you want to work here? | Have you done research? | Mention something specific about the company and link it to your interests |
| What are your strengths? | Can you do the job? | Name 2 strengths relevant to the job, each with a short example |
| What is your weakness? | Are you honest and self-aware? | A real but small weakness, plus what you are doing to improve |
| Where do you see yourself in five years? | Will you stay and grow? | Show ambition connected to the field, e.g. becoming a supervisor |
| Why should we hire you? | Summary of your fit | Two or three reasons that match the advert |
| What salary do you expect? | Are you realistic? | Research typical pay first; give a reasonable range or say you are open to their standard rate for the role |
| Do you have any questions for us? | Are you interested? | Always ask one or two (see below) |

## Model weakness answer

```text
"Sometimes I find it hard to speak up in big groups. In my last
year of school I joined the debate club to work on it, and I'm now
much more comfortable sharing my ideas."
```

## Good questions to ask them

- "What does a typical day look like in this role?"
- "What training do new staff receive?"
- "What would success look like in the first three months?"
- "When can I expect to hear about the next step?"

> **Warning:** Avoid asking about salary, leave or off-days as your very first question. Wait for an offer, or until they raise it.

## Practice task

Write STAR answers for three questions: a time you solved a problem, a time you worked in a team, and a time you made a mistake and fixed it. Then practise "Tell me about yourself" out loud until you can say it naturally in about one minute.
