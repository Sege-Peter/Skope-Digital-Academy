The interview is over, but you are not done yet. What you do in the next few days can still make a difference. This final lesson covers following up, handling offers and rejections, and then tests what you have learned.

## Send a thank-you message

Within 24 hours, send a short thank-you email or message. Few candidates do this, so it helps you stand out.

```text
Subject: Thank you – Sales Assistant interview

Dear Mr. Omondi,

Thank you for meeting me yesterday to discuss the Sales Assistant
role. I enjoyed learning about how your team serves customers at the
Kisumu branch, and I am even more excited about the opportunity.

Please let me know if you need any more information from me.

Kind regards,
Achieng Odhiambo
0712 000 000
```

## Following up if you hear nothing

If they said they would reply within a week and two weeks have passed, it is fine to send **one** polite follow-up asking about the status. If there is still no reply, move on and keep applying. Silence is common and is not personal.

## If you get an offer

- Thank them and ask for the offer **in writing**: job title, pay, working hours, start date.
- Read it carefully. Ask questions if anything is unclear.
- Statutory deductions and requirements change; check current information on official sites such as kra.go.ke rather than relying on what friends say.
- Be suspicious of any "offer" that asks you to pay money before starting.

## If you are not selected

Rejection is part of every job search. Reply politely and ask for feedback:

```text
Thank you for letting me know. I appreciated the chance to interview.
If possible, I would be grateful for any feedback that could help me
improve. I hope to apply again in future.
```

Then update your notes: which questions were hard? What would you answer differently? Each interview makes you better at the next one.
