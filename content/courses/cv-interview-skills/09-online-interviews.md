More employers now do the first interview by phone or video, using Google Meet, Zoom or Microsoft Teams. Online interviews save transport money, but they come with their own challenges. Here is how to handle them.

## Before the day

- **Test the platform.** Install the app or open the link a day early. Join a test call with a friend.
- **Check your name.** Your display name should be your real full name, not "Brayo the King".
- **Check data and power.** Buy enough bundles, charge your phone or laptop fully, and have a power bank if possible. Know where you can go if your area has a blackout.
- **Choose your spot.** A quiet room, plain background, light in front of you (facing a window), not behind you.
- **Prepare your setup.** Put your phone or laptop at eye level, on books or a box if needed. Earphones with a microphone help with sound.

> **Tip:** If your home is noisy, ask a cyber café, library or a friend's office whether you could use a quiet corner for an hour. Plan this in advance.

## During the interview

- **Join 5 minutes early** and wait in the waiting room calmly.
- **Look at the camera** when you speak, not at your own face on the screen. This feels like eye contact to the interviewer.
- **Speak a little slower** than normal. Connections can lag.
- **Mute** only if there is background noise while they talk, and remember to unmute.
- **Keep notes short.** A few keywords on a sticky note are fine; reading a script is obvious on camera.

## If something goes wrong

Technical problems happen to everyone. What matters is how you handle them.

- If the call drops, rejoin immediately using the same link.
- If you cannot rejoin, send a short message or email right away:

```text
Good morning Ms. Achieng, my connection dropped during our interview.
I am trying to rejoin now. If it is easier, I am also available on
phone at 0700 000 000. Apologies for the interruption.
Brian Mutua
```

Staying calm and solving the problem actually shows the interviewer a useful skill.

## Phone interviews

- Take the call somewhere quiet, with your CV in front of you.
- Answer professionally: "Hello, this is Wanjiku speaking."
- Smile while talking; it changes how your voice sounds.
- Since they cannot see you, say short confirmations like "Yes, I understand" so they know you are listening.

> **Warning:** A real employer may interview you online, but be careful if the whole "interview" is a text chat on Telegram or WhatsApp with someone you have never met, especially if it ends with a request for money or personal documents.

## Practice task

Set up a 15-minute mock video interview with a friend on Google Meet or Zoom. Use the checklist above. Ask them three questions from the previous lesson and then tell you honestly how your sound, light and eye contact looked.
