You got the call: "We would like to invite you for an interview on Thursday at 10 a.m." Congratulations! Your CV did its job. Now the interview decides whether you get the offer, and good preparation makes the biggest difference.

## Step 1: Confirm the details

Reply quickly and politely, and make sure you know:

- The date, time and exact location (or the video link for online interviews).
- The name and title of the person you will meet, if possible.
- What to bring: copies of your CV, certificates, ID, or a portfolio.

```text
Thank you for the invitation. I confirm I will attend the interview
on Thursday 14 March at 10:00 a.m. at your Moi Avenue office.
Kind regards, Fatuma Hassan
```

## Step 2: Research the employer

Spend at least 30 minutes learning about the company:

- What do they sell or do? Who are their customers?
- How many branches, and where?
- Any recent news, new products or community projects?

You will use this when they ask "What do you know about us?" or "Why do you want to work here?"

## Step 3: Study the job

Re-read the advert. For each requirement, think of **one example** from your life that shows you can do it. If the advert says "must be able to work under pressure", remember the time you handled a long queue at the family shop on market day.

## Step 4: Practise out loud

Write down answers to common questions (next lesson) and practise speaking them, not memorising word for word. Ask a friend or family member to act as the interviewer. Record yourself on your phone and watch it back: are you speaking clearly? Looking up? Saying "eeh" too often?

## Step 5: Plan the day

| What | Why |
|---|---|
| Lay out clean, neat clothes the night before | Less stress in the morning |
| Plan transport and leave early | Aim to arrive 10–15 minutes early; matatus can be slow |
| Have airtime/bundles and a charged phone | In case you need to call or find the location |
| Pack a folder with documents | Looks organised; keeps papers clean |
| Eat something | Hard to think clearly when hungry |

## What to wear

Dress one step smarter than the people who already do the job. For an office: a shirt and trousers or a simple dress or skirt and blouse, clean shoes. For a hotel or shop: smart casual and neat. Clothes do not need to be expensive or new; they need to be **clean, ironed and fitting**.

## On the day

- Greet the security guard and receptionist politely; sometimes they are asked their opinion.
- Switch your phone to silent.
- Offer a firm handshake if the interviewer offers one, make eye contact and smile.
- Sit when invited, sit upright, and keep your hands calm.
- It is fine to pause and think before answering.

> **Tip:** Feeling nervous is normal. It means you care. Slow breathing for a minute before you walk in helps calm your body.

## Practice task

Pick a company from your application tracker. Write one page of research notes about it: what it does, its customers, its locations and one recent piece of news. Then plan your outfit and route as if you had an interview there tomorrow.
