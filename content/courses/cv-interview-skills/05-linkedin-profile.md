**LinkedIn** is a free social network for work. Recruiters in Kenya and abroad use it to find candidates, and many companies post jobs there. Think of it as an online CV that people can find even when you are not applying.

## Why bother as a school leaver?

- Recruiters search LinkedIn for skills like "customer service" or "data entry".
- You can follow companies you would like to work for and see their job posts.
- You can connect with former classmates, teachers and people in careers you admire.
- A complete profile shows you are serious and up to date.

## The key parts of your profile

| Part | What to do |
|---|---|
| Profile photo | A clear, well-lit photo of your face, plain background, smiling. Not a group or party photo. |
| Headline | One line under your name saying what you do or want to do |
| About | 3–5 sentences, written in the first person |
| Experience | Same points as your CV, including volunteering and family business |
| Education | Secondary school, college, online courses |
| Skills | Add 5–10 relevant skills |
| Location | Your town, e.g. Kisumu, Kenya |

## Writing a headline

Your headline is the first thing people see in search results. Do not leave it as just "Student".

```text
Weak:   Student
Better: KCSE 2025 Graduate | Customer Service | Basic Excel & Canva
Better: Aspiring Accountant | CPA Student | Nairobi
```

## Writing your About section

```text
I am a recent KCSE graduate from Machakos with a strong interest in
customer service and sales. For the last two years I have helped run
my family's mobile phone accessories stall, where I learned to deal
with customers, handle M-Pesa payments and track stock. I am now
studying basic accounting online and I am looking for a sales or
front-office role where I can keep learning.
```

Notice it is friendly, specific and honest.

## Growing your network

- Connect with people you actually know first: classmates, teachers, family friends who work.
- When connecting with someone you do not know, add a short note: "Hello Mr. Mwangi, I am a school leaver interested in banking. I enjoyed your post on financial literacy and would like to follow your work."
- Follow companies such as banks, supermarkets, NGOs and tech firms you would like to join.
- Like and comment thoughtfully on posts. A good comment is better than ten empty "Congrats!"

> **Warning:** LinkedIn also has scammers. Be careful of strangers who offer you a job very quickly, ask you to move the chat to WhatsApp or Telegram, or ask for money or your ID copy. We cover scams in the next lesson: [Job Searching and Avoiding Scams](job-search-and-scams).

## Keep it consistent

Your LinkedIn and CV should tell the same story: the same dates, same job titles, same school. Employers often check both.

## Practice task

Create a LinkedIn account (or update your existing one). Add a proper profile photo, write your headline and About section, add your education and at least five skills. Then send connection requests to five people you know.
