title: The Structure of a School Leaver CV
module: 1
minutes: 10

A good CV follows a familiar order so the reader can find things fast. Here is the structure that works best when you are just starting out.

## The sections, in order

| Section | What goes in it |
|---|---|
| Contact details | Full name, phone number, professional email, town |
| Personal profile | 2–3 sentences about who you are and what you want |
| Education | Secondary school, KCSE (year and mean grade if it helps you), any college or short courses |
| Skills | Practical skills: computer, languages, customer service, driving, etc. |
| Experience | Jobs, casual work, family business, volunteering, school leadership |
| Achievements and activities | Awards, clubs, sports, competitions |
| Referees | 2–3 people who know you well, with their permission |

## Contact details

Put your name at the top in slightly larger letters. Below it, one line with your phone number, email and town.

- Use an email that sounds professional, like `brian.otieno@gmail.com`, not `sweetboy254@gmail.com`.
- You do **not** need to include your ID number, religion, tribe, marital status or a photo unless the advert asks for them.

## Personal profile

This is a short paragraph at the top that sums you up. Make it specific to the kind of job you want.

```text
Hardworking and friendly KCSE 2025 graduate with experience serving
customers in my family's retail shop in Kakamega. Comfortable with
M-Pesa transactions, basic Excel and keeping daily sales records.
Looking for a cashier or shop assistant role where I can grow.
```

Avoid empty phrases like "I am a go-getter who works well under pressure" without proof. Mention real things.

## Education

List the most recent first. For a school leaver this section comes **before** experience because it is your strongest section.

```text
EDUCATION
Kenya Certificate of Secondary Education (KCSE), 2025
Moi Girls High School, Eldoret — Mean grade: B
Strong subjects: Mathematics, Business Studies, English

Computer Packages Certificate, 2026
Skope Digital Academy (online) — Word, Excel, email, internet
```

> **Note:** If your mean grade is low, you may leave it out and list your strongest subjects instead. Never write a grade you did not get.

## Referees

Choose people who can talk about your character and work: a teacher, a pastor or imam, a former employer, a youth group leader. Avoid relatives. **Always ask them first** and tell them which jobs you are applying for so they are ready for a call.

```text
REFEREES
Mr. Peter Wanyama — Class Teacher, St. Mary's Secondary, Bungoma
0712 000 000 | pwanyama@example.com

Mrs. Grace Njeri — Owner, Njeri General Stores, Thika
0722 000 000
```

## Formatting checklist

- One simple font (Calibri, Arial or similar), size 10–12.
- Bold headings, no fancy colours or borders.
- Save and send as **PDF** named like `Achieng-Odhiambo-CV.pdf`.
- Spell-check, then ask a friend to read it aloud.

```exercise
Q: Complete the CV rules.
For school leavers, the ___ section usually comes before experience.
Always send your CV as a ___ file.
Never list a ___ without asking their permission first.
A: education | PDF | referee / referees
H: Think about your strongest section, the safest file type, and the people who vouch for you.
```

## Practice task

Open Google Docs or Microsoft Word and create the skeleton of your CV: write all seven headings, fill in your contact details, and write your education section. Then draft a three-sentence personal profile for one type of job you would like.
