Many freelancers lose clients not because their work is bad, but because their communication is. A client who feels ignored or confused will not hire you again. A client who feels informed will – and will recommend you.

## The four habits of good communication

| Habit | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| **Reply on time** | Answer messages within a few hours on working days, even if only to say "Received, I'll reply fully by 4 pm." |
| **Be clear** | Short sentences, one topic per message, numbered points for questions. |
| **Confirm in writing** | After a call, send a short summary of what you agreed. |
| **Update before being asked** | Tell the client how the work is going before they have to chase you. |

## Start every project with a clear brief

Before starting, confirm these points with the client:

- What exactly will be delivered (and in what format)?
- When is the deadline – date **and time zone**?
- How many rounds of edits are included?
- What is the price and how will payment happen?
- Who should you contact with questions?

A short confirmation message protects both of you:

```text
Hi Daniel, just confirming what we agreed:
- 10 product descriptions, 100–150 words each, in Google Docs
- Deadline: Friday 14 March, 5 pm Kenya time (EAT)
- Includes 2 rounds of edits
- Price: $30 through Upwork
I'll send the first 3 descriptions on Wednesday for your feedback.
```

## Tone: friendly and professional

Write as you would speak to a respected teacher or employer – polite, but not stiff.

| Avoid | Better |
|---|---|
| "hey boss, am through" | "Hi Daniel, the work is ready – here is the link." |
| "Why are you changing again??" | "Thanks for the feedback. Just to confirm, you'd like the headline shorter?" |
| "sorry sorry I will finish" | "I'm running about a day behind. I'll deliver by Saturday 12 pm. Sorry for the delay." |

Avoid heavy slang, too many emojis, and voice notes unless the client uses them first. Many international clients prefer written messages they can read later.

## When things go wrong

Problems happen: a power cut, a sick day, a misunderstanding. What matters is how you handle it.

1. **Tell the client early** – before the deadline, not after.
2. **Explain briefly** without long excuses.
3. **Offer a solution** – a new delivery time, or partial delivery now.
4. **Keep your promise** the second time.

> **Tip:** Under-promise and over-deliver. If you think a job takes two days, say three. Delivering early makes you look excellent; delivering late, even by a few hours, damages trust.

## Handling feedback and edits

Clients will ask for changes. That is normal – not an insult. Read the feedback carefully, ask questions if something is unclear, and make the changes. If the client asks for something **outside the agreed scope** (for example, five extra descriptions), politely explain that it is extra work and offer a price.

## Ending well

When the job is complete, deliver the final files, thank the client and, on platforms, politely invite a review: "If you're happy with the work, a short review would really help me. Thank you!"
