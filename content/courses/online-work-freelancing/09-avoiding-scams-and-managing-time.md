Two things decide whether a new freelancer lasts: staying **safe** and staying **organised**. Scammers target young people who are eager for their first job, and poor time management leads to missed deadlines and bad reviews.

## Common online job scams in Kenya

| Scam | How it looks | The truth |
|---|---|---|
| **Registration or "activation" fee** | "Pay KES 1,000 to unlock your writing account." | Real employers do not charge you to work. |
| **Account selling or renting** | "Buy a verified Upwork account with reviews." | Against platform rules; accounts get closed and money frozen. |
| **Fake cheque or overpayment** | Client "overpays" and asks you to send back the difference. | The original payment is fake or reversed; you lose what you sent. |
| **Task / "like and earn" apps** | Earn by liking videos – but first deposit money to "upgrade". | You may get a small payout at first, then lose your deposit. |
| **Fake support agent** | "Your account is suspended. Send the code we've sent you." | Platforms and Safaricom never ask for your PIN or OTP. |
| **Free test that never ends** | Client asks for a big "test" piece for free, then disappears. | Real tests are small. Large work must be paid. |

### Red flags checklist

- You are asked to **pay before you earn**.
- The pay sounds **too good** for very little work.
- The "client" pushes you to move **off the platform** immediately.
- They ask for your **password, PIN, OTP or ID photos** over chat.
- Spelling is poor, the company has no real website, or the email is a free address claiming to be a big company.
- You feel **rushed**: "Pay in the next 30 minutes or lose the slot."

> **Warning:** Never send your M-Pesa PIN, bank PIN or a one-time code to anyone. If you are scammed, report it immediately to the platform, your bank or Safaricom through their official channels, and to the police. The sooner you report, the better the chance of help.

## Managing your time

Freelancing gives you freedom – which can easily turn into wasted days. Treat it like a job.

### Plan your week

Use a simple weekly plan in Google Calendar or a notebook.

| Time | Monday | Tuesday | Wednesday |
|---|---|---|---|
| 8–10 am | Client work | Client work | Client work |
| 10–11 am | Proposals | Learning | Proposals |
| 2–4 pm | Client work | Portfolio | Client work |
| 4–5 pm | Messages & admin | Messages & admin | Messages & admin |

### Useful habits

- **Work in focused blocks.** For example, 25–45 minutes of focus, then a short break. Put your phone face-down.
- **Do the hardest task first** while your mind is fresh.
- **Keep a deadline list** with every job, its due date and its time zone.
- **Plan for power and internet problems.** Keep your laptop and phone charged, have a backup data bundle, and save work to Google Drive often.
- **Don't overbook.** Taking three urgent jobs at once leads to three unhappy clients.
- **Rest.** Burnout is real; protect your sleep.

> **Tip:** Set your working hours and tell clients. "I'm available 8 am – 6 pm Kenya time, Monday to Saturday" is professional and protects your evenings.

Kiprono kept missing deadlines because he worked late at night and slept through mornings. He set fixed hours (8 am – 4 pm), blocked time for proposals, and added each deadline to Google Calendar with a reminder one day before. He has not missed a deadline since.

## Practice task

Make a **weekly plan** for next week with fixed blocks for client work (or practice projects), proposals, learning and rest. Then write the red flags checklist on paper or your phone notes, so you can check every new job offer against it before replying.
