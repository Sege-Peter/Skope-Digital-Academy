Finishing the work is only half the job – you also need the money to reach you safely. Kenyan freelancers usually receive money through one of three routes: a **bank account**, an **online wallet such as PayPal**, or **M-Pesa**, sometimes in combination.

## The main options

| Option | How it usually works | Things to know |
|---|---|---|
| **Bank account** | Many platforms let you withdraw directly to a Kenyan bank account, or via a payment service. | You may need your bank's SWIFT code and account details. Banks may charge for receiving international money. |
| **PayPal** | Clients or platforms send money to your PayPal account; you then withdraw it. | Safaricom has offered a service linking PayPal and M-Pesa. Check safaricom.co.ke for whether it is available and what it costs today. |
| **Payment services** (e.g. Payoneer, Wise) | Give you account details for receiving international payments, then you withdraw to a local bank. | Check which services your platform supports and their current fees. |
| **M-Pesa directly** | Local clients pay you by Send Money, Till or Paybill. | Simple and fast for Kenyan clients. Keep records of every payment. |

> **Note:** Which withdrawal methods are available depends on the platform and changes over time. Before you accept your first job, open the platform's "Get Paid" or "Withdraw" page and confirm a method that works for you in Kenya.

## Setting up safely

1. **Use your real name everywhere.** The name on your freelancing profile, your bank, PayPal and M-Pesa should match your ID. Mismatched names are one of the most common reasons withdrawals get stuck.
2. **Verify early.** Platforms and payment services may ask for your ID, a selfie or proof of address. Do this before you have money waiting.
3. **Turn on two-step verification** for your email, platform and payment accounts.
4. **Never share** your passwords, PINs or one-time codes (OTPs) with anyone – not a "client", not a "support agent", not a friend.

> **Warning:** A common scam: someone "accidentally" sends you money, or sends a fake payment message, and then asks you to refund part of it. Always check your **real balance** in the official app – never trust an SMS screenshot.

## Getting paid by local clients

For Kenyan clients you will often be paid via M-Pesa. Protect yourself:

- **Agree the price in writing** (WhatsApp or email) before starting.
- **Ask for a deposit** for bigger jobs – for example half upfront and half on delivery.
- **Send a watermarked preview** or a low-resolution version before final payment for design work.
- **Confirm the payment in your M-Pesa messages from MPESA** or in the M-PESA app, not from a forwarded screenshot.

## Keep records and think about tax

Keep a simple record of every payment: date, client, job, amount and method. A spreadsheet in Google Sheets works well.

| Date | Client | Job | Amount | Method |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5 Mar | Grace (Upwork) | Typing 20 pages | $40 | Upwork → bank |
| 9 Mar | Salon, Kisii | 2 posters | KES 1,500 | M-Pesa |

Income from freelancing can be taxable in Kenya. You will need a **KRA PIN**, and the rules for who must file and how much is due can change. Check the current guidance on **kra.go.ke** or ask a qualified accountant – do not rely on rumours.

## Exchange rates

Clients abroad pay in US dollars or other currencies. When the money is converted to shillings, the rate used by the platform, bank or service may differ from the rate you see on the news. Compare what actually lands in your account, not just the headline rate.
