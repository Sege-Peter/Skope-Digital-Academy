Online work means doing a job for someone using a computer or phone and the internet, instead of going to their office. Your client could be a shop in Kisumu, a school in Nakuru or a small company in London. You never have to meet them in person.

A **freelancer** is someone who works for different clients, one project at a time, instead of being employed by one company. You decide which jobs to take, how much to charge and when to work. In return, nobody pays you a salary at the end of the month – you only earn when you finish work for a client.

## Common kinds of online work

Most online jobs fall into a few groups. You do not need to know all of them – you only need one skill to start.

| Kind of work | Examples of tasks | Skill you need |
|---|---|---|
| Writing | Blog articles, product descriptions, social media captions | Good English, research |
| Design | Posters, logos, flyers, social media graphics | Canva or other design tools |
| Admin / Virtual assistant | Email, scheduling, data entry, research | Organisation, Google tools |
| Customer support | Answering chats and emails for a business | Patience, clear writing |
| Transcription | Typing what is said in audio or video | Fast, accurate typing |
| Social media management | Planning and posting content for a business | Creativity, consistency |
| Tech | Building websites, fixing small bugs, setting up tools | Coding or technical skill |

> **Note:** Some online work is *remote employment* – a normal job with a fixed salary, done from home. Freelancing is different: you are your own small business.

## What freelancing is really like

Freelancing sounds easy on social media: "Work from your bed and earn dollars!" The truth is more balanced.

**The good side**
- You can start with a skill you already have.
- You can work from anywhere with power and internet.
- You can earn from clients outside Kenya.
- Your income can grow as your skill and reputation grow.

**The hard side**
- The first client usually takes the longest to get – often weeks of applying.
- Income goes up and down from month to month.
- You must manage your own time, tools and money.
- There are many scammers who target beginners.

> **Warning:** A real client pays *you*. If anyone asks you to pay a "registration fee", "training fee" or "account activation fee" before you can start a job, it is almost certainly a scam.

## Meet Achieng

Achieng finished Form Four in Homa Bay. She types fast and writes neat English. She starts by offering short product descriptions to small online shops on WhatsApp and Instagram. After a few small jobs, she has real samples to show, and she uses them to apply for bigger writing jobs on freelancing platforms. She did not start with an expensive course or laptop – she started with one skill and a phone, and later saved up for a second-hand laptop.

Achieng's story shows the usual path: **one skill → small first jobs → samples → bigger clients**.

## What you need to start

- A **skill** someone will pay for (even a basic one).
- A **device** – a laptop is best, but many people start on a smartphone.
- Reliable **internet** – a data bundle or Wi-Fi.
- An **email address** that looks professional, like `achieng.writes@gmail.com`, not `sweetgal2006@...`.
- **Patience** – expect the first weeks to be slow.
