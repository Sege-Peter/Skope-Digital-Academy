## The situation

You have decided to sell one skill online – for example writing, data entry, simple graphic design, transcription or social media help. Before you send a single proposal, a client will judge you on three things: your **profile**, your **samples** and the **message** you send them. In this project you will put all three together into one "starter kit" that you can use for real after the course.

> **Note:** Use made-up details wherever privacy matters. Do **not** include your real phone number, ID number, M-Pesa number, email password or home address. A first name and town (e.g. "Achieng, Kisumu") is enough.

## What you will produce

A single document (typed in the text box, or uploaded as a PDF or Word file) with four parts:

1. **Your skill and client** – one paragraph: the one skill you will start with, and the type of client who needs it (e.g. "small shops in Nakuru that want better Facebook posts").
2. **Your profile** – a headline (one line, under 12 words) and an overview of 120–200 words written for that client, not about yourself.
3. **Your portfolio plan** – three sample pieces. For each: a title, what the client problem was (it can be imagined), and what you made. If you have already made a sample, add a link to it (Google Drive, Canva, a free portfolio site).
4. **One proposal** – a short proposal (80–150 words) answering this made-up job post:

```text
Job: Need help with weekly social media captions
We run a small juice bar in Thika. We post on Instagram and Facebook 3 times a week
but our captions are boring. Looking for someone to write 12 captions for next month.
Please tell us how you would approach this.
```

## Steps

1. Re-read the lessons on profiles, portfolios and proposals.
2. Pick **one** skill. Beginners who pick one skill get replies faster than those who list ten.
3. Write your headline and overview. Read them aloud – would a busy client understand them in 20 seconds?
4. Plan (or make) your three samples.
5. Write the proposal. Mention the juice bar's problem, how you would solve it and a question for the client.
6. Check spelling, then submit.

## What to submit

- Your starter kit in the text box **or** as a PDF/Word file (max 5 MB).
- Optional: a link to your portfolio or a sample.

## Tips

- Talk about the client's problem first, your experience second.
- Short, specific sentences beat long lists of adjectives like "hardworking, passionate, dedicated".
- Do not promise results you can't control ("I will get you 10,000 followers").
- Never quote prices or fees as facts in your kit – you can say you will agree a price after understanding the work.

```rubric
Skill and client focus | One clear skill and a specific type of client; everything else in the kit matches that choice | 4
Profile headline and overview | Headline is short and specific; overview speaks to the client's needs, is 120–200 words and free of filler words | 5
Portfolio plan | Three relevant samples, each with a problem and what was made; links work if given | 5
Proposal | Answers the juice bar's actual request, explains an approach, asks a useful question, 80–150 words | 4
Presentation | Clear headings, correct spelling and grammar, no real personal data shared | 2
```
