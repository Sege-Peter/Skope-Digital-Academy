Your profile is your shop window. A client who has never met you will decide in a few seconds whether to read more or move on. A good profile answers three questions quickly: **What do you do? Who do you help? Why should I trust you?**

## The parts of a strong profile

### 1. Photo

Use a clear, recent photo of your face, looking at the camera, with a plain background and good light (stand facing a window). Smile. No sunglasses, group photos, cartoons or logos.

### 2. Headline

The headline is one line under your name. Make it specific.

| Weak headline | Strong headline |
|---|---|
| Hardworking freelancer | Product Description Writer for Online Shops |
| I can do anything | Canva Poster & Flyer Designer |
| Student looking for work | Virtual Assistant – Email, Scheduling, Google Sheets |

A specific headline helps the right client find you. "I can do anything" makes clients think you are not good at any one thing.

### 3. Overview (About section)

Write it for the client, not about your feelings. Start with the client's problem, then show how you solve it.

```text
Running an online shop is busy. Good product descriptions help
customers decide to buy – but writing them takes time.

I write clear, friendly product descriptions for clothing,
beauty and electronics shops. I research each product, write
in simple English, and deliver on time in Google Docs.

What you get:
- Descriptions of 80–150 words per product
- Two rounds of edits
- Delivery within 48 hours for up to 10 products

Send me a message with one product link and I will show you
how I would describe it.
```

Notice: short paragraphs, a clear list, and an invitation to act at the end.

### 4. Skills and tags

Platforms let you add skills or tags. Choose the ones clients actually search for, such as "data entry", "Canva", "copywriting", "Google Sheets". Only list skills you can really deliver.

### 5. Portfolio and samples

We cover this in the next lesson, [Creating a Simple Portfolio](creating-a-portfolio). A profile with real samples gets far more trust than one without.

## Honesty matters

> **Warning:** Never invent reviews, fake certificates, or claim experience you do not have. Platforms check, clients check, and one lie can end your account. It is better to say "I am new, so I offer quick delivery and careful work" than to pretend.

You also need to verify your identity on most platforms. Use your **real name** and your own national ID or passport. The name on your profile should match the name on your payment account, or you may have trouble withdrawing money later.

## Fatuma's before and after

Fatuma first wrote: *"Hello, I am Fatuma, 19 years old from Mombasa. I need work please. I am hardworking."*

She changed it to: *"I design eye-catching posters and social media graphics for small businesses using Canva. Fast delivery, unlimited small edits, and files ready for WhatsApp, Instagram and printing."*

The second version talks about the client's needs, names the tool and lists clear benefits. Fatuma also added three poster samples she had made for her aunt's salon.

## Practice task

Write your **headline** and a **five-line overview** for the skill you chose in the first lesson. Read it aloud. Remove any sentence that is about what *you* need ("I need work") and replace it with what the *client* gets. Ask a friend which version sounds more trustworthy.
