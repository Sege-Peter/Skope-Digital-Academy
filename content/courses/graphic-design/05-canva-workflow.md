**Canva** is a browser and mobile design tool with templates, photos, fonts and elements. It has a free plan and paid plans with extra features (check canva.com for current plans). Many paid designers in Kenya use Canva for social media, flyers and presentations because it is fast. Using it **professionally** means more than picking a template and changing the words.

> **Note:** Canva updates its menus and features often. This lesson describes what to do rather than exact button positions. If something moves, use Canva's search bar or help centre.

## Step 1: Start with the brief

Before opening Canva, get a clear **brief** from the client:

```text
Client:      Wanjiru's Salon, Nyeri
Purpose:     Advertise Tuesday student discount
Where:       Instagram post + WhatsApp Status
Must include: Logo, "Student Tuesday", conditions, location, phone number
Deadline:    Friday 5pm
Brand:       Purple #5b2a86, gold #d4a537, font Montserrat
```

Many problems with clients come from a missing brief. Write it down and confirm it with them.

## Step 2: Choose the right size

Create the design at the correct size for where it will be used (you will learn common sizes in [File Formats and Sizes](file-formats-and-sizes)). Canva offers ready-made sizes for common uses and also lets you enter custom dimensions.

## Step 3: Use templates wisely

Templates are a starting point, not the finished product. To make a template your own:

- Replace the colours with the client's brand colours.
- Replace fonts with the brand fonts.
- Use the client's own photos where possible.
- Rearrange the layout so it follows the hierarchy of **this** message.
- Remove decorations that do not help the message.

Many people use the same popular templates. If your design looks exactly like a template others will recognise, it looks less professional.

## Step 4: Set up a brand kit

Canva has a feature for saving brand colours, fonts and logos (availability depends on your plan). If you cannot use it, keep a simple text note per client with their HEX codes and fonts. This keeps all designs consistent.

## Step 5: Work in an organised way

- **Name your designs** clearly, e.g. `Wanjiru-Salon_Student-Tuesday_IG_v1`.
- Use **folders** per client.
- **Duplicate** a design before making big changes, so you can go back.
- **Group** elements that belong together and **lock** backgrounds so you do not move them by mistake.
- Use **alignment guides** and the position tools to align precisely.

## Step 6: Check before sending

Run a quick checklist:

1. Spelling, phone numbers, dates and prices are correct.
2. Text is readable on a phone screen (zoom out to check).
3. Contrast, alignment, hierarchy and whitespace are good.
4. Logo is clear and not stretched.
5. Nothing important is too close to the edges.

## Step 7: Share for feedback and export

Share a view link or a low-quality preview for approval first. After approval, export the final file in the correct format. Agree beforehand how many rounds of changes (**revisions**) are included in the price.

> **Warning:** Only use photos, fonts and elements that you have the right to use for commercial work. Canva's licence terms explain what is allowed – for example, there are limits on using some stock elements inside a logo. Read the current terms on canva.com.

## Practice task

Write a full brief for your practice client using the template in Step 1. Then create the design in Canva, following steps 2–6. Save it with a proper file name, export it, and keep both the brief and the design for your portfolio.
