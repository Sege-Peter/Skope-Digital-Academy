Clients rarely ask for a certificate first. They ask: **"Can I see your work?"** A **portfolio** is a collection of your best designs that proves what you can do. With a good portfolio and a professional approach, you can start earning from design.

## What to put in your portfolio

- **6–12 of your best pieces**, not everything you have ever made. Quality beats quantity.
- **Variety**: social media posts, a flyer or poster, a logo with its brand guide, maybe a menu or business card.
- **Real or realistic projects**. If you have no clients yet, create projects for real local businesses (as practice, clearly labelled as "concept" or "personal project"), or redesign a poor poster you have seen.
- **Short case studies** for your best 2–3 pieces: the problem, your process (sketches), and the final result.

```text
Case study: Shamba Fresh Eggs – logo and launch posts (concept project)
Problem: New egg supplier in Kiambu needed a simple, trustworthy brand.
Process: 12 sketches -> 3 digital options -> chose leaf icon + bold wordmark.
Result:  Logo in 4 versions, brand guide, 3 launch posts for Instagram.
```

## Where to show it

| Place | Good for |
|---|---|
| Instagram or Facebook page for your design work | Local clients who already use social media |
| Behance (free portfolio site) | Looking professional, global audience |
| A PDF portfolio (5–10 pages) | Sending to clients on WhatsApp or email |
| WhatsApp catalogue | Showing your packages and samples |
| LinkedIn | Corporate clients, NGOs, jobs |

## Finding clients

1. **Start local.** Salons, shops, churches, schools, restaurants, youth groups and event organisers all need designs.
2. **Show, don't just tell.** Make a free sample post for a business you want to work with and show them (keep it as portfolio work even if they say no).
3. **Use freelancing platforms** such as Fiverr and Upwork once your portfolio is ready. Expect competition; a niche helps (for example church event posters or restaurant menus).
4. **Ask happy clients for referrals and reviews.**

## Pricing your work

Prices depend on your skill, the client, the job's complexity and your town. Common ways to price:

- **Per design** – e.g. one poster or one post.
- **Packages** – e.g. a set of social media posts per month.
- **Per project** – e.g. logo plus brand guide.

As an illustration only, beginners often start with modest prices for single social media designs and charge noticeably more for logos, because logos involve research, several concepts and multiple file versions. Look at what other designers in your area and on platforms charge, start fair, and raise prices as your portfolio and reviews grow.

## Working professionally

- **Agree in writing**: what you will deliver, the formats, deadline, price and number of revisions.
- **Deposit**: ask for part of the payment upfront for bigger jobs, the rest on approval before you send the final files.
- **Watermark or low-quality previews** until payment is complete.
- **Communicate**: reply on time and give honest deadlines.
- **Keep records** of payments received through M-Pesa.

> **Warning:** Be careful of "design jobs" that require you to pay a registration fee, and of "clients" who want you to log in to accounts using codes they send. Never share your M-Pesa PIN or passwords. If an offer sounds too good to be true, it probably is.
