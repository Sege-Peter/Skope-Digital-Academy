Social media graphics are the most common paid design job for beginners. Businesses need them every week: offers, new stock, events, announcements, tips and greetings for holidays. A good post must stop the scroll, deliver the message in a few seconds and tell people what to do.

## The 3-second test

People scroll fast. Ask yourself: if someone sees this post for three seconds, will they know **what** it is about, **who** it is from and **what to do**? If not, simplify.

## Anatomy of a strong post

| Part | Role | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Visual focus | Stops the scroll | Big, clear product photo or bold headline |
| Headline | The main message in a few words | "School shoes – all sizes" |
| Supporting info | Only key details | Price, date, location |
| Call to action | What to do next | "WhatsApp 07XX XXX XXX" |
| Branding | Who it is from | Logo and brand colours, small and consistent |

## Keep text short

Put the **headline** and essential details on the image, and the longer explanation in the **caption**. A post covered with paragraphs of tiny text gets ignored. As a rule of thumb, if you need more than about 20 words on the image, consider a carousel (several slides) instead.

## Post types and design tips

- **Product post** – one product, clean background, price clearly visible.
- **Offer post** – the offer is the hero ("Buy 2, get 1 free"), conditions in smaller text.
- **Event post** – event name, date, time, venue, how to register.
- **Tips / educational post** – a numbered list across a carousel; the first slide must make people want to swipe.
- **Testimonial post** – a short customer quote, their first name and town (with permission).
- **Holiday greeting** – e.g. Jamhuri Day or Eid; keep the brand visible but the message warm, not salesy.

## Mobile first

Almost everyone will see your post on a phone. Check:

- Text size is readable without zooming.
- Important content is near the centre, because some apps crop the edges in previews and profile grids.
- High contrast so the post works in bright sunlight.

## Consistency builds a brand

When a business's posts use the same colours, fonts, logo position and photo style, followers recognise them instantly. Create a small **template set** for each client: one for offers, one for events, one for tips. Then each new post is quicker to make and the feed looks professional.

```text
Template set for "Mama Pendo Cafe"
- Colours: deep green #1f5f3f, cream #f7f1e3, orange accent #e67e22
- Fonts: Poppins Bold (headings), Poppins Regular (details)
- Logo: bottom right, small
- Photo style: warm, natural light, wooden table background
```

## Photos

- Use real photos of the client's products where possible; customers trust them more.
- Remove clutter from the background before taking the photo.
- Do not use photos from Google search – they usually belong to someone else.
- Get permission before using photos of customers, and be extra careful with children.

## Practice task

Create a **template set** of three posts in Canva for your practice business: an offer post, an event or announcement post, and a tips carousel (at least 3 slides). Use the same colours, fonts and logo position in all three. Apply the 3-second test to each before you finish.
