A **brand** is how people recognise and feel about a business. A **logo** is just one part of it. Branding also includes colours, fonts, photo style, tone of voice and how the business treats customers. As a designer, your job is to make the brand look consistent everywhere – signboard, receipts, social media and uniforms.

## What makes a good logo?

A good logo is:

- **Simple** – easy to recognise at a glance and easy to remember.
- **Readable when small** – it must work as a WhatsApp profile picture and on a business card.
- **Works in one colour** – for stamps, embroidery, photocopies and engraving.
- **Appropriate** – a law firm logo should not look like a nursery school logo.
- **Original** – not copied from another business.

## Types of logos

| Type | Description | Example idea |
|---|---|---|
| **Wordmark** | The name in a styled font | "AchiengTech" in bold custom lettering |
| **Lettermark** | Initials only | "KMC" for Kisumu Motor Centre |
| **Icon / symbol** | A picture or shape | A leaf for an organic farm |
| **Combination** | Icon plus name | A cup icon next to "Mama Pendo Cafe" |
| **Emblem** | Name inside a badge or shape | School crests, sports clubs |

For most small businesses, a **combination mark** works well: the full logo for signboards, and the icon alone for small spaces like profile pictures.

## A simple logo process

1. **Research** – ask the owner about the business, customers, competitors and values. Look at competitors' logos to avoid looking the same.
2. **Sketch** – draw 10 or more quick ideas on paper. Most first ideas are not the best.
3. **Choose 2–3** strong ideas and create them digitally in black and white first. If it works in black and white, colour will only make it better.
4. **Add colour and font** – choose from the palette and typography rules you learned.
5. **Test** – view it very small and very large, on light and dark backgrounds, and in one colour.
6. **Present** – show the logo on mock-ups (a shop sign, a T-shirt, a phone screen) so the client can imagine it in use.

## The brand guide

After the logo is approved, give the client a short **brand guide** (one or two pages): the logo versions, colour HEX codes, fonts, and simple do's and don'ts (e.g. "do not stretch the logo", "keep space around the logo"). This helps anyone who designs for them later.

## Originality and the law

Never copy another company's logo or use famous brand logos in your client's design. Copying can lead to legal problems over trademarks and damages your reputation. A business that wants to protect its logo can register a trademark – in Kenya this is handled by the Kenya Industrial Property Institute (KIPI); direct clients to kipi.go.ke for the current process. Be careful with stock icons too: some licences do not allow them to be used as part of a logo.

## Practice task

Choose a business with no logo (or a weak one). Follow steps 1–5: write 3 research notes, sketch 10 ideas on paper, develop your best two in black and white in Canva, then add colour. Test them at a small size. Write a one-page brand guide for the final version.
