Hongera! In four weeks you went from your first `console.log` to a searchable product catalogue. Let us look back, plan what comes next, and then take the final quiz.

## What you built

| Week | You learned | Project |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | variables, maths, if/else, functions | bill-splitting logic |
| 2 | the DOM, events, forms, validation | counter app, tip calculator |
| 3 | arrays, objects, loops, localStorage, JSON | to-do list that remembers |
| 4 | putting it all together | quiz app, product catalogue |

The most important idea in the whole course is one pattern: **keep your data in variables and arrays, and write one `render()` function that draws the page from that data.** Every button or input only changes the data and calls `render()`. Popular tools like React are built on this same idea, so you are already thinking like a modern front-end developer.

## Build your portfolio

Clients and employers want to *see* what you can do. Put your best three projects online for free:

1. Create a free account on **GitHub** and upload your `.html` files to a repository.
2. Turn on **GitHub Pages** in the repository settings to get a free public link.
3. Share the links on your CV, LinkedIn profile and WhatsApp status.

For each project, write two sentences: what problem it solves and what you used to build it. For example: *"A product catalogue for a phone shop with live search and WhatsApp ordering, built with HTML, CSS and JavaScript."*

## Ways beginners earn with JavaScript

- **Small business sites:** catalogues, price lists, booking or enquiry forms.
- **School and church tools:** quizzes, timetables, event countdowns.
- **Freelancing:** small fixes and features on platforms like Upwork and Fiverr once you have a portfolio.
- **Internships and junior roles:** many start with HTML, CSS and JavaScript.

Income varies a lot with skill, speed and the client, so start with small paid jobs and raise your prices as your portfolio grows.

> **Warning:** Be careful with "jobs" that ask you to pay a registration or training fee first, or ask for your M-Pesa PIN or account passwords. Genuine clients pay you; they do not charge you to start work.

## What to learn next

- **Git and GitHub** properly, to manage your code versions.
- **fetch()** to load data from real APIs (weather, exchange rates).
- **A framework** such as React or Vue once plain JavaScript feels comfortable.
- **A back end** (Node.js, PHP or Python) so your apps can save data on a server for every user.

Keep building. One small project a week will teach you more than reading ten tutorials.
