You can now plan, build, style and publish a website for a small business. Many businesses around you, from salons in Thika to hardware shops in Kisii, still have no website, or have one that looks broken on a phone. In this final lesson you will learn how to turn your skills into paid work, then take the course quiz.

## Start with a portfolio

Clients want to see examples before they pay. Build two or three sample sites:

- Your Baraka Bakery (or own-business) project from this course.
- A one-page site for a real business you know, built with the owner's permission (some beginners do the first one free or at a small fee in exchange for a testimonial).
- A simple personal site about you, with links to the others.

For each, show a screenshot, the live link, and one sentence on the result: "A one-page site so customers can order cakes on WhatsApp."

## Packaging your services

Clear packages are easier to sell than "I build websites". For example:

| Package | What's included |
|---|---|
| Starter one-page site | One responsive page, WhatsApp and call buttons, map, up to 6 photos |
| Business site | Up to 5 pages, contact form, Google Business Profile setup |
| Monthly care | Small text and photo updates, checking the site works, renewal reminders |

Pricing depends on your skill, the client and your town, so research what others charge locally and online, and start modestly while your portfolio grows. Always state separately what the client pays for **domain and hosting**, so there are no surprises.

> **Tip:** Monthly care plans turn one-off projects into regular income. Even a small monthly fee from several clients adds up.

## Finding clients

- **Walk in:** visit businesses on your street with your phone ready to show a sample site. Show the owner how their competitor or a similar business looks online.
- **Your network:** churches, chamas, schools, relatives' businesses, former teachers.
- **Social media:** post before-and-after screenshots on WhatsApp Status, Facebook, Instagram and LinkedIn.
- **Online platforms:** Fiverr and Upwork have many small web design jobs; start with focused offers such as "Responsive one-page website for a small business".

## A simple proposal

Before starting, send a short written proposal (WhatsApp or email is fine) and get a "yes" in writing:

```text
Hi Mama Akinyi,

Here is the plan for the Baraka Bakery website:
- One-page mobile-friendly site: menu, cakes gallery, about, contact
- WhatsApp order button and Google Maps location
- 2 rounds of changes included
- Ready 10 days after I receive your photos and price list

Price: KES ____ (50% deposit to start, 50% on launch)
Domain and hosting: paid separately, registered in your name.

Kind regards,
Wanjiru
```

## Getting paid safely

- Take a **deposit** before you start, and the balance before handing over full control.
- Accept payment through M-Pesa (a till or your personal number) or bank transfer, and send a simple receipt.
- Agree on the number of revision rounds, so a project doesn't go on forever.

> **Warning:** Be careful with "clients" who overpay and ask you to send back the extra money, who send payment screenshots that never appear in your M-Pesa messages, or who ask for your PIN to "send" payment. Check your actual M-Pesa balance or statement, and never share your PIN.

## Course wrap-up

You planned a site around one business goal, built it with semantic HTML, styled it with CSS, chose readable colours and fonts, laid it out with Flexbox, made it work on phones, optimised images, added an accessible form and published it online. That is the full workflow of a junior web designer. Keep improving one project at a time and keep showing your work.
