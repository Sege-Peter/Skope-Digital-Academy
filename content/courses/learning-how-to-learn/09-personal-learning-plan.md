You now have a toolbox: active recall, spaced repetition, focused and relaxed thinking, good notes, Pomodoros and motivation habits. In this lesson you will put them together into a **personal learning plan** for a real skill you want to earn from.

## Step 1: Choose one skill

Trying to learn five things at once usually means finishing none. Pick **one** main skill for the next 4–8 weeks. Ask:

- Is there demand for it around me or online? (Look at job adverts, ask local businesses.)
- Can I practise it with what I have (phone, laptop, tools)?
- Do I enjoy it, or at least find it interesting?

Examples: graphic design with Canva, basic bookkeeping, Excel, digital marketing, coding, photography, tailoring, phone repair.

## Step 2: Set a clear goal

Make it specific and measurable, with a date.

```text
Vague:  "Learn Excel."
Clear:  "By 31 March, I can build a monthly budget and a simple sales
         tracker in Excel using SUM, AVERAGE and IF, and I have shown
         it to two small businesses in Kisumu."
```

## Step 3: Find your resources

List where you will learn from: a structured course, a book, YouTube channels, a mentor or someone already doing the work. One main course plus one or two extras is enough.

## Step 4: Plan your weekly routine

Be realistic. If you work or help at home, maybe you have one hour on weekdays and three on Saturday.

| Day | Time | Activity | Method |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mon–Fri | 6:30–7:20 a.m. | 2 Pomodoros: new lesson + exercises | Focus, notes (Cornell) |
| Mon–Fri | On the matatu | 10 min flashcards | Active recall, spacing |
| Saturday | 9:00 a.m.–12:00 p.m. | Mini project | Practice, building |
| Sunday | 30 min | Weekly review: test myself on the week | Active recall |

## Step 5: Build in practice and projects

Plan at least one real project you can show people: a poster for a local shop, a budget sheet for your chama, a simple website. Projects prove your skill to employers and clients far better than certificates alone.

## Step 6: Track and review

Every Sunday, ask yourself:

1. What did I learn this week?
2. What did I find hard? What will I do about it?
3. Did I follow my routine? If not, what got in the way?
4. What is my small goal for next week?

Adjust the plan as needed. A plan that changes is better than a perfect plan you abandon.

## A complete sample plan

```text
MY LEARNING PLAN – Kiprono, Eldoret

Skill:     Graphic design with Canva
Why:       Earn money designing for small businesses and churches
Goal:      By 30 April, design 10 portfolio pieces and get 1 paid job
Resources: Skope Digital Academy design course, Canva's own tutorials
Routine:   Weekdays 6:30–7:20 a.m. (2 Pomodoros); Saturday 3 hours
Recall:    Flashcards on design terms, 10 min daily in Anki
Projects:  Wk 2 church poster, Wk 4 logo for uncle's shop,
           Wk 6 social media set for a salon
Review:    Sunday evenings, 30 minutes
Buddy:     Wanjiku (same course), check-in every Friday
```

> **Tip:** Share your plan with someone who will ask you about your progress. Being accountable to another person makes you more likely to follow through.

## Practice task

Write your own learning plan using the sample format: skill, why, goal with a date, resources, routine, recall method, projects, review time and a buddy. Put your study sessions for the next two weeks into your phone calendar with reminders.
