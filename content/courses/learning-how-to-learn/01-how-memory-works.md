After school, learning does not stop; it becomes your responsibility. Whether you want to master Excel, graphic design, a trade, a driving test or a college course, the people who earn more are usually the people who keep learning. This course teaches you **how** to learn, so every other skill becomes easier.

## Learning is a skill too

Many people believe they are "just not clever" at maths, or "bad at remembering". Usually the problem is not their brain but the **methods** they were never taught. Reading notes over and over the night before an exam is one of the least effective ways to learn, yet it is what most of us do.

The good news: your brain changes when you learn. Each time you practise something, the connections in your brain for that skill get stronger. Ability grows with the right kind of practice.

## Memory in plain terms

Think of memory in three simple stages:

| Stage | What happens | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Taking in | You pay attention to new information | Watching a video on Excel formulas |
| Holding briefly | Your **working memory** holds a few things for a short time | Keeping a phone number in your head while you dial |
| Storing | Information moves into **long-term memory** | Remembering how to ride a bicycle years later |

**Working memory** is small. It can only juggle a handful of things at once, which is why learning gets hard when you try to take in too much at the same time.

**Long-term memory** is huge, like a big store. The challenge is getting things into it, and being able to find them again later.

## Why we forget

Forgetting is normal. Without review, a large part of what we learn fades within days. Psychologists have studied this for over a century, and the pattern is well known: we forget quickly at first, then more slowly.

What helps memories last:

- **Attention**: you cannot remember what you never really noticed.
- **Meaning**: connecting new ideas to things you already know.
- **Practice pulling it out**: testing yourself, not just re-reading.
- **Review over time**: coming back to it on several days.
- **Sleep**: your brain strengthens memories while you sleep.

We will look at each of these through the course.

## Make it meaningful

New information sticks better when you link it to something familiar. Learning that a spreadsheet cell reference like `B3` means "column B, row 3"? Picture seats in a matatu: row and seat number. Learning about profit? Think of a mama mboga's daily sales minus what she paid at the market.

> **Tip:** Ask yourself "What does this remind me of?" whenever you learn something new.

## Practice task

Think of one skill you learned well in the past, such as riding a bicycle, cooking chapati, playing a game, or a song you know by heart. Write down **how** you learned it: how often you practised, whether someone corrected you, and how long it took. Notice which of the "what helps" points above were part of it.
