You now know that testing yourself beats re-reading. The next question is **when** to test yourself. The answer is: spread it out over time. This is called **spaced repetition**, and together with active recall it is one of the most reliable ways to remember things for the long term.

## Cramming versus spacing

Fatuma and Kiprono both spend three hours learning 60 Excel shortcuts and terms.

| | Fatuma (crams) | Kiprono (spaces) |
|---|---|---|
| Plan | 3 hours the night before the test | 30 minutes on six different days |
| Test result | Good | Good |
| One month later | Remembers very little | Remembers most of it |

Cramming can get you through tomorrow's test, but the knowledge fades quickly. Spacing uses the same total time and gives you knowledge that lasts. For skills you want to **earn money from**, lasting knowledge is what matters.

## Why spacing works

Each time you review something just as you are starting to forget it, your brain has to work a little to recall it. That effort strengthens the memory, and the next time it fades more slowly. So the gaps between reviews can get longer and longer.

## A simple spacing schedule

There is no single perfect schedule. A practical one for beginners:

| Review | When |
|---|---|
| 1st | The same day you learn it (or the next day) |
| 2nd | About 2–3 days later |
| 3rd | About 1 week later |
| 4th | About 2–3 weeks later |
| 5th | About 1–2 months later |

If you get something wrong during a review, bring it back to a short gap again.

## The box method with paper flashcards

You do not need an app. Use three envelopes or boxes:

- **Box 1:** new cards and cards you got wrong. Review **every day**.
- **Box 2:** cards you got right once. Review **every few days**.
- **Box 3:** cards you got right twice in a row. Review **once a week**.

When you get a card right, move it up one box. When you get it wrong, move it back to Box 1.

## Apps that do the spacing for you

Free flashcard apps like **Anki** schedule the reviews automatically based on how well you remember each card. You just open the app each day and answer what it shows you. A few minutes on the matatu or during a break adds up.

> **Tip:** Little and often beats long and rare. Fifteen minutes every day is better than two hours once a week.

## Mixing topics

When practising, mixing different types of problems (called **interleaving**) often helps more than doing one type many times in a row. For example, mix questions on SUM, AVERAGE and IF in Excel instead of doing twenty SUM questions, then twenty AVERAGE questions. It feels harder, but it trains you to choose the right method, just like real work.
