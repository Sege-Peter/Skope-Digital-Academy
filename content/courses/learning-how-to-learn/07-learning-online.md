Today you can learn almost any skill online, often for free: coding, design, digital marketing, bookkeeping, languages and more. But online learning has a big challenge. There is no teacher checking your attendance, so many people start courses and never finish. This lesson helps you be one of the finishers.

## Choosing good sources

Not everything online is accurate. Look for:

- **Well-known platforms**: university courses, official training from companies like Google or Microsoft, established learning sites, and academies like this one.
- **Clear structure**: lessons in order, exercises, and a way to check your progress.
- **Recent content**: especially for technology, where tools change quickly.
- **Good reviews** from other learners.

> **Warning:** Be careful of "courses" that promise you will earn huge amounts quickly if you pay a fee, or that ask you to recruit others to earn. Real skills take practice; quick-money promises are a common scam.

## Set up for success

| Challenge | Solution |
|---|---|
| Data costs | Download videos on Wi-Fi when possible; use lower video quality; buy weekly bundles in advance |
| Power cuts | Charge devices when power is on; keep a power bank |
| Only a phone | Many courses work on phones; use landscape mode and earphones |
| Distractions | Use a fixed time and place; Do Not Disturb mode |

## Learn actively, not passively

Watching videos feels like learning, but just watching is passive. Active online learning means:

1. **Pause and do.** When a tutorial shows a step, pause and do it yourself.
2. **Take notes** in your own words (Cornell or mind maps).
3. **Do every exercise and quiz.** They are active recall practice.
4. **Build something.** After a Canva module, design a real poster for your church event. After an Excel module, make a budget for your family.
5. **Teach or share.** Explain what you learned in a WhatsApp study group.

## Avoid "tutorial hell"

Some learners watch tutorial after tutorial but never feel ready to work on their own. Break out by setting a small **project** after every few lessons, without following a video step by step. Getting stuck and finding your own way out is where real learning happens.

## Learning with others

Learning alone online can feel lonely. Try:

- A **study buddy**: a friend taking the same course; check in with each other weekly.
- **Online communities**: course forums, Facebook groups, or local tech and creative communities in your town.
- **Asking good questions**: say what you tried, what happened, and what you expected.

```text
Weak question:  "My formula is not working, help."

Good question:  "In Excel I typed =SUM(B2:B10) in cell B11 but it
                 shows 0. The cells B2 to B10 contain numbers like
                 1,500 and 2,000. What could be wrong?"
```

## Track your progress

Keep a simple log: date, what you studied, for how long, and one thing you learned. Seeing your progress grow on paper is motivating, and it helps you notice if you have skipped days.

## Practice task

Choose one free online course or tutorial series for a skill you want to earn from. Write down: the platform, the number of lessons, your study days and times for the next two weeks, and one small project you will build when you are halfway through.
