"I'll start tomorrow." Everyone says it. **Procrastination** means delaying something you know you should do, often by doing something easier or more fun instead. It is one of the biggest reasons people do not finish courses or build new skills.

## Why we procrastinate

When we think about a task that feels hard or boring, we feel a small moment of discomfort. Checking WhatsApp or watching one more video makes that feeling go away instantly. The relief is real, but short, and the task is still waiting.

So procrastination is usually not laziness. It is a way of avoiding an uncomfortable feeling. The trick is to make **starting** easier.

## Focus on the process, not the product

"Finish my whole website" feels huge. "Work on my website for 25 minutes" feels possible. Instead of focusing on the end product, focus on **showing up and putting in the time**. The product will come from many small sessions.

## The Pomodoro technique

A popular and simple method, named after a tomato-shaped kitchen timer:

1. Choose one task.
2. Set a timer for **25 minutes**.
3. Work only on that task. Phone face down, notifications off.
4. When the timer rings, take a **5-minute break**.
5. After four rounds, take a longer break of 15–30 minutes.

```text
Achieng's Saturday plan (learning graphic design)
08:00 – 08:25  Pomodoro 1: watch Canva tutorial, take notes
08:25 – 08:30  Break: stretch, drink water
08:30 – 08:55  Pomodoro 2: design a practice poster
08:55 – 09:00  Break
09:00 – 09:25  Pomodoro 3: redesign the poster from memory
09:25 – 09:30  Break
09:30 – 09:55  Pomodoro 4: review flashcards on design terms
09:55 – 10:25  Long break
```

Twenty-five minutes is a starting point. Some people prefer 15 minutes at first, others 45. Adjust it to what works for you.

> **Tip:** If you do not want to start, tell yourself, "Just one Pomodoro." Starting is usually the hardest part; once you begin, it often feels easier to continue.

## Control your environment

| Distraction | Fix |
|---|---|
| Phone notifications | Turn on Do Not Disturb or flight mode during study |
| Social media | Log out, or move the apps off your home screen |
| Noisy home | Study early in the morning, at a library, or use earphones |
| No clear task | Write tomorrow's tasks the night before |

## Small habits

Habits run on a simple loop: a **cue** (a trigger), a **routine** (the action), and a **reward**. Build a learning habit on purpose:

- **Cue:** After breakfast, sit at the same table.
- **Routine:** One Pomodoro on your course.
- **Reward:** Tick it off on a chart, then enjoy a cup of tea.

Do it at the same time each day and it becomes automatic.

## Practice task

Choose one task you have been putting off. Do two Pomodoros on it today with your phone in another room. Afterwards, write down how much you got done and how you felt before versus after starting.
