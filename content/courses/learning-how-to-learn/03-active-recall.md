If you remember only one method from this course, make it this one. **Active recall** means pulling information **out** of your memory instead of just putting it in. It is one of the best-supported study methods in learning research.

## Re-reading versus recalling

Imagine Achieng is studying for a driving theory test.

- **Method A:** She reads her driving school handbook three times. It feels familiar, so she feels ready.
- **Method B:** She reads it once, then closes it and asks herself, "What does a triangle sign mean? What are the rules at a roundabout?" She checks her answers and repeats the ones she got wrong.

Method B feels harder, but it builds much stronger memory. Re-reading creates an **illusion of knowing**: the page looks familiar, so you think you know it, but you cannot produce it when the book is closed.

## Ways to practise active recall

| Method | How it works |
|---|---|
| Close the book | After reading a section, close it and write or say everything you remember |
| Flashcards | Question on one side, answer on the other; test yourself |
| Practice questions | Use past papers, end-of-lesson quizzes, or write your own questions |
| Teach someone | Explain the topic to a friend or younger sibling without notes |
| Blank page | Write a topic at the top of a blank page and fill it from memory |

## Making flashcards

You can use small pieces of paper or a free app such as Anki or Quizlet.

```text
Front: What does the SUM function do in Excel?
Back:  Adds up the numbers in a range, e.g. =SUM(B2:B10)

Front: What is gross profit?
Back:  Sales minus the cost of the goods sold.

Front: Kiswahili for "customer"?
Back:  Mteja
```

Good flashcards ask **one** clear question each. Make them in your own words.

## The "explain it simply" test

Try explaining a topic as if to a 12-year-old. Where you get stuck or start using vague words, you have found a gap in your understanding. Go back to that part, learn it again, then explain it once more.

## Mistakes are part of it

When you test yourself, you will get things wrong. That is good. Each mistake shows you exactly what to study, and correcting it strengthens the memory. Do not just look at the right answer; try to understand **why** it is right.

> **Note:** Active recall feels harder and slower than re-reading. That effort is a sign it is working, not a sign you are failing.

## Practice task

Pick a topic you are learning right now (or one from school you want to keep, like a Business Studies topic). Make ten flashcards in your own words. Test yourself today, put the ones you got wrong in a separate pile, and test those again tomorrow.
