**Saving** means keeping part of your income for later instead of spending it now. Savings give you choices: to handle emergencies without borrowing, to pay for training, or to start a business.

## The emergency fund

An **emergency fund** is money set aside only for real emergencies: a sudden illness, losing your job, a broken phone you need for work, or a family crisis.

A common target is enough to cover **three to six months of essential expenses**. If that sounds impossible right now, start smaller:

1. First target: **KES 1,000–2,000** – covers a small shock.
2. Next target: **one month** of needs.
3. Then build towards three months.

```text
Brian's essential monthly costs: KES 8,000
One month target:     KES 8,000
Three months target:  3 × 8,000 = KES 24,000
If he saves KES 1,000 a month, three months of cover takes 24 months.
If he saves KES 2,000 a month, it takes 12 months.
```

> **Tip:** An emergency fund is not for a sale on shoes or a friend's party. Write down what counts as an emergency so you are not tempted.

## Where to keep savings

| Option | Good for | Things to check |
|---|---|---|
| Mobile money savings products (e.g. linked to your M-Pesa) | Small, regular savings you can reach quickly | How easy it is to withdraw; any charges; whether it earns interest |
| Bank savings account | Emergency fund, medium goals | Minimum balance and fees |
| **SACCO** | Regular saving with the chance to borrow later | Membership rules, how long before you can borrow, whether it is licensed |
| **Chama** | Group discipline and support | Written rules, honest officials, records of every contribution |
| Money market fund | Medium-term savings | Licensed fund manager, fees, how long withdrawals take |

> **Note:** Savings products, interest and fees change. Compare current terms on the provider's official website, and for SACCOs and fund managers, check that they are licensed by the relevant regulator before joining.

## Chamas: saving together

A **chama** is a group of people who pool money regularly. Common types:

- **Merry-go-round:** every member contributes each month and one member takes the whole pot in turn.
- **Savings and lending:** contributions build a fund that lends to members and shares the returns.
- **Investment chama:** members save towards a joint investment, such as land or a business.

Good chamas have a written constitution, keep records of every payment, use a group account that needs more than one signatory, and hold regular meetings.

## Habits that build savings

- **Automate it:** set a standing order or move savings the day you are paid.
- **Start small:** KES 50 a day adds up to about KES 1,500 a month.
- **Save windfalls:** when you get unexpected money, save at least half.
- **Keep savings out of sight:** separate account, not your daily M-Pesa balance.
