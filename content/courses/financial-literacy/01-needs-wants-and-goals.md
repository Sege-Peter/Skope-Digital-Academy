**Financial literacy** means understanding how money works so you can make good decisions with it: earning, spending, saving, borrowing, investing and protecting yourself. It is not about how much you earn – people with big salaries can still be broke, while careful people with small incomes can build security.

## Needs and wants

- A **need** is something you must have to live, stay healthy, or keep earning: food, shelter, basic clothing, transport to work, school fees, medical care.
- A **want** is something nice to have, but you can live without it: the latest phone, eating out, new sneakers, a bigger data bundle for entertainment.

Some things are partly both. You *need* a phone to find work and receive M-Pesa; you *want* the newest model. The need is the basic version; the upgrade is the want.

| Item | Need or want? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Rent for a bedsitter | Need | Shelter |
| Fare to your job | Need | Keeps your income coming |
| Weekly takeaway with friends | Want | Enjoyable, but optional |
| Basic smartphone | Need (for many) | Work, M-Pesa, job applications |
| Upgrading to a new phone every year | Want | The old phone still works |
| Hospital visit when sick | Need | Health |

> **Tip:** Before buying something, wait 24 hours and ask: "Is this a need or a want? Can I afford it without touching my savings?" Many impulse buys disappear after a night's sleep.

## Why it matters

Wants are not bad – enjoying your money is part of life. The problem comes when wants are paid for before needs, or with borrowed money. That is how many young people end up taking a digital loan for school fees after spending their pay on a weekend out.

## Setting money goals

A goal gives your money a job. Make goals **specific**, with an amount and a date.

| Vague | Specific |
|---|---|
| "I want to save." | "I will save KES 6,000 for a laptop deposit by 30 June." |
| "I want a business." | "I will save KES 4,000 to start a smocha stand in 4 months." |

Divide the goal by the time to get your target:

```text
Goal: KES 6,000 in 6 months
Monthly target: 6,000 ÷ 6 = KES 1,000 a month
Weekly target:  6,000 ÷ 26 weeks = about KES 231 a week
```

## Short, medium and long-term goals

- **Short-term** (under a year): emergency fund, a course fee, a phone repair.
- **Medium-term** (1–5 years): college fees, business equipment, a plot deposit.
- **Long-term** (over 5 years): building a home, retirement.

Write them down and put them where you will see them. A written goal is easier to remember, track and share with someone who can keep you accountable.

```exercise
Q: Classify each item: rent, then a new pair of designer sneakers.
Rent is a ___; designer sneakers are a ___
A: need | want
```

```exercise
Q: Kiprono wants KES 9,000 in 3 months. How much must he save each month?
Monthly target = KES ___
A: 3000 / 3,000
```

## Practice task

List **ten things you spent money on** in the last two weeks and mark each as a need or a want. Then write **one specific money goal** with an amount and a date, and work out the monthly and weekly saving target.
