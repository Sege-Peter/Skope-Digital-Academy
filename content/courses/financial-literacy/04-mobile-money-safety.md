Mobile money like **M-Pesa** has made sending, receiving and saving money easy for millions of Kenyans. It has also become a favourite target for fraudsters. Most mobile money fraud succeeds not by hacking, but by tricking people into giving away their PIN or sending money themselves.

## Your PIN is the key

- **Never share your M-Pesa PIN** with anyone – not a friend, an agent, a family member or someone who says they are from customer care.
- Real customer care staff will **never** ask for your PIN.
- Choose a PIN that is not your birth year, `1234`, or `0000`.
- Cover the keypad when entering your PIN in public.
- Lock your phone with a PIN, pattern or fingerprint.

## Common scams in Kenya

| Scam | How it works | How to protect yourself |
|---|---|---|
| **"Wrong number" refund** | A fake SMS says someone sent you money by mistake, then they call begging you to send it back | Check your actual M-Pesa balance. If no money arrived, send nothing. Real reversals go through the provider |
| **Fake customer care** | Caller claims your line will be blocked unless you give your PIN or dial a code | Hang up. Call the official customer care number yourself |
| **Fake prize or promotion** | "You have won KES 50,000! Send KES 500 to claim" | Real promotions do not ask winners to pay |
| **SIM swap** | Fraudster tricks the network into moving your number to their SIM, then empties your account | If your phone suddenly loses network for a long time, contact your provider immediately |
| **Fake job offers** | "Pay a registration fee to secure the job" | Genuine employers do not charge to hire you |
| **Impersonating relatives** | "Mum, I'm using a friend's phone, send money urgently" | Call the person on their usual number to confirm |

> **Warning:** If you feel rushed, pressured or excited by a message about money, stop. Urgency is the fraudster's main tool. Take five minutes and verify.

## Check the message carefully

Real M-Pesa messages come from the official sender name, not a normal phone number, and include a transaction code. But fake messages can look very real, so the only proof that money arrived is **your actual balance**.

## Safe habits for everyday use

1. **Confirm the name** shown before you complete a payment – if it does not match, cancel.
2. **Keep messages** as records; they help if there is a dispute.
3. **Set a daily limit** for yourself, and keep most savings in a separate savings product.
4. **Use official apps only**, downloaded from the official app store.
5. **Report fraud quickly** to your mobile money provider and, where needed, to the police.

## If you sent money to the wrong person

Contact your provider through its official channels as soon as possible and follow its reversal process. Do not pay anyone who offers to "help" reverse it for a fee.

## Digital footprint

Avoid posting your ID number, phone number and M-Pesa screenshots publicly on social media. Fraudsters collect these details to make their stories believable.
