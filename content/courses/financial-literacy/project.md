## The situation

Brian has landed his first casual job in Eldoret and expects to earn about KES 12,000 in his first month. His friends have already invited him to a "double your money" group and offered him a quick phone loan. Brian wants a plan before the money arrives. In this project you will build a **one-page money plan** for Brian (a made-up person), using the made-up figures below, and then judge two offers he receives.

> **Note:** This project uses a fictional person and fictional numbers. Do **not** share your real income, savings, debts, M-Pesa number, PIN or bank details. If you want to adapt the plan for yourself, keep that copy private.

## What you will produce

One document (text box, or a PDF/Word/spreadsheet file) with four parts:

1. **Budget table** – a monthly budget for Brian with at least 8 lines, grouped into needs, wants and savings. The total must equal KES 12,000 exactly. Show the sums.
2. **Goal and emergency fund** – one specific goal with an amount and a date (e.g. a phone, school fees), the monthly and weekly saving needed, and an emergency fund target based on Brian's essential costs. Say where Brian should keep it and why.
3. **Two offers compared** – use these made-up offers:

```text
Offer A: A phone app lends KES 3,000 today. Repay KES 3,600 in 14 days.
Offer B: A SACCO lends KES 3,000. Repay KES 3,300 in 3 months, in three equal parts.
```

   Work out the extra cost of each (repay minus borrowed), say which is cheaper, and explain why in 2–3 sentences.
4. **Scam check** – read this message and list at least three warning signs: "Join today! Put in KES 2,000 and get KES 6,000 in 7 days. Invite 3 friends to earn more. No risk!" Say what Brian should do.

## Steps

1. Re-read the lessons on budgeting, saving, borrowing and investment scams.
2. List Brian's needs first, then wants, then savings. Make the total match.
3. Set the goal and divide it into months and weeks.
4. Do the sums for both loan offers and compare.
5. Write the scam warning signs and Brian's next step.

## What to submit

- Your plan in the text box **or** as a PDF/Word file (max 5 MB), or a Google Sheets link with sharing set to "anyone with the link can view".

## Tips

- Check that the budget lines really add up before you submit.
- Show your working – a peer cannot give you marks for an answer they cannot follow.
- Do not claim real interest rates or legal rules; use only the numbers given here.
