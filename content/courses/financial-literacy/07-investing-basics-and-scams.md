**Investing** means putting money into something with the hope that it will grow in value or produce income over time. Unlike saving, investing usually carries **risk** – you could get back less than you put in.

> **Note:** This lesson explains general ideas so you can understand and ask good questions. It is not advice on what *you* should invest in. For personal advice, speak to a licensed financial adviser.

## Save first, then invest

Before investing, most people should have:

1. A budget they follow.
2. An emergency fund.
3. No expensive debt (such as rolling digital loans).

Otherwise, an emergency may force you to sell an investment at a bad time.

## Risk and return

A basic rule of money: **higher possible returns come with higher risk.** Anyone promising high returns with "no risk" is either mistaken or lying.

| Type | What it is | General risk level |
|---|---|---|
| Savings account / fixed deposit | Money held by a bank | Low |
| Money market fund | A fund that lends to governments and banks for short periods | Low to medium |
| Government securities (bills and bonds) | Lending money to the government | Low to medium |
| SACCO shares and deposits | Member-owned savings and credit | Depends on how well the SACCO is run |
| Shares (stocks) on the stock exchange | Owning part of a listed company | Medium to high; prices go up and down |
| Your own business | Building income from a skill or product | Varies; you control more of it |
| Land and property | Physical assets | Needs large amounts; check title documents carefully |

## Key ideas

- **Diversify:** don't put all your money in one place.
- **Think long term:** investments like shares can fall in the short term.
- **Understand fees:** costs reduce your returns.
- **Use licensed providers:** in Kenya, check fund managers and brokers with the Capital Markets Authority ([cma.or.ke](https://www.cma.or.ke)), banks with the Central Bank of Kenya, and SACCOs with the SACCO regulator.
- **Only invest in what you understand.** If you cannot explain how it makes money, don't put money in.

## Pyramid and Ponzi schemes

A **pyramid scheme** pays members mainly from the joining fees of new recruits, not from selling a real product. A **Ponzi scheme** pays "returns" to early investors using money from later investors. Both collapse when new money stops, and most people lose.

Warning signs:

| Red flag | Example |
|---|---|
| Guaranteed high returns | "Double your money in 30 days" |
| Pressure to recruit | "Bring three people to unlock your earnings" |
| Payment to personal numbers | "Send to this Till/number to activate your account" |
| Vague business | "Forex robot", "crypto mining", "global investment" with no details |
| Secrecy and urgency | "Only today", "don't tell your family yet" |
| Early payouts to build trust | You receive a small profit first, so you put in more |

> **Warning:** Many Kenyans have lost savings to schemes promoted on WhatsApp, Telegram and TikTok – often by friends and relatives who genuinely believed in them. If it promises easy, fast, guaranteed money, walk away.

## Betting is not investing

Betting and gambling are designed so that, on average, the house wins. Treat any betting as entertainment spending from your "wants", never as a way to grow money or repay debt.
