A **budget** is a plan for your money before you spend it. Instead of wondering where your money went at the end of the month, you decide in advance where it will go.

## Step 1: Know your income

Write down all the money you expect this month: pay from a job, money from a small business, casual work, or support from family. If your income changes from month to month (as with many hustles), use a **low but realistic** figure.

## Step 2: Track your spending

For two weeks, write down **everything** you spend, even KES 20 for sweets. Many people are surprised by how much goes to small items: airtime, snacks, fare, betting, mobile data.

## Step 3: Plan with a simple rule

One popular guideline is the **50/30/20 rule**:

| Share | For | On KES 12,000 a month |
|---|---|---|
| 50% | **Needs** – rent, food, fare, basic bills | KES 6,000 |
| 30% | **Wants** – entertainment, eating out, extras | KES 3,600 |
| 20% | **Savings and debt repayment** | KES 2,400 |
| 100% | | KES 12,000 |

> **Note:** 50/30/20 is a guideline, not a law. If you live with your parents, you might save much more. If rent in Nairobi takes most of your pay, needs may be 70%. Adjust the percentages to your life, but always keep **some** amount for savings.

## Step 4: Write the budget

```text
Fatuma's monthly budget – income KES 15,000 (salon assistant, Mombasa)

NEEDS
  Rent (shared room)          4,000
  Food                        3,500
  Fare                        1,500
  Phone and data                500
  Total needs                 9,500

WANTS
  Outings and treats          1,500
  Clothes and beauty          1,000
  Total wants                 2,500

SAVINGS
  Emergency fund              1,500
  Chama contribution          1,500
  Total savings               3,000

TOTAL                        15,000
```

Every shilling has a job, and the total equals her income. This is called a **zero-based budget** – income minus planned spending and saving equals zero.

## Step 5: Compare and adjust

At the end of the month, compare what you planned with what really happened. If food cost KES 4,200 instead of 3,500, find the reason and either cut elsewhere or change next month's plan.

## Tips that work

- **Pay yourself first:** move savings out the day you are paid, before spending starts.
- **Use envelopes or separate wallets:** for example, keep savings in an M-Pesa savings product or a SACCO account, not in your main balance.
- **Plan for irregular costs:** birthdays, school events, a phone repair – set aside a little each month.
- **Avoid betting money "to top up" the budget** – it is a common way to lose the money you planned for needs.

```exercise
Q: Use the 50/30/20 rule on an income of KES 10,000.
Needs KES ___, wants KES ___, savings KES ___
A: 5000 / 5,000 | 3000 / 3,000 | 2000 / 2,000
```

```exercise
Q: Income is KES 8,000. Needs are KES 5,500 and wants are KES 1,500. What is left for savings?
Savings = KES ___
A: 1000 / 1,000
```

## Practice task

**Track every shilling you spend for three days.** Then write a one-month budget using your real income, with needs, wants and savings sections that add up exactly to your income.
