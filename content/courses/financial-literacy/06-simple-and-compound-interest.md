**Interest** is the price of using money. When you borrow, you pay interest. When you save or invest, you may earn interest. Understanding how it is calculated helps you see why debt can grow so fast – and why starting to save early is powerful.

> **Note:** The rates in this lesson (like 10%) are chosen only to make the maths easy. They are not real rates from any bank or lender. Real rates change – always check the current terms with the provider.

## Key words

- **Principal** – the original amount borrowed or saved.
- **Rate** – the percentage charged or earned per period (per month or per year).
- **Time** – how many periods the money is borrowed or saved for.

## Simple interest

With **simple interest**, interest is calculated only on the original principal.

```text
Simple interest = Principal × Rate × Time
```

Example: Achieng saves KES 10,000 at 10% per year simple interest for 3 years.

```text
Interest = 10,000 × 0.10 × 3 = KES 3,000
Total after 3 years = 10,000 + 3,000 = KES 13,000
```

She earns the same KES 1,000 every year.

## Compound interest

With **compound interest**, interest is added to the balance, and the next period's interest is calculated on the new, bigger balance. You earn (or pay) **interest on interest**.

Same example, but 10% per year compounded yearly:

| Year | Start balance | Interest (10%) | End balance |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 10,000 | 1,000 | 11,000 |
| 2 | 11,000 | 1,100 | 12,100 |
| 3 | 12,100 | 1,210 | 13,310 |

Total interest = 1,000 + 1,100 + 1,210 = **KES 3,310**, compared with KES 3,000 simple interest. The gap grows larger the longer the time.

The formula is:

```text
Final amount = Principal × (1 + rate) ^ number of periods
10,000 × (1.10)^3 = 10,000 × 1.331 = KES 13,310
```

## Compounding works against you in debt

If unpaid interest and penalties are added to a loan balance, you start paying interest on them too. That is why an unpaid loan can seem to grow "by itself". Example with an illustrative 10% per month:

| Month | Balance owed (no payments made) |
|---|---|
| 0 | 5,000 |
| 1 | 5,500 |
| 2 | 6,050 |
| 3 | 6,655 |

In just three months the debt has grown by KES 1,655.

> **Tip:** Compare loans by the cost per **year**, not per month or per week. A small-sounding monthly charge can be very large over a year.

## Compounding works for you in savings

Starting early matters. Someone who starts saving at 18 gives their money more years to compound than someone who starts at 30 – even if the 30-year-old saves more each month. Time is a young person's biggest advantage.

```exercise
Q: Find the simple interest on KES 20,000 at 5% per year for 2 years.
Interest = KES ___
A: 2000 / 2,000
H: Principal × rate × time = 20,000 × 0.05 × 2.
```

```exercise
Q: KES 1,000 earns 10% compound interest per year. Fill in the balance after year 1 and year 2.
Year 1: KES ___, year 2: KES ___
A: 1100 / 1,100 | 1210 / 1,210
```
