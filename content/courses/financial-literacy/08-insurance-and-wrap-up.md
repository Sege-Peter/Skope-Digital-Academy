**Insurance** is a way of sharing risk. Many people pay a small, regular amount (a **premium**) into a pool. When one of them suffers a covered loss – an illness, an accident, a theft – the insurer pays from the pool. You pay a little for certain to avoid a large loss that might happen.

## Key words

| Word | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Premium** | The amount you pay for cover, e.g. monthly or yearly |
| **Cover** | What the policy protects you against |
| **Claim** | Your request to the insurer to pay after a loss |
| **Excess** | The part of a loss you pay yourself before the insurer pays |
| **Exclusions** | Situations the policy does **not** cover |
| **Beneficiary** | The person who receives the money (for example, in life cover) |

## Types of insurance relevant to young people

- **Health insurance** – helps pay hospital and medical bills. Kenya has a national public health insurance scheme; check the current scheme, registration and contributions on the official government website. Private health covers also exist.
- **Personal accident cover** – pays if you are injured, useful for boda boda riders and others who travel a lot.
- **Motor insurance** – a legal requirement for vehicles and motorcycles on the road. Check the current minimum cover required by law before you ride or drive.
- **Business or stock insurance** – protects a shop or stall against fire, theft or damage.
- **Phone and gadget insurance** – sometimes offered when you buy a phone; check the exclusions and the excess.
- **Life insurance** – pays your dependants if you die; more important once others depend on your income.

## Example: why the excess matters

```text
Kiprono's phone insurance
Premium: KES 200 a month
Excess: KES 2,000 per claim
His screen breaks and the repair costs KES 6,000.
Kiprono pays the excess:    KES 2,000
The insurer pays:   6,000 − 2,000 = KES 4,000
```

If the repair had cost KES 1,500, which is less than the excess, the insurer would pay nothing.

## Before you buy a policy

1. Is the insurer licensed? Check with the Insurance Regulatory Authority ([ira.go.ke](https://www.ira.go.ke)).
2. What exactly is covered, and what is excluded?
3. What is the excess, and how do you make a claim?
4. Can you afford the premium every month, even in a bad month?
5. Is anything hidden in the small print? Ask questions until you understand.

> **Warning:** Insurance agents should give you written policy documents. Be careful with anyone who asks you to pay premiums to a personal number, or who promises investment returns that sound too good to be true.
