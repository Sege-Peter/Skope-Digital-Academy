A **prompt** is the instruction or question you give an AI tool. The same chatbot can give you a weak, generic answer or an excellent, ready-to-use one – the difference is usually the prompt.

## Weak prompt versus strong prompt

```text
Weak:   Write a post about my business.
```

```text
Strong: You are a social media marketer in Kenya. Write a Facebook post for
        Mama Njeri's Kitchen, a small food kiosk in Nakuru town that sells
        chapati, beans and tea from 6 a.m. Mention our new KES 100 breakfast
        combo. Use friendly English with a little Sheng, keep it under
        80 words and end with our phone number placeholder [PHONE].
```

The strong prompt tells the AI *who to be*, *what to do*, *the details it needs* and *how the answer should look*.

## The RTCF recipe

| Letter | Means | Ask yourself | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **R** | Role | Who should the AI act as? | "You are a patient maths tutor…" |
| **T** | Task | What exactly must it do? | "…explain how to calculate percentage profit…" |
| **C** | Context | What background does it need? | "…to a Form 4 leaver who runs a small mitumba stall…" |
| **F** | Format | How should the answer look? | "…in 5 short steps with one KES example." |

You do not need all four every time, but adding **context** and **format** almost always improves the answer.

## More prompt habits that work

1. **Be specific about length.** "In 3 bullet points", "under 150 words", "one paragraph".
2. **Give an example.** Paste a sample of the style you want: "Write it in a style like this: …".
3. **Ask for options.** "Give me 5 different names for a car-wash business in Thika."
4. **Ask it to ask you.** "Before you write my CV summary, ask me 5 questions about my experience."
5. **Iterate.** Treat the first answer as a draft: "Make it shorter", "More formal", "Add a call to action", "Translate to Kiswahili".
6. **Break big tasks into steps.** First an outline, then each section.

> **Tip:** Keep a "prompt notebook" – a notes file where you save prompts that gave great results. Reusing and adjusting good prompts saves a lot of time, especially in freelance work.

## Follow-up prompts

A conversation is more powerful than a single question:

```text
You:  Give me a 4-week study plan for KCSE-level chemistry revision,
      1 hour per day.
AI:   (gives a plan)
You:  Turn week 1 into a table with columns Day, Topic, Activity.
You:  Now add one practice question for each day.
```

## What not to put in a prompt

- Passwords, M-Pesa PINs, ID numbers or bank details
- Other people's private information (phone numbers, health details) without permission
- Confidential company documents, unless your employer has approved the tool

We cover privacy fully in [Privacy and Responsible Use](privacy-and-responsible-use).

## Practice task

Pick a real need – a CV summary, a WhatsApp advert for a friend's business, or revision notes. Write a **weak** prompt and run it in a chatbot. Then rewrite it using RTCF and run it again. Copy both answers into a document and write two sentences on what improved. Save the strong prompt in your new prompt notebook.
