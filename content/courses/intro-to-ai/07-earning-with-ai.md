AI does not replace the need for skills – it multiplies them. A school leaver who can combine AI tools with good judgement can offer useful services to businesses, online and offline.

## Services you can offer

| Service | How AI helps | Your part (what clients pay for) |
|---|---|---|
| **Writing** – blog posts, product descriptions, social media captions | Drafts, outlines, headline ideas, grammar checks | Research, accuracy, local knowledge, a human voice |
| **Graphic design** – posters, flyers, social media posts | Canva Magic Design, background removal, image generation | Layout, brand colours, correct spelling and prices, taste |
| **Virtual assistant (VA)** – email, scheduling, data entry, research | Drafting replies, summarising, organising lists, formulas | Reliability, confidentiality, following up |
| **Transcription and captions** | Auto-transcribe audio, auto-caption videos | Correcting names, Sheng and Kiswahili terms, timing |
| **Translation** (English ↔ Kiswahili and others) | First-draft translation | Making it natural and culturally correct |
| **Social media management** for local businesses | Content calendars, caption ideas, hashtag ideas | Knowing the customers, posting consistently, replying |

## A worked example: a local client

Brian in Eldoret offers social media help to small businesses. A salon owner pays him monthly to post three times a week.

```text
1. Brian asks the owner for photos, prices and offers each week.
2. He prompts a chatbot: "Write 3 short Instagram captions for a salon in
   Eldoret offering box braids at KES 1,500 this weekend. Friendly tone,
   2 emojis max, include a call to book on WhatsApp."
3. He picks the best caption and edits it to sound like the salon.
4. He designs the post in Canva using the salon's colours.
5. He checks the price and phone number twice, then schedules it.
```

AI cut his time per post from about an hour to about 20 minutes, so he can serve more clients. What he charges depends on the client and his experience – agree on a price in writing before starting.

## Online platforms

You can find clients locally (WhatsApp, Facebook groups, walking into shops) or on global platforms such as **Upwork**, **Fiverr** and **LinkedIn**. Many beginners start locally because it is easier to build trust and get reviews. Read each platform's terms, because some have rules on disclosing AI use.

> **Warning:** Scammers target people looking for online work. Never pay a "registration fee", "training fee" or "account activation fee" to get a job. Never share your passwords, M-Pesa PIN or OTP codes. Real clients pay *you*.

## Be honest about AI

- Never sell raw, unchecked AI output. Clients notice, and errors damage your reputation.
- If a client asks whether you use AI, tell the truth. Explain that you use it as a tool and that you check and edit everything.
- Do not use AI to copy someone else's design, logo or writing style closely, or to create fake reviews.
- Check the terms of image tools before using AI images commercially.

## Build a small portfolio

Clients want proof. Create 3–5 sample pieces (a poster for an imaginary bakery, three product descriptions, a cleaned-up spreadsheet) and save them as PDFs or images in a Google Drive folder you can share.
