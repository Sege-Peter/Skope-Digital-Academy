AI tools are impressive, but they make mistakes in predictable ways. Knowing these weak spots is what separates a careful AI user from a careless one.

## Hallucinations

A **hallucination** is when an AI produces information that sounds confident and correct but is false or invented. It happens because a language model predicts *likely-sounding* words; it does not truly "know" facts.

Common examples:

- Inventing a book, court case or research paper that does not exist
- Giving wrong dates, prices, phone numbers or statistics
- Making up quotes from real people
- Describing a Kenyan law, fee or procedure that has changed or never existed
- Getting a maths calculation wrong while sounding certain

> **Warning:** Never use AI answers for medical, legal or financial decisions without checking with a qualified professional or an official source (for example kra.go.ke for tax, or a licensed doctor for health).

## How to check AI answers

| Check | How |
|---|---|
| **Ask for sources** | "Give me the sources for this." Then open each link – some may be fake. |
| **Cross-check** | Search the key fact on Google or an official website. |
| **Recalculate** | Redo important numbers yourself or in a spreadsheet. |
| **Ask again differently** | Rephrase the question; if answers disagree, be suspicious. |
| **Use common sense** | If a claim seems surprising, it needs stronger proof. |

## Bias

**Bias** means an AI system treats some people or groups unfairly or gives a one-sided picture. It usually comes from the training data, which reflects the world's existing inequalities and gaps.

Examples of how bias appears:

- An image generator asked for "a doctor" shows mostly men, and "a nurse" mostly women.
- A CV-screening tool trained on past hires that were mostly from one group scores others lower.
- A chatbot knows a lot about the USA but gives shallow or wrong answers about Kisumu or Garissa, because there was less text about them in its training data.
- Speech recognition struggles with some accents or with mixing English, Kiswahili and Sheng.

## What you can do about bias

1. **Notice it.** Ask: who might be missing or misrepresented in this answer or image?
2. **Prompt for balance.** "Show doctors of different genders and ages, in a Kenyan hospital."
3. **Add local knowledge.** You know your community – correct the AI where it is wrong.
4. **Don't let AI make final decisions about people** (hiring, lending, discipline) without human review.

## Other limits

- **Knowledge cut-off:** a model may not know recent events unless connected to web search.
- **No real understanding:** it can follow patterns without understanding meaning.
- **Inconsistency:** the same question can get different answers on different tries.
- **Cost and connectivity:** most tools need internet data, and the best features may be paid.

## Practice task

Ask a chatbot: "List 3 books written by [a Kenyan author you know] with the year each was published." Check every title and year using a Google search or a library website. Record how many were fully correct. Then ask an image tool (or imagine the result) for "a successful business owner" and note who it shows – and how you would rewrite the prompt to be more balanced.
