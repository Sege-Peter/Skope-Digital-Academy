Used well, AI is like a patient tutor and a fast assistant available at any hour. Used badly, it becomes a shortcut that stops you from learning. This lesson shows the good way.

## AI as a study partner

| Study need | Example prompt |
|---|---|
| Understand a hard topic | "Explain compound interest like I'm 15, using an M-Pesa savings example." |
| Test yourself | "Give me 10 multiple-choice questions on the Kenyan Constitution, Chapter 4. Don't show answers until I reply." |
| Revise from notes | "Turn these notes into a one-page summary with key terms in bold: [paste notes]" |
| Plan your time | "Make a 6-week plan to learn basic Excel, 45 minutes a day." |
| Improve your writing | "Point out grammar mistakes in this paragraph and explain each one. Don't rewrite it." |
| Practise a language | "Let's chat in French at beginner level. Correct my mistakes gently." |

> **Tip:** Ask the AI to *explain* and *quiz* you, not to *do* the work for you. If you can't explain the answer without the AI, you haven't learned it yet.

## Honesty at college and school

Many colleges and universities now have rules on AI use. Some allow it for brainstorming but not for writing submitted work; some require you to say when you used it. Submitting AI-written work as your own where it is not allowed is **academic dishonesty**, and it can lead to failing a unit or worse. Read your institution's policy and ask your lecturer when unsure.

## AI at work

Even in non-tech jobs, AI tools save time:

- **Emails and letters** – draft a polite reminder to a client who hasn't paid an invoice.
- **Meeting notes** – turn rough notes into clear action points.
- **Spreadsheets** – ask how to write a formula ("How do I count cells that say 'Paid' in column D?").
- **Customer service** – draft replies to common questions, then personalise them.
- **Research** – get a quick overview of a topic before reading proper sources.
- **Planning** – create checklists, schedules and simple project plans.

## A simple workflow: Draft – Check – Personalise

```text
1. DRAFT        Ask the AI for a first version.
2. CHECK        Verify facts, names, numbers, dates and prices.
3. PERSONALISE  Add your own knowledge, voice and local details.
```

The AI does the blank-page work; *you* add accuracy and judgement. Employers value people who can do this – it is the skill, not just "knowing ChatGPT".

## Tools inside apps you already use

AI is now built into common tools: Google Docs and Gmail (Gemini features), Microsoft Word and Excel (Copilot), Canva (Magic Write and Magic Design), and WhatsApp (Meta AI). Availability varies by country and plan, so check what your account shows.

## Practice task

Choose a topic you find hard (from school or something new you want to learn). Ask a chatbot to explain it simply, then ask it to give you **5 quiz questions** without the answers. Answer them yourself first, then ask the AI to mark your answers. Write down your score and one thing you now understand better.
