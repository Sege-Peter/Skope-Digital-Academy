Every school, hospital, bank, hotel, county office and cyber café needs working networks. ISPs keep extending fibre to new towns and estates, and businesses keep adding Wi-Fi, CCTV and cloud services. That creates steady demand for people with practical networking skills.

## Jobs you can aim for

| Role | What they do | Where |
|---|---|---|
| IT support technician | Sets up PCs, printers and connections; fixes everyday problems | Schools, offices, cyber cafés |
| Network installation technician | Runs and terminates cables, mounts access points and racks | Contractors, ISPs |
| Fibre technician | Installs and splices fibre, connects customers' homes and businesses | ISPs and their contractors |
| Field / customer support engineer | Visits customers to install and fix internet connections | ISPs, telecom companies |
| Network administrator | Manages routers, switches, Wi-Fi and security for an organisation | Banks, universities, counties, large companies |
| Network engineer | Designs and builds larger networks | Telcos, data centres, consultancies |
| Freelance / small business | Sets up and maintains networks for shops, salons, churches and homes | Self-employed |

## Certifications (general overview)

Certifications show employers that you have tested skills. Common ones include:

| Certification | Level | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| **CompTIA A+** | Entry | PC hardware, operating systems, basic networking and support |
| **CompTIA Network+** | Entry to intermediate | Vendor-neutral networking concepts and troubleshooting |
| **Cisco CCNA** | Associate | Routing, switching, IP addressing, security and automation basics on Cisco equipment |
| **MikroTik MTCNA** | Entry | Configuring MikroTik routers, which are widely used by small ISPs and hotspot businesses in East Africa |

> **Note:** Exam fees, versions and content change regularly. Always check the official certification website for current details, and buy exam vouchers only from official sources or authorised partners.

## A realistic path

1. **Practise at home and at school.** Explore your router (with permission), make and test cables, run the commands from this course.
2. **Learn free online.** Cisco Networking Academy (netacad.com) offers free beginner courses such as networking basics; packet-tracer simulation software lets you build virtual networks without buying equipment.
3. **Get hands-on experience.** Volunteer in your school's computer lab, help a local cyber café, or seek an industrial attachment with an ISP or IT company.
4. **Take a certification** when you are ready, or join a diploma/certificate course in IT or computer networking at a TVET college or university.
5. **Build a portfolio**: photos (with permission) of tidy installations you did, network diagrams you drew, and recommendation letters from clients.

> **Warning:** Beware of "guaranteed ISP jobs" or "fibre training with job placement" that require large payments to individuals via M-Pesa. Verify training providers and employers through official channels before paying anything.

## Course summary

- **Module 1:** what a network is; PAN, LAN, MAN, WAN and the Internet; modems, routers, switches and access points.
- **Module 2:** IPv4 addresses, subnet masks, public vs private addresses, NAT, DHCP, DNS, and the OSI and TCP/IP models.
- **Module 3:** cables and connectors, Wi-Fi setup and security, and planning a small office or cyber café network.
- **Module 4:** troubleshooting with ipconfig and ping, and building a networking career.
