When a cyber café in Kakamega shares one internet connection between ten computers, when an office prints to a single printer from every desk, or when you send a WhatsApp message to a friend in Mombasa – a **network** is at work.

A **computer network** is two or more devices connected so they can share information and resources. Learning how networks work opens doors to jobs in IT support, cyber cafés, internet service providers (ISPs) and businesses of every size.

## What networks let us share

| Resource | Example |
|---|---|
| Internet connection | One fibre line shared by a whole office |
| Files | A shared folder with the shop's price list |
| Printers | Ten computers printing to one printer |
| Communication | Email, video calls, chat |
| Applications | A school management system used from many PCs |

## Nodes and links

- A **node** is any device on the network: computer, phone, printer, smart TV, CCTV camera.
- A **link** is the connection between nodes. It can be **wired** (cables) or **wireless** (Wi-Fi, mobile data, satellite).

## Types of networks by size

| Type | Full name | Covers | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **PAN** | Personal Area Network | A few metres around one person | Phone connected to Bluetooth earphones |
| **LAN** | Local Area Network | One building or site | The computers and Wi-Fi in a cyber café or school lab |
| **MAN** | Metropolitan Area Network | A city or town | A provider's fibre network across Nairobi |
| **WAN** | Wide Area Network | Large distances – counties, countries | A bank connecting its branches in Kisumu, Eldoret and Nairobi |

The **Internet** is the largest WAN of all: a global "network of networks" connecting millions of smaller networks owned by homes, businesses, ISPs and governments.

> **Note:** A **LAN** is usually owned and managed by one organisation. A **WAN** often uses lines rented from telecom companies and ISPs.

## How you reach the Internet

Your device does not connect to the whole Internet directly. It connects to a local network, which connects to an **ISP** – a company that sells internet access, such as a mobile network or a home fibre provider. The ISP connects to bigger networks, including undersea fibre cables that land on the Kenyan coast at Mombasa and link East Africa to the rest of the world.

```text
Your phone → home Wi-Fi router → ISP network → national backbone
           → undersea cable → servers around the world
```

## Clients and servers

- A **client** requests a service – your browser asking for a web page.
- A **server** provides the service – the computer that stores and sends that web page.

In a small office, one computer might act as a **file server**, while the others are clients. In a **peer-to-peer** network, there is no dedicated server; each computer shares with the others directly. Small cyber cafés and homes often use peer-to-peer setups.

## Wired or wireless?

| | Wired (Ethernet) | Wireless (Wi-Fi) |
|---|---|---|
| Speed | Usually faster and steadier | Can drop with distance and walls |
| Security | Someone needs physical access to plug in | Signals pass through walls – must be protected |
| Convenience | Cables to install | Easy to move around |
| Best for | Desktops, printers, servers | Phones, laptops, visitors |

Most real networks mix both.

```exercise
Q: Name the network type.
The computers in one school lab: ___
A bank linking branches in different towns: ___
Your phone and Bluetooth earphones: ___
A: LAN | WAN | PAN
```

```exercise
Q: Complete the sentence.

A: Provider
```
