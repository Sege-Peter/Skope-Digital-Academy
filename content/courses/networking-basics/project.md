## The situation

Fatuma is opening a small cyber café in Eldoret, with eight computers, one printer and a Wi-Fi hotspot for customers. She has asked you, as a beginner network technician, for a **network plan** and a short **troubleshooting guide** she can stick on the wall. This is a made-up business, so you design it on paper and test your skills on a device you are allowed to use.

> **Note:** Do **not** include real Wi-Fi passwords, router admin passwords, your public IP address, or full MAC addresses from your own devices. Hide or change them with a made-up value (for example AA:BB:CC:00:11:22). Only look at a router with the owner's permission.

## What you will produce

One document with four parts:

1. **Network diagram** – a clear drawing (hand-drawn photo, Canva or draw.io) showing the modem or router, a switch, 8 computers, a printer and a Wi-Fi access point, and how each is connected (cable type or wireless).
2. **Address plan** – a table with at least 6 rows (device, private IP address, how it gets it: static or DHCP). Use a private range and state the gateway and DNS. Explain in 2–3 sentences why the printer gets a fixed address.
3. **Security and backup plan** – 5–7 bullet points: Wi-Fi security mode, changing default admin passwords, a separate guest network, and what to do during power cuts.
4. **Troubleshooting guide** – a "ping ladder" on one page, naming the four things to test in order and what each failure means. Add a real log: paste the results from running these on a computer you may use, with personal details hidden.

## Steps

1. Review the lessons on devices, IP addresses, DHCP and DNS, and troubleshooting.
2. Draw the diagram first, then build the address plan from it.
3. Write the security points in simple words Fatuma can understand.
4. Run the ping ladder and `ipconfig` (or a phone's Wi-Fi details screen if you have no computer) and paste the output.
5. Check that the IP addresses in your table all fit one range, then submit.

## What to submit

- Your document as a PDF, Word file or clear photo (PNG or JPG) – max 5 MB – **or** typed in the text box with the diagram uploaded.
- Optional: a link to your Canva or draw.io diagram.

## Tips

- Every device needs a different IP address – duplicates cause problems.
- Label cables and devices clearly so a peer can follow your drawing.
- Do not state prices of equipment as facts. Say "to be quoted".

```rubric
Network diagram | Includes every required device with clear labels and correct connection types | 5
Address plan | At least six devices with valid private addresses in one range, gateway and DNS stated, and static versus DHCP explained | 5
Security and backup | Five or more sensible, specific points, including default passwords and a guest network | 4
Troubleshooting guide and log | Ping steps in the right order with meanings, plus pasted results with personal details hidden | 4
Presentation | Neat, readable, correct terms and no real passwords or private data | 2
```
