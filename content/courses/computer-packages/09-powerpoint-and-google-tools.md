PowerPoint helps you explain ideas to a group – at a church meeting, a chama, a job interview or a business pitch. In this lesson you will also meet **Google Docs, Sheets and Slides**, the free online tools that do the same jobs.

## PowerPoint basics

- A presentation file (`.pptx`) is made of **slides**.
- **Home** › **New Slide** adds a slide; the arrow beside it lets you choose a **layout** (Title Slide, Title and Content, Two Content, Picture with Caption…).
- **Design** tab: ready-made **themes** with matching fonts and colours.
- **Insert** tab: pictures, shapes, tables, charts, icons and videos.
- **Transitions** tab: effects between slides. **Animations** tab: effects on items within a slide.

## Designing slides people can read

| Do | Avoid |
|---|---|
| One main idea per slide | Whole paragraphs copied from a report |
| 3–5 short bullet points | Ten bullets in tiny font |
| Text at least 24 pt; titles larger | Fonts smaller than 18 pt |
| Strong contrast (dark text on light, or the reverse) | Yellow text on white, red on green |
| One theme throughout | A different design on every slide |
| A relevant photo or simple chart | Blurry images or random clip art |
| Simple transitions (Fade) | Spinning, bouncing animations on everything |

> **Tip:** A useful guideline is the **6 × 6 idea**: no more than about six bullet points, with about six words each. Your slides support your talk – they are not the talk.

## Presenting

| Shortcut | Action |
|---|---|
| **F5** | Start the slide show from the first slide |
| **Shift + F5** | Start from the current slide |
| **Right arrow / Space / click** | Next slide |
| **Left arrow** | Previous slide |
| **B** | Turn the screen black (press again to return) |
| **Esc** | End the slide show |

Write what you want to say in the **Notes** area under each slide. **Presenter View** (offered when you connect a projector) shows your notes on your laptop while the audience sees only the slides.

Presenting tips:

1. Practise aloud at least twice and time yourself.
2. Face the audience, not the screen.
3. Carry your file on a flash disk **and** in your email or Google Drive, and save a PDF copy in case the venue's computer has a different version.

## Google Docs, Sheets and Slides

Google's free tools run in a web browser (and as phone apps) with a Google account. Your work saves automatically to **Google Drive**.

| Microsoft Office | Google equivalent | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Word | **Google Docs** | Styles, tables, headers and page setup all exist |
| Excel | **Google Sheets** | SUM, AVERAGE, IF, VLOOKUP and XLOOKUP work the same way |
| PowerPoint | **Google Slides** | Themes, layouts and presenter view |

Why they matter:

- **Free** – no licence to buy, good for students and small businesses.
- **Sharing** – click **Share** and give others *Viewer*, *Commenter* or *Editor* access.
- **Real-time teamwork** – several people can edit the same file at once, e.g. a group assignment or a chama register.
- **Version history** – see and restore earlier versions (in the **File** menu).
- **Download as** Word, Excel, PowerPoint or PDF from the **File** menu.

Most shortcuts you learnt (**Ctrl + B**, **Ctrl + C**, **Ctrl + Z**) work in Google tools too.

> **Warning:** Be careful with "Anyone with the link" sharing. If a sheet contains phone numbers or ID numbers, share it only with named people.

> **Note:** Google tools need internet. If data is costly, turn on offline mode in Google Drive settings for files you need without a connection, or work in Office and upload later.

## Practice task

Make a 6-slide presentation called *My Plan for the Next Year* in PowerPoint or Google Slides: a title slide, four content slides (skills, savings goal, job or business idea, next steps) and a thank-you slide. Follow the design rules above, add speaker notes, and present it to a friend or family member.
