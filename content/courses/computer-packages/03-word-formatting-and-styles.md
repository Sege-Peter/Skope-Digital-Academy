Microsoft Word is the program most Kenyan offices use for letters, reports, minutes and CVs. This lesson covers the skills you will use every single day: typing, selecting, formatting and using **styles**.

## The Word window

Word versions look slightly different (Word 2016, 2019, 2021 and Microsoft 365), but the main parts are the same:

- The **ribbon** across the top, with tabs such as **Home**, **Insert**, **Layout** (called **Page Layout** in some versions), **References** and **View**.
- The **document area** where you type.
- The **status bar** at the bottom, showing the page number and word count.

> **Note:** Menu names and positions change between versions. If a button is not where this course says, use the **search box** at the top of Word ("Tell me" or "Search") and type what you want, e.g. *line spacing*.

## Selecting text

You must select text before you format it.

| To select | Do this |
|---|---|
| One word | Double-click it |
| One paragraph | Triple-click it |
| Any amount | Drag across it, or hold **Shift** and use the arrow keys |
| The whole document | **Ctrl + A** |

## Character formatting

These change how letters look. Most are on the **Home** tab.

| Shortcut | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Ctrl + B** | **Bold** |
| **Ctrl + I** | *Italic* |
| **Ctrl + U** | Underline |
| Font box | Change the font, e.g. Calibri, Arial, Times New Roman |
| Size box | Change size – 11 or 12 pt for body text is standard |

## Paragraph formatting

These change whole paragraphs.

| Shortcut | Alignment |
|---|---|
| **Ctrl + L** | Left (normal for letters) |
| **Ctrl + E** | Centre (titles) |
| **Ctrl + R** | Right (dates on some letters) |
| **Ctrl + J** | Justify (straight edges on both sides, common in reports) |

Also on the **Home** tab: **bullets**, **numbering**, **line spacing** (1.0, 1.15, 1.5…) and **indents**.

## Why styles matter

A beginner makes headings by selecting text and clicking bold, bigger font and a colour – again and again. A professional uses **styles**.

A style is a saved bundle of formatting with a name, such as **Heading 1**, **Heading 2** or **Normal**. You find them in the **Styles** gallery on the **Home** tab.

Benefits:

1. **Consistency** – every heading looks the same.
2. **Speed** – one click instead of five.
3. **Easy changes** – right-click a style › **Modify**, and every heading in the document updates at once.
4. **Automatic table of contents** – Word builds it from your headings (**References** › **Table of Contents**).
5. **Navigation** – the **Navigation Pane** (**View** tab, or **Ctrl + F**) lists your headings so you can jump around a 40-page report.

```text
Example report outline using styles
Heading 1:  Chapter 1 – Introduction
Heading 2:    1.1 Background of the study
Heading 2:    1.2 Objectives
Heading 1:  Chapter 2 – Literature Review
Normal:     (body text in 12 pt, 1.5 line spacing)
```

> **Tip:** University and college students in Kenya pay for well-formatted project reports. If you can apply styles and insert an automatic table of contents, you can offer this as a service.

## Format Painter

The **Format Painter** (paintbrush icon on the **Home** tab) copies formatting from one place to another. Click inside formatted text, click the paintbrush, then drag over the text you want to change. Double-click the paintbrush to use it many times; press **Esc** to stop.

## Spelling and grammar

Red underlines mean a possible spelling mistake; blue or double underlines suggest grammar issues. Right-click for suggestions, or press **F7** to check the whole document. Set the language to **English (United Kingdom)** if your client prefers British spelling, which is the norm in Kenyan offices (*organise*, *colour*, *programme*).

## Practice task

Type a one-page "About Me" document with a title and three sections (*My background*, *My skills*, *My goals*). Use **Title** or **Heading 1** for the title, **Heading 2** for section names, then insert an automatic table of contents at the top. Change the Heading 2 style colour once and watch all three headings update.
